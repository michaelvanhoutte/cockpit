# Glossary

- **Account** — the person or organization Cockpit holds work for, and the outermost boundary: everything belongs to exactly one, and nothing is shared between two. Each User has one. Not the *source accounts* (a Gmail login, a Slack workspace) a Workspace connects to. The **register** is the list of which Accounts exist, who the Users are and who is signed in; it is what says an Account is real before any of its data is opened.
- **Workspace** — top-level context inside an Account and the privacy boundary (Work, Personal, Customer 1…); defines which sources are connected.
- **Dashboard** — a switchable named view inside a Workspace, holding a layout of Panels.
- **Panel** — a movable, titled box on a Dashboard, sharing a row with up to three others, its title unique within its Dashboard. It holds **the Items filed onto it, text you write in it, or the Items a rule gathers onto it** (a **Filter**, below), and which of the three is settled when the Panel is made and never after. A Panel of text is **read-only** or written in, and its words are drawn as the characters typed or as what they mean — both the Panel's own state, so the same for everybody, and neither a change to the text itself. Nothing is ever filed onto a Panel of text.
- **Filter** — a Panel that shows every open Item filed on a Panel of its Workspace meeting all its **conditions**, rather than the Items filed onto it. Never one still in the Inbox, which is what keeps triaging worth doing. A funnel beside its name marks it and reads its conditions back on hover; until it has one it says to choose what it shows from its own menu. Its rows go by due date, then Priority, then oldest, with no due date last, and they are neither reordered nor filed: nothing is ever filed onto a Filter.
- **Condition** — one rule a Filter's Items must meet, all of which hold together. Today a **Due date** window: *overdue*, *today*, *this week* (Monday to Sunday), *this month*, *this quarter* or *not set* — the four periods widened by *or overdue* unless it is unticked — measured against the calendar of whoever is looking.
- **Layout** — one named arrangement of a Dashboard's Panels — which rows they are on, in what order along each, and the share of the row each takes — together with the screen width it was made at. A Dashboard can have several, so it reads well on a phone and on a 4K screen; the one nearest the screen in front of you is the one drawn, and picking another by name from the Dashboard's own bar overrides that for as long as you are on that screen.
- **Inbox** — the one place in a Workspace holding what has arrived and not been dealt with: every open Item filed on no Panel. Beside the Dashboards rather than one of them, and never renamed or deleted, because it is not a Panel — it is what is left when nothing holds an Item.
- **Item** — the single object everything is stored as, whether it arrived from a source or was created in the app. **Task** and **Note** are *types* of Item, not separate objects.
- **Type** — what kind of thing an Item is: *Task*, *Note*, and whatever else you name. The set is open and account-wide. Each wears a colour from the palette, which is the dot at the head of a row; the name beside it is what carries the meaning. A Type says what an Item *is*, where **Done** says where it stands. Every Item is captured as one — there is no *No type* to choose — though one whose Type was deleted has none. Types are made, renamed, recoloured, ordered and deleted in a window of their own — the one place a Type's name is typed — the name and the colour together on one form, and an Item whose Type is deleted keeps everything but the label.
- **Task** — an Item representing something to do. One source Item can produce several; the *next-action label* keeps that word and is a different thing.
- **Note** — an Item created in the app as a note or idea, with no source behind it.
- **Captured message** — what arrived, or what you said, as it stood when the Item was made. Never edited afterwards, and shown only when you ask to see it, so there is always a way back to what was originally captured.
- **Title** — the short name an Item goes by. Yours to edit, not required, and what a row shows when there is no next action. **Proposed** for a captured note until you edit it.
- **Description** — what you have to say about an Item: as long as it needs to be, edited on the Item's form. Formatted text, stored as Markdown, and editable as either. **Proposed** for a captured note until you edit it.
- **Attachment** — a file added to an Item, on its form below Description: an image, a short clip or a PDF, added by a button or by dropping it there. Drawn as a chip — a thumbnail for an image, an icon with its name and size for anything else — and a click opens or downloads it. Removing one takes it off the Item; the file it named is not undone by anything else about it having existed.
- **Proposed** — written for you by Cockpit rather than by you, and replaceable by Cockpit until you edit it; from your first edit it is yours and nothing overwrites it again. Today the Title and the Description of a captured note are proposed together, so editing either settles both. The same rule holds wherever Cockpit proposes something: it may replace what it proposed and never what you settled.
- **Reading** — one other way a captured note could genuinely be read, offered beside the one Cockpit proposed: its own title and description, and a few words saying what that reading takes the note to mean. Offered only where the difference would change what you do about the note, and rarely at all. Choosing one fills the two boxes, exactly as typing them in by hand would — it is Save that settles them, not the choosing.
- **Association** — a link from an Item to a Person, Project or Topic; many-to-many, which is why an Item can appear in several Panels.
- **Capture** — creating an Item directly in the app instead of receiving it from a source.
- **Done** — finished with, recorded as the time it happened. An Item is either yours to deal with or done; there is nothing in between, and a Type says what kind of thing it is rather than where it stands.
- **Priority** — low / normal / high importance, independent of the Focus horizon.
- **Due date** — a calendar date an Item is due by, or none. Drawn plainly on the row when set; colouring it by proximity is a separate, unbuilt idea (`docs/ideas.md`).
- **Next action** — the short, always-editable label describing what to actually do about an Item.
- **Focus horizon** — Today / This Week / This Month / This Quarter, date-anchored so it escalates to overdue.
- **Triage / process** — dealing with what arrived: associating an Item, filing it where it belongs, and finishing with it or dismissing it.
- **Undo** — putting back what the last change took away, offered briefly after it and never afterwards. One step, not a stack: what an accident needs is a way back, offered where you are looking.
- **Form** — where more than one field is edited at once: a modal with a route of its own, so the back button closes it and a link opens it. **Save** writes; Cancel, Escape and pressing outside discard without asking.
- **Filing** — putting an Item on a Panel. An Item can be filed on several Panels at once, which is why one thing to do can appear on *Project Falcon* and on *Anna*; one filed nowhere is in the Inbox. Each filing carries its own place in that Panel's order, so an Item can be first on one Panel and fifth on another.
- **Offline** — working from the local copy when the connection is not there. Cockpit opens instantly and shows what it already holds; a write made offline is not yet queued or retried (see `docs/product/offline.md`). Nothing new arrives from a source until the connection is back.
- **User** — a person who uses this Cockpit. Each User owns one Account, which is what makes two Users' work separate. A User has a **Role**.
- **Role** — *user* or *admin*. An admin can open the **admin pages**; everyone else is refused them, by the server rather than by not being offered the way in. It is read afresh on every request, so a role taken away applies to the next thing that person does without ending the sign-in they hold.
- **Disabled** — somebody whose access was taken away. They cannot sign in, the sign-ins they were holding are ended, and **everything they own stays exactly as it was** — which is what makes it the reversible half of removing somebody, and why deleting a User is rarely the thing to reach for. Their row stays in the list, marked, and enabling them puts them back; the sign-in they held is not revived, so they sign in afresh. Trying to sign in tells them their access was removed rather than that their Google account cannot sign in here: it can, and was stopped, and the person it happens to is a colleague who would otherwise go looking for a problem that is not theirs.
- **Admin pages** — where an admin manages the Cockpit rather than an Account: today, who can sign in at all, adding somebody, renaming one or changing their Role, taking somebody's access away or giving it back (**Disabled**, above), and deleting somebody. **Adding takes a name and the Google address they sign in with, and nothing else**: everyone arrives ordinary, and the Account they own is made with them rather than chosen. **A Role is changed on the person's own row afterwards, and never taken from the last admin or from yourself** — either would leave the admin pages reachable by nobody, with no way back but the bootstrap the environment was seeded from. **The same two refusals guard Disabling and deleting**, since an admin who cannot sign in, or no longer exists, is no more use than one who is not an admin. **Deleting somebody takes the Account they own with them**: their sign-ins end, everything the Account holds is destroyed, and then they leave the register — so whoever is next given their name, added here or signing in for the first time, starts with an empty Account rather than inheriting theirs, and the person deleted who signs in again is somebody new. It is asked first, naming how many Workspaces go with them, and only a Backup taken beforehand brings any of it back. It is also refused for an Account another User uses too — which only a restored register can produce — since destroying it would take their work as well. **Renaming somebody renames the Account they own**, which is named after them. The address is not editable: it is what somebody signs in by, and changing it is a question of its own. Two people may share a name — the address is what a Cockpit holds to one person — and somebody added is in the register before they ever sign in, under the name the admin gave them rather than Google's. **The one part of the app that crosses Accounts**, since who exists is the environment's question and not any one Account's; and the one management screen with an address, for the same reason — there is no Workspace behind it to keep.
- **Sign in** — proving which User you are, with your **Google account**, from the **logon page**. Any Google account gets in: one the register has never seen is made an ordinary User, called what Google calls them, with an Account of their own. An address the register holds for a *different* Google account is refused and told so. A sign-in lasts a set time, renews while used, and expires on its own, and Cockpit says so rather than failing at you.
- **Google account** — the account somebody signs in with. The register holds the address it recognises them by the first time and, once they have signed in, the identity Google keys them by, so a changed address neither locks them out nor lets a new owner of it in.
- **Guest** — somebody who pressed *Continue as guest* on the logon page instead of signing in with a Google account. There is **one guest account and everybody who does this shares it**, so two guests see each other's work; it is made the first time anybody asks for it, and it is offered on the deployment meant to be shown to strangers rather than on every one. **It opens on a fixed demonstration** — three Workspaces of a contractor's week, their Dashboards, Panels and Items already there — rather than on the empty starter every other Account is given, so a stranger sees Cockpit working instead of a blank page. **It goes back to that demonstration every night, and whenever an operator asks**: whatever guests added, changed or deleted is gone, and nothing a guest does is kept. Ordinary in every other way: the same session, the same commands, the same rules.
- **Sign out** — ending a sign-in deliberately. Cockpit forgets it and the browser is left holding nothing of what you were looking at.
- **Backup** — a copy of everything an environment holds, written to local files: the register, and each Account's own data in a file of its own. Taken by an operator with a command, never from inside the app, and it holds no sign-ins — those are re-made by signing in, and putting an old one back would revive a sign-in somebody ended. Taking one changes nothing about the environment it reads.
- **Restore** — putting a Backup back, a whole environment or one User's Account. It **replaces** an Account rather than merging into it: what that Account held is gone, which is why one that already holds anything is refused unless replacing it was asked for. The register is only ever added to — a User the Backup needs and the register lacks is created, one already there is left alone, and a Backup that disagrees with the register about who somebody is refuses rather than deciding.

**Menu**, **Deleting**, **Ordering**, **Live updates**, **Selection** and **Updating** are shared behaviour, defined once in `docs/product/across-the-app.md` rather than here — a shared behaviour with no word is one nobody can say is tested (`tools/test-explorer/concepts.json`).

This glossary is binding on anything written to be read as a description of the product, test names included (see `docs/testing-strategy.md`, "Tests are named in the product's language"). Words that live only in `docs/architecture.md` — command, envelope, tombstone, idempotency, last-write-wins — are implementation and stay there.
