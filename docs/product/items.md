# Items

Everything that flows in — email, Slack message, Notion page, later a calendar event or bookmark — normalizes into a single **Item**, storing: source app (or *internal*), source ID, a deep link back, sender/author, timestamp, its three texts, optional priority, optional due date, and whether it has been started or finished with. Native notes and to-dos created in the app have no source app and open in the app rather than deep-linking out. An Item with a deep link offers to open at its source, in a new tab, from its row and from its "..." menu ("Open in Microsoft Teams"); an Item on a Panel also offers **Copy link** there and in its form (see `docs/product/across-the-app.md`); every Item not made in the app also names its source on a "From <source> - <sender>" line in its form, which is where the same link sits, and as "<source> · <sender>" on its row. The source goes by its product's name — "Gmail", "Microsoft Teams" — never by what is stored.

**An Item carries three texts, answering three different questions.** The **captured message** is what arrived, or what you said: written once when the Item is made and never changed by anything afterwards, because it is the record of what was actually captured. The **title** names the Item and the **description** is what you have to say about it; both are yours to edit and neither is required. **The description is formatted text, stored as Markdown**: bold looks bold as it is written, and the same description can be worked on as its source. Bold, italic, links and both kinds of list have a button and a keyboard shortcut; a heading, a table, a code block or strikethrough has neither and is kept all the same, because a description pasted out of somewhere else must arrive whole.

**An image goes into the description by its own button, or by pasting or dropping it into the text.** It is in the text at once, where it was put — the cursor, or the drop point — drawn from the file on the device until it has uploaded; it is also one of the Item's Attachments, listed with the rest. An upload that fails takes the image back out of the text and says why in the undo bar. Saved before it has uploaded, the description shows a missing image on another device until it lands. Only a PNG, JPEG, GIF or WebP goes in the text; any other file is refused there, naming Attachments as where it can go.

**Only two of the three are ever shown.** The captured message is a record, not a name, and it is what the title and description are read back against. It stood in as a label until an Item had two names — the one its row showed and the empty one its form offered under it — which is what **capture writing the title** fixed: what you capture becomes the title, and where it does not fit one line of 200 characters the title takes the first 200 and the whole of it goes into the description, so nothing you typed is only in a text you cannot edit.

**And a moment later Cockpit rewrites both of them, having read the note.** Capture is meant to be fast, so what arrives is clipped, abbreviated, half-typed and often a mix of English and Dutch — and two weeks later the row says `part 11 audit trail q for validation protocol, who signs off eod` with none of the context around it left. So the note is read and given a **title** as short as it can be while still naming this note and no other, and a **description** that writes out what was said so it still makes sense in two weeks.

- **It adds nothing the note does not contain.** Abbreviations are expanded, spelling corrected, a clipped sentence finished and a dictated one reordered; a fact, a name, a date, a reason or a next step is never supplied. Where the note refers to something it never states, the description says the note does not say.
- **It never invents a priority, and takes one you stated.** "High priority", "urgent" or "ASAP" sets High, and "low priority" or "no rush" sets Low; tone never counts. The words it read come out of the title and stay in the description. A priority you chose in the Capture strip, or set or cleared before the reading lands, is never replaced, and *Suggestions* lists the one it set.
- **It never invents a due date, and takes a day you named.** "Tomorrow", "Friday" or "the 24th" sets that day, counted on your calendar from the moment you captured the note, so a note kept offline and sent the next day still means the day after you said it. A time is dropped, since a due date has none, and an unclear day ("end of the month", "soon") is left unset. A note captured with no time zone - from Claude or a connector - is read in the time zone of your latest capture that had one, and with none no date is read. As with the priority, a due date you chose, set or cleared is never replaced, and *Suggestions* lists the one it set. The one date it does replace is a date a Gmail or Outlook connection gave the Item (see "Settings › Connections" in `docs/product/workspaces.md`), which is not yours until you set it.
- **It files the note where you said it goes, and only where that points to one place.** Say any part of the path — workspace, dashboard, panels: "for Home", "Day to day, Admin and money", "on Admin and money and on This week". Names are matched to yours however you say them, so "admin and money" finds *Admin & money*; a name that matches nothing is ignored, and nothing is ever created.
  - **A panel you name gets the note at the top**, skipping the Inbox, as if you had filed it yourself, and several named get it on each. A dashboard only narrows, so two panels of one name are told apart by saying the dashboard, and two in different workspaces by saying the workspace. A name that still points to more than one place files nothing, and the note waits in the Inbox with its usual suggestion. A panel set to Never propose is still filed on; a Filter or a panel of text never is.
  - **An *Any workspace* note is settled in the workspace the path points to**: the one you named, the one holding the panels it was filed on, or the one holding the dashboard you named when only one workspace has a dashboard of that name. It leaves the other workspaces' Inboxes, and where it is not filed, the panel suggested among the old workspace's panels is withdrawn and one suggested among the new workspace's.
  - **A note captured inside a workspace only matches within it**, and one you filed, dismissed or finished before the reading landed is left where you put it.
  - **Only names are shown, and only when you say a place**: the reading lifts the words out, and a second, cheaper reading matches them against your workspace, dashboard and panel names — never an Item. The words leave the title and stay in the description, and *Suggestions* lists the workspace it set and, in a *Panels* line, the panels it filed the note on, named as they were when filed.
- **A Teams message saved with a note is read as both, and your words win.** The note you type when saving it sits in the Item's captured message after the message, marked as yours, so it is still there without a reading. Where it and the sender disagree about a priority, a due date or a place ("urgent, call me today" against your "no rush, call her Friday": Low, due Friday) yours decides it, and a field your note leaves unsaid is still read from the sender's words.
- **A note's formatting stays on the same words.** Bold, italics, links and lists in the note come back in the description it proposes; a note without any gets none, and no headings or bullets unless it was a list.
- **Both texts are *proposed*, and yours the moment you edit either.** Editing the title or the description takes both over for good, and nothing overwrites either again.
- **The answer is in the language the note was written in**, whichever of the two that is, and a note mixing them stays mixed.
- **Capture never waits for it and never fails because of it.** The Item is written and answered first; the reading follows. Where nothing is set up to read a note, where the reading fails, where what comes back is not usable, or in the shared guest account, which never calls the model, the Item simply keeps the title capture wrote.
- **Only notes captured after this shipped are read.** Nothing sweeps what is already there, so a title you wrote by hand is out of reach by construction rather than by a rule.

**The same reading may say the note genuinely takes two meanings, and offer both.** `bel jan` is *call Jan* or *call in January*; picking the wrong one silently is a wrong answer nothing afterwards can spot. Where that is true, Cockpit lists the other **Readings** beside the one it wrote onto the Item — each its own title and description, and a few words saying what that reading takes the note to mean — and choosing one puts its title and description in the two boxes, exactly as typing them in by hand would. **Reporting none is the common case and the right one:** a note that is merely terse has one reading, and only a difference that would change what you do about the note is worth naming a second time. The row carries one quiet mark saying a note reads more than one way; the readings themselves wait for the form, the way the captured message does.

**A note that may repeat another says so, and offers both answers.** Cockpit pairs notes that seem to say the same thing. A flagged row wears a short chip, *Duplicate?*, whose hover names the other note; the Item form lists it under *Possible duplicate of*, beside which **Dismiss this one** dismisses the note the form is open on (with the usual Undo, and the form closes) and **Not a duplicate** settles the pair. The row's menu offers the same two as **Dismiss as duplicate** and **Not a duplicate**. Nothing is merged: the other note stays as it is. The chip goes once either note is dismissed, done, or the pair settled, the last at the click and back at the Undo's; a refusal brings it back with the reason in the bar from the row, or beside the warning in the form.

**A note that lands in the description is Markdown, not escaped text**, decided when capture started writing there: somebody who captured a `- ` list meant a list, and the source view and the *Details* tab both hold the note exactly as typed for the times they did not. The cost is that a note written over several lines reads as one paragraph, a single line break being a soft break in CommonMark; making capture propose formatting rather than inherit it belongs with the rest of the smarter capture (see "Capture and the task creator" in `docs/ideas.md`).

**Each Item can also carry any number of Attachments** — an image, a short clip or a PDF, added on the Item's own form by **+ Attach** (at a desk on the status line, on a phone among the chips) or **+ Add** on its Attachments tab, by dropping it anywhere on the form outside the description text, or by pasting it with the cursor in no text box, whichever tab is open. **The files have a tab of their own, *Attachments*, with their count beside its name.** It lists each as a chip: a thumbnail for an image, an icon with its name and size for anything else; a click opens or downloads the real file. The Item tab keeps one line for them under *On panels*: the first four as small icons, each opening its file, then *N attachments →*, which opens the tab; with no files there is no line.  Removing one only takes it off the Item — nothing else about it having existed is undone by that. **A file chosen shows at once, marked *Attaching…* until it has uploaded and then as its chip, and a file removed is gone at once**; a file the server refuses leaves, and a removal it refuses brings the chip back, with its reason under the files (on the Item tab, under its line).

**The Panels an Item is filed on are listed and changed in its form**, in an *On panels* field under the description, the same search and **+ New panel** as Capture's *File on* and within the Item's own Workspace. **A Panel made there shows at once**, on its Dashboard and as the Item's chip, before the server has answered; if the Panel is refused, both go back and the undo bar says why. Removing the last returns the Item to the Inbox. A Filter panel that happens to show the Item is listed nowhere on the form: the Item is not filed on it.

**A row shows the title**, and *Untitled* where an Item has none — the best label it has, worked out where the row is drawn. **Where the row is too narrow to draw the whole label, hovering it spells the label out**, and where the row drew all of it, hovering says nothing: a tooltip repeating what you can already read is noise on every row of a list you scan. The cut is in the drawing alone, so a screen reader has had the whole label the entire time; a finger, which cannot hover, reaches **Open** from the row's own menu instead, and the form shows it whole.

Each Item can carry any number of **Associations**, to one or more **People**, **Projects** or **Topics/Areas** — stored today (`packages/shared/src/domain/item.ts`), with no view yet that adds, shows or filters by one. What Panels do with associations once there is one is designed, not built: see "Associations" in `docs/ideas.md`.

**An Item is To do, In progress or Done.** Starting it and finishing with it are each a time, so both say *when* — an Item in progress wears a pill saying so on its row. **The Item's form and its row's menu both offer all three**, the menu as a **Status** row opening a submenu. The form commits the choice at once when docked and saves it with the rest when not; In progress keeps a start time the Item still carries, and To do clears it. Undoing Done returns to In progress rather than to To do where it was started before being finished with, and returns the Item to where it was filed. **A Done Item leaves every list but is not lost:** the Workspace's **All items** tab lists it — and any Item not yet assigned to a Workspace, as the Inbox does — with *Done* chosen in its **Status** filter to see only the finished ones, each row saying where it was filed ("Day to day ▸ Admin & money", each Panel named when there are several, *Inbox* when it was filed nowhere or its Panel has been deleted) and when it last changed. Opening a row opens its form, where changing the status reopens it. Dismissed Items have no such list yet. dismissing one is a separate tombstone, the one that makes it reversible. A due date is a field.

**An Item can be set to repeat, from its form.** A **↻ Repeats** chip follows the form's status, priority and Due on their line (one more chip in the chip row on a phone) and opens a popover: *Never*, *Daily*, *Weekly*, *Monthly*, *Quarterly* or *Yearly*, a **Next on** day, and **Due __ days after it appears** (blank for none), with one line reading the rule back - *Weekly on Monday · next Mon 2 Nov · due Sat 7 Nov*. Once set, the chip wears the rule and the Item's row wears a ↻ among its title marks, which reads *Repeats weekly on Monday* on hover. **Next on** starts one period from today (31 January's month falls on the last day of February) and cannot be moved to before today on the calendar of the time zone the browser was in when it was set, which is stored with the rule. Setting Repeats leaves the Item's own due date as it was. The form writes it like any other field: at once when docked, on Save otherwise.

**On its day, a repeating Item comes back as a fresh copy.** At midnight on the calendar its rule was last set on, a new Item appears at the top of each of the series' Panels still live, or in the Inbox where none is, in a tab already open as in one opened later. It is made from the series, never from an earlier copy: the title, description, priority and Panels the Item it was first set on had then, and no files, so an edit to one copy stays on that copy; it starts To do, due the set number of days after the day it appears. **Never more than one is open**: while the latest is still To do or In progress on the day, nothing is added and it keeps its *Overdue* pill; finished with or dismissed, the next appears on the first day the rule comes round after today, never on a day already passed, so a week nobody opened brings back one, not seven. Monthly on the 31st falls on the last day of a shorter month and returns to the 31st; yearly on 29 February falls on the 28th between leap years. **Moving one Item's due date moves only it; moving Next on moves the series**, and a rule set again after its Next on has passed starts from the first day it comes round from today. **Never on any of them stops the series**, a Done one opened from All items included; dismissing one skips only it. A series whose Workspace is deleted stops.

**A change to an Item shows the moment it is made.** Done, In progress, priority, due date, repeat, title, description, next action, Dismiss, *Not a duplicate* and filing on a Panel (moving, adding, removing and reordering) — from a row, a drag, the form, a selection, an Inbox proposal or Undo — are drawn at once and stay as drawn while the server answers, however slow it is. One the server refuses goes back, with its reason where that surface already gives one (the form beside the field, the selection's bar, above a list's rows for a filing), and a dismissal from the form closes it and says so in the bar. **Undo is offered at the click**, and pressed before the change it reverses has landed it waits for it. Changes reach the server one at a time, in the order they were made. **A filing is built on what is drawn, filings still waiting included**, so two in a row both land, and no read of the Workspace stands between the click and the send unless the tab has been told something changed elsewhere (it then reads once first). The Panel having changed on another device is a filing's refusal: it goes back with *this panel changed while you were looking at it: try the move again*.

Focus horizons and the AI layer beyond the note cleanup and Readings above are designed, not built: see "Focus horizons" and "AI layer" in `docs/ideas.md`. Deadline pills have shipped: within a week of its due date a row wears a pill — a quiet amber outline ("Due in 5d"), a soft amber fill within two days ("Due tomorrow"), solid amber on the day ("Due today"), red once it has passed ("Overdue 2d") — and the row itself stays plain. **At a desk the pill sits on the title line, the date under it and the age at the end; on a screen narrower than 768px the title line holds only the title and its marks, the pill takes the date's place under it, a date over a week off still reads "Due <date>", and the age is not drawn**, so the title gets the width. A day is the viewer's own calendar day, and nothing escalates past red, only the count.
