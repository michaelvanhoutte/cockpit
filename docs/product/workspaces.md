# Workspaces

Everything is built from a **container hierarchy**: **Workspace → Dashboard → Panel**.

**Workspace** — everything you want in front of you while you work in one context, and the privacy boundary. It controls **which source accounts are connected and visible**, so private email is invisible while in *Work*: scoping rather than filtering after the fact.

**What decides between a Workspace and a Dashboard is whether the whole context changes**, and the pair of cases is the definition: a contractor working for two customers wants a Workspace each — different accounts, different mail, nothing shared — while somebody at one company serving two customers wants one Workspace and a Dashboard per customer, because the context is the same and only the view of it changes. The obvious advice, *one for work, one for personal, one per customer*, gives the second of those the wrong answer, which is why the app says this in the question that makes one rather than leaving it to be inferred.

Each Workspace has a **colour** that follows it into three places only: the dashboard band, the accent (headings, buttons, focus rings, type labels, and the menus and windows opened over the page) and the dot in the logo. The page, the top bar and the agents' dock are the same neutral in every Workspace, so a Workspace's colour never washes the screen. The tab you are on is filled with the band and runs down into it. Colours are picked as a set from a fixed palette, on the form the Workspace's own tab opens (open decision #13); the palette and its contrast rules are in `docs/design-system.md`. A Workspace storing a set the palette does not have — every one stored before the page went neutral, or a tint it never had — is drawn in the theme its tint belongs to rather than in what it stores, and nothing stored is rewritten.

**A Workspace is made from the `+` at the end of the tabs**, which opens the question a Dashboard and a Panel are named in and says what a Workspace is. **Everything else is on the tab itself, and on the header's own button for the tab you are on**: the menu holds *Edit…*, then *Show All items tab* (*Hide All items tab* once it is on) in a group of its own, since it switches a tab of the Workspace rather than editing it, and *Delete* last. It acts on the Workspace whose menu it is, so another Workspace's tab can be switched without opening it; hiding it while that Workspace's *All items* is on screen returns to its first Dashboard (see `docs/product/dashboards.md`). There is one entry for changing a Workspace rather than a *Rename* beside it, the form being what renames. **The tab opens its own menu three ways, one per input**: a right-click, a press on the tab you are already on (that press has no other job, and it is what a finger has), and the keyboard's menu key — and the Workspace you are on also carries the same menu as a visible **…** at the right of the header (see "One menu, however it opens" in `docs/product/across-the-app.md`). **The name and colour are changed together, on a form over the page**; nothing is sent until *Save*, so a colour is chosen and looked at rather than applied on the press, and *Cancel*, *Escape* and a press outside all discard both halves. A name is required, stored trimmed, one line of at most 60 characters, and unique among live Workspaces whatever the capitalization. **Deleting a Workspace keeps everything that was in it**: its Items stay filed against it, because the router learns from the decisions filed there (see [routing-learning.md](routing-learning.md)). The name becomes available again. The last Workspace can be deleted, and the app then opens on the screen that makes one.

**The Workspaces are in the order you put them in, left to right.** A new Workspace goes last, after every Workspace the account has ever had; deleting one closes the gap. A tab is moved by dragging it along the strip — the pointer's alone, absent on a touchscreen and unreachable from a keyboard. **The tabs move as the drag does**, so the strip under the hand is the order dropping would keep. A move shows itself before the server agrees, so a second move can follow a first and a dropped tab does not snap back for a round trip. If it is refused, because a Workspace was made or deleted in another tab, the strip goes back.

**Ask up front only what has to be decided up front, and teach the rest at first use.** An account arrives holding one Workspace called *Workspace 1* with one Dashboard, *Dashboard 1*, and opens on that Workspace, asking nothing. **The Dashboard holds a Panel called *Getting started* and, beside it, the empty *Panel 1***; *Getting started* holds the steps of finding your way round Cockpit, each an open Task ticked off with its row's ✓, and the Inbox holds one Task saying what the Inbox is for. They are ordinary Items and an ordinary Panel, renamed, filed and deleted like any other, and only an account nobody has used gets them — never one somebody has, and never the shared guest account. Nothing is asked at sign-in: nobody knows when they want a second Workspace or Dashboard until they have used the first for a while, so each is explained where it is made (the `+` dialogs), not before.

The full path to any box:

```
Workspace: "Work"
├── Dashboard: "Today"       ← landing dashboard
│   ├── Panel: "Focus Today"
│   ├── Panel: "Project Falcon"
│   └── Panel: "People to talk to"
├── Dashboard: "Dormant projects"
│   └── Panel: "On hold"
└── Dashboard: "Research"
    └── Panel: "To read"
```

The landing dashboard is the desk landing: a bare `/` opens the first Workspace's dashboard at a desk, and the Capture page on a phone (see `docs/product/inbox.md`).

A Workspace connects its sources in Settings › Connections: a Microsoft Teams account, whose saved messages land in its Inbox, and Gmail accounts. Every conversation labelled `Cockpit` in a connected mailbox, whatever its age, arrives in the Workspace's Inbox within a minute of connecting, one Item per conversation ever: a Task (or, where the account no longer has its Task type, what any other capture nobody chose a Type for takes), titled with the subject — "(no subject)" where there is none — described with the plain text of the most recent labelled message, from "Gmail · sender", opening the conversation in Gmail; the usual clean-up then runs. Each mailbox is checked about every five minutes, and its row reads "last checked …", or "Failing:" and why — no label called `Cockpit` in the mailbox (create it in Gmail), or Google no longer accepts the sign-in (connect again); connecting the same mailbox again checks it from the start, finding what is already in rather than bringing it in twice. The same mailbox connected to two Workspaces gives each its own Items. A conversation labelled afterwards, by hand or by a Gmail filter, arrives within about five minutes too, once; a reply in a conversation already in adds nothing. A mailbox that has not been checked for about a week is read from the start again, so nothing labelled meanwhile is missed. When the label comes off a conversation, or its mail is binned or deleted, its Item is marked done at the next check; labelled again, the same Item reopens, undismissed if it had been dismissed. Every night each mailbox is read from the start again, which corrects anything the checks between missed; a mailbox with no label called `Cockpit` marks nothing done. **The other way, within seconds:** marking a Gmail Item done or dismissing it takes the `Cockpit` label off its whole conversation, and reopening it or undoing the dismiss puts the label back. Nothing else in the mailbox is touched, though Gmail's permission would allow it. Where Gmail refuses — the sign-in expired or revoked — the Item keeps what you did, the row says it is failing, and the label follows once the connection works again; one conversation Gmail will never relabel is given up on, the Item keeping what you did, so it holds up no other. A change made in Cockpit and not yet in Gmail wins over whatever Gmail did meanwhile, since Gmail does not say when a label changed. Disconnecting stops this: a change not yet in Gmail is dropped, and the Items stay as they are. **A Gmail connection follows one mark, chosen when connecting**: *Labelled `Cockpit`*, chosen to start and followed by every connection made before there was a choice, or *Starred (flagged in Outlook)* — Outlook, its phone app included, cannot label Gmail mail, and its flag for follow-up is stored by Gmail as a star. Everything above is the label's. **By star, only a conversation starred after connecting comes in**, within about five minutes and once, as the label's Items are made; one already starred never does — not on connecting, on connecting again, or at the nightly read — because Gmail records that a conversation is starred but never when. Its row reads "Gmail · starred · last checked …", and it never fails for want of a label called `Cockpit`. **Its Item is kept in step with the star both ways, as the label's is**: unstarring the conversation, or binning or deleting its mail, marks the Item done at the next check, and starring it again reopens the same Item, undismissed if it had been dismissed; marking the Item done or dismissing it takes the star off the whole conversation within seconds, and reopening it or undoing the dismiss puts the star back — the star alone, and nothing else. Refusals, and a change made in Cockpit winning, are as for the label. The nightly read lists what is starred only to mark done what is not, and makes no Item. The same mailbox followed by star in two Workspaces shares one star: done in one unstars it, and the other's Item is done at its next check. **What a connection follows changes without connecting again**: its row's *Change what's followed…* offers the same choice, starting at the mark followed now, and the row then names the new one. A switch counts from its own moment, as connecting does — to the star, nothing starred already comes in; to the label, every conversation labelled `Cockpit` does — and a change made in Cockpit and not yet in Gmail under the old mark is dropped, as disconnecting drops it. Items that came in under the other mark stay as they are and are not kept in step until the connection follows that mark again. Switching to the mark already followed changes nothing. Only a connection following the label fails for want of a label called `Cockpit`, so a failing one switched to the star stops failing at the next check. Slack, Notion and the rest are not built; see "Sources to connect" in `docs/ideas.md`.
