# Dashboards

**How an Item gets onto a Panel is shown until it has been done once**, at both ends of the gesture: while nothing in a Workspace has been filed, an empty Panel says how one arrives and the Inbox says it from the other side once it holds anything. Both name the row's own menu, since there is no drag from the Inbox to a Panel. The first filing anywhere in that Workspace ends both, the gesture having been done rather than read about.

**Dashboard** — a named view *inside* a Workspace, switched between like tabs. The bar under the workspace tabs is where they live: a `+` after them adds one, and each tab carries a menu of what can be done to it. **The Inbox is not a Dashboard**; it sits beside them (see "The Inbox and the triage flow" in `docs/product/inbox.md`). Every Workspace has at least one, and a Dashboard's name is unique within its Workspace, so two Workspaces may each have a *Research*.

**The Dashboards of a Workspace are in the order you put them in, left to right along their bar**, stored per Workspace and the same on every device. A new one goes last, after every Dashboard that Workspace has ever had; deleting one closes the gap. A tab is moved by dragging it along the bar, the gesture the Workspace tabs take and with the same preview and the same answer to a refusal (see "The Workspaces are in the order you put them in, left to right" in `docs/product/workspaces.md`). The Inbox is no Dashboard, so it neither moves nor gives up its place.

**A Dashboard is changed on its own tab**, exactly as a Workspace is and opened the same three ways: *Edit…* and *Delete*, on the tab that already names it. The form opens over the Workspace rather than replacing it, because renaming a Dashboard is a detour from working on one: the panels, the Inbox and the bar stay behind it, closing puts you back with nothing to reload, and there is no address for it. Adding is not on the tab — the `+` belongs on the bar the new tab will appear on. **What it opens is the same question a Panel is named in, and it says what a Dashboard is**: pressing `+` is the moment somebody is asking, and that is the moment worth answering in. The Inbox has no such menu, being no Dashboard of the Workspace. Renaming obeys the rules adding does, so *Research* may become *RESEARCH* while a name another Dashboard of that Workspace holds is refused with a message saying which. Deleting asks first in the same dialog as everywhere else and says what the Dashboard takes with it: how many Panels go with it, or that there is nothing on it. **A Workspace's last Dashboard cannot be deleted**, the one place the app refuses to delete something: the last Workspace may go because the app can offer to make one, while a Workspace with no Dashboard has no view at all. The entry is in the menu, unavailable, with that sentence on it, rather than offered and then refused. Deleting the one you were looking at moves the Workspace on behind it, its own address deciding where you land.

**Panel** — a movable, titled box on a Dashboard, sharing a row with up to three others, each a **saved view of the Items filed onto it**, with a user-editable title.

Panels are added, renamed, moved and deleted **on the Dashboard itself**, not on a settings page, because dragging one Panel past another *is* the editing and has to happen where the Panels are drawn. **Add a Panel from the control at the right of the Dashboard bar**, which is where the arrangement it joins is also chosen and which asks what to call it in the same question a Dashboard is named in, saying what a Panel is; each Panel carries a menu opened the three ways a Dashboard's or a Workspace's own tab opens its, plus a button on its header a tab does not carry (see "One menu, however it opens" in `docs/product/across-the-app.md`), offering rename, move to another dashboard and delete. Moving within a Dashboard is by dragging a Panel's header onto another Panel to join its row, or into the gap between two rows to take a row of its own. **The Panels move as the drag does**, so the arrangement under the hand is the one dropping will keep — the Panel in the air is drawn back in its slot, the rest step aside as it passes, and a placement that cannot happen, a row already four across, shows nothing happening rather than promising a move the drop would decline. Letting go keeps what is drawn; a drag that ends where it started changes nothing. **A Panel still has no size of its own — it fills its share of a row, and the row is what carries a height — but both are set by dragging the lines the row is drawn with.** The line **under a row** sets how tall it is, between 160 and 720 pixels so a row can neither swallow the screen nor shrink below a list worth reading, and double-clicking it puts the row back to being as tall as what is in it. The line **between two Panels** moves whole twelfths from one to the other, so the row still adds up to a whole and the Panels beside the pair keep their share. A size goes into the Layout on screen exactly as a move does — drawn as the hand goes, kept when it stops — so a row sized on *Wide* is untouched on *Phone*. Both lines are the pointer's, so sizing is absent from a keyboard and a touchscreen until entries that step them land instead. A title is required, stored trimmed, one line of at most 60 characters, and unique among a Dashboard's live Panels whatever the capitalization, so two Dashboards of one Workspace may each have a *Reading list*. Deleting asks first, in the same dialog as everywhere else, and says the Panel goes from every Layout of its Dashboard. **Every Dashboard arrives with one, called *Panel 1***, so a Workspace has somewhere to file an Item into from the moment it exists — the Inbox holds what no Panel holds, so a Dashboard with no Panels is an Inbox with no way out of it. That is a starting condition and not a rule: a Dashboard may end up with no Panels at all, unlike a Workspace, whose last Dashboard cannot be deleted. **A Panel holds the Items filed into it**, in an order you set, and drawing them is all it does — a rule for what *arrives* in one without being filed is designed, not built (see "Panel types and live rules" in `docs/ideas.md`).

**Layout** — one named arrangement of a Dashboard's Panels, remembering the screen width it was made at, so the same Dashboard reads well on a phone and on a 4K screen. A Dashboard may have several, and which one it is drawn with is decided below.

**A Panel holds Items, text, or what a rule gathers**, chosen when the Panel is made and never after. A Panel of **text** holds prose written straight onto the Dashboard — a standing agenda, a note the team reads, a heading over a row — and its own menu settles two things about it. It is **read-only** or open for writing; and its words are drawn as the **characters that were typed** or as **what they mean**. Neither is a conversion: the same Markdown is stored either way, so switching back and forth loses nothing. **Characters is the default**, because drawing them costs nothing to fetch, where formatting needs a renderer and writing formatted text needs an editor — so a Dashboard costs nothing to open until somebody asks. Read-only is what a Panel of text settles into, being a thing to read; only the Panel you have just made arrives open, because an empty box refusing to be written in is nonsense one menu away from the gesture that made it. **Nothing is filed onto a Panel of text**: it is not offered when filing, it takes no drop, and an Item asked onto it directly is refused — one filed there would leave the Inbox and be drawn nowhere.

**A Dashboard is an ordered list of rows.** Each holds up to four Panels, which fill its width between them and share its height; a Panel's width is its share of its row rather than a measurement, so the Dashboard never scrolls sideways on any device. **Nothing wraps**: which Panels share a line is something a person put there, not a consequence of their widths, so it survives the screen changing under it. Moving a Panel is therefore a drop — onto another Panel to join its row, or into a gap to take a row of its own — rather than a move to a coordinate, and there are no holes to leave behind.

A **Dashboard** is a saved arrangement of Panels; there can be as many per Workspace as you like. What a Panel of Items can be filtered or classified by beyond manual filing — a Project, a Person, a Topic, Focus, an AI classification, a live query — is designed, not built: see "Panel types and live rules" in `docs/ideas.md`.

## What a Panel shows — manual filing

**Filing.** Any Item can be filed onto a Panel from its own menu, which is what takes it out of the Inbox (see `docs/product/inbox.md`). An Item filed on several Panels appears on all of them: **Add to…** in a row's menu shows it on one more, and **Remove from this panel** stops one Panel showing it while every other carries on — an Item removed from the only Panel holding it is back in the Inbox. Dragging a row reorders it within the Panel it is already on — Ordering (see `docs/product/across-the-app.md`).

**A Panel of items is drawn in the order you set, unless it is sorted.** **Sort…** in its menu opens a question with a **Manual / Sorted** switch; on *Sorted* it holds one row per criterion — Title, Priority, Created, Due date or Type, each offered once — each **Ascending** or **Descending**, reordered with ↑ ↓ and added with *+ Then by…*, the last row never removable. Rows go by the first criterion, then the next wherever two tie, then the order you set. Ascending is A→Z ignoring case, Low→High, oldest first, soonest first and your Types' order, which hovering the direction says; a new Priority row starts on Descending, every other on Ascending. **An Item with no value for a criterion** — no title, Priority, due date or Type, or a Type since deleted — **goes last in either direction.** Flipping to Manual and back inside the question keeps the rows until Save; Cancel and Escape discard.

The sort is **kept on the Panel**, the same on every device, and **Manual is having none**: switching back gives the order you set, untouched. While sorted, **a row cannot be dragged to a new place in that Panel** — the pointer says no and no landing line is drawn — but Items are still filed onto it, going to the top of the order you set and drawn where the sort puts them. A **sort mark** beside the Panel's name, only while sorted, reads the sort back on hover — *Sorted: Due date ascending, then Priority descending*. A change of sort has no undo, as *Filter…* has none. Neither a Panel of text nor the Inbox is sorted; a Filter's rows have their own order, below.

**A drag held near the top or bottom edge of a box scrolls that box, faster the nearer the edge**, for an Item and a Panel alike, on a desktop. One box scrolls at a time and keeps scrolling until the pointer leaves its edge, so heading for a Panel further down scrolls the Dashboard rather than the Panels sliding past under the pointer; to scroll a Panel's own list, move into that Panel and then to its edge. The band sits inside the box's edge, never past it, so the Dashboard bar above — where resting a drag on a tab switches Dashboards — is not part of it. The Inbox column never scrolls under a drag, being sorted by age with nowhere in it to aim. A Panel drag scrolls only the Dashboard, and the arrangement drawn under the hand follows the scroll while the pointer is still.

## What a Filter shows — gathered by rule

**A Filter shows every open Item filed on a Panel of its Workspace that meets all its conditions, or any of them where it is set to *any***, wherever it is filed, so seeing everything due today no longer means opening every Dashboard in turn. **Never an Item still in the Inbox**, which is what keeps triaging worth doing, and an Item filed on two Panels appears once.

**Four conditions: Due date, Priority, Type and Panel**, each offered once per Filter. A Due date condition asks for a window — *overdue*, *today*, *this week* (Monday to Sunday), *this month*, *this quarter* or *not set* — the four periods carrying **or overdue**, ticked unless you untick it, because "due today" without it hides exactly the work most in need of looking at, measured against the calendar of whoever is looking. A Priority or Type condition takes several values and matches an Item holding any of them — *Priority is High or Normal*, *Type is OKR* — and an Item with no Priority, or no Type, matches neither. A Panel condition takes several Panels of items and matches an Item filed on any of them. A Type or Panel condition's values ignore one since deleted; left with none live, it matches nothing rather than every Type or Panel.

**All or any.** A Filter is set to *All of these* unless somebody chooses *Any of these*, which shows an Item meeting one condition where *All* asks for every one — *due this week or overdue, and High priority* in one list. The switch appears once there are two or more conditions; on *Any* the description says so and a small *or* sits between the cards. The choice is kept when conditions are removed down to one. A condition with nothing ticked adds nothing under *Any*, where under *All* it empties the Filter. Every Filter that was never asked means *All*, and so does a save that carries no setting — a stale tab saving over an *Any* Filter puts it back to *All*, and one tick undoes that. Mixes such as *(A and B) or C*, sets of conditions joined by *or*, are a decided design and not built (see "Panel types and live rules" in `docs/ideas.md`).

**Its own menu is where it is told what to show.** *Filter…* opens a question with one row per condition and *+ Add a condition* under them; saving with none left puts it back to saying it has nothing chosen. A funnel beside the Panel's name marks it, and hovering the funnel reads the conditions back as a sentence — *Any of: Due this week or overdue; Priority is High* under *Any*.

**Rows go by due date, then Priority, then oldest first, with no due date last** — a Filter is read top-down for what is closest, so a row that cannot say when it is wanted has nothing to be at the top about.

**Nothing is filed onto a Filter**, exactly as nothing is filed onto a Panel of text: it is not offered when filing, it takes no drop, and an Item asked onto it directly is refused. A row's menu is the usual one without *Remove from this panel*, and rows are not reordered — where a row sits is a consequence of the Item rather than a filing anybody made.

## Layouts: the arrangement that follows your screen

A 4K screen fits more Panels side by side than a laptop, which fits more than a phone, so a Dashboard can hold **several Layouts**. Each one **has a name** and **records the screen width it was made at**. That width is whatever the screen happened to be, not one of a fixed set of breakpoints, so there is nothing to belong to and nothing to configure; it is read only when a screen is matched to a Layout.

**A Layout is named, and the name is what you pick it by.** Required, stored trimmed, one line of at most 60 characters, and unique among its Dashboard's Layouts whatever the capitalization — the rules a Panel's title obeys, in the same scope, so two Dashboards of one Workspace may each have a *Wide*.

**A Dashboard is drawn with the Layout nearest the screen in front of you**, a tie going to the narrower so two devices of the same size agree. Moving a window to another monitor redraws it there, and a monitor no Layout was made for gets the nearest one rather than a Layout of its own — making one is always yours to do, never something that happens because a screen appeared.

**Which Layout a Dashboard is drawn with is a control at the right of its own bar**, beside the tab whose arrangement it names, along with the way to add a Panel. Both are drawn only where a Dashboard is open, since the bar is also the Inbox's. The control names the Layout in use; its menu lists each Layout by name with the width it was made at, and offers *New layout from this one*, *Rename* and *Delete*. **Picking one by name overrides the screen only while you are on that screen**: the pick lasts as long as the nearest Layout would not have changed anyway, so resizing a window or opening the devtools keeps it and moving to another monitor drops it. Making a Layout puts you on it the same way, on the screen you made it for. Picks are remembered in the browser rather than stored, since the whole point of Layouts is that the phone and the desktop want different ones. A Layout that is deleted, or a picked one that another device deletes, simply falls back to the nearest remaining.

**Changing the arrangement changes the Layout you are on, and asks nothing.** Dragging a Panel goes into it, whatever screen you are on.

**A Dashboard that has never been arranged is drawn to fit the screen it is on**: the Panels in the order they were added, on rows of a count that keeps a Panel worth reading — one across on a phone, three on a laptop, four on anything wider. It has no Layout at all, and the first move records one, named for the size of screen it was made on — *Phone*, *Tablet*, *Laptop* or *Wide*, and yours to change. **A Dashboard that has a Layout keeps one**, so deleting the last is refused with that sentence on the entry: what it protects is an arrangement somebody made, not the existence of a row.
