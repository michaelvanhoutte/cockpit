# Glossary

- **Account** — the person or organization Cockpit holds work for, and the outermost boundary: everything belongs to exactly one, and nothing is shared between two. Each User has one. Not the *source accounts* (a Gmail login, a Slack workspace) a Workspace connects to. The **register** is the list of which Accounts exist, who the Users are and who is signed in; it is what says an Account is real before any of its data is opened.
- **Workspace** — top-level context inside an Account and the privacy boundary (Work, Personal, Customer 1…); defines which sources are connected.
- **Source account** — an account at a source that a Workspace is **connected** to: a Microsoft Teams, Gmail or Outlook sign-in today. **It belongs to the Workspace that connected it** — no other Workspace sees it, and deleting the Workspace takes its connections with it. Connecting signs you in at the source and asks who you are — and, for Gmail and Outlook, permission to change your mail, so Cockpit can take its label, star, flag or category off. A connector that asks one question of a connection (what to bring in) has Connect ask it first, shows the answer on the row, and changes it from the row's *Change…* without connecting again. A Gmail connection follows one mark, chosen when connecting and changed from its row without connecting again: the `Cockpit` label, chosen to start, or the star, which is what Outlook's flag for follow-up sets on Gmail mail. An Outlook connection, for a Microsoft 365 work or school account, follows one mark likewise: the flag for follow-up, chosen to start, or the category `Cockpit`. The same account connected again refreshes what is there rather than adding a second, and a Workspace can connect several accounts of one source. **Disconnecting forgets the sign-in outright**, and for Gmail revokes it at Google too unless another Workspace still holds the mailbox (Microsoft offers Outlook no such revoke), which is what makes it worth asking about first: nothing Cockpit already holds goes, and connecting again is a fresh sign-in at the source. A connection Cockpit **checks** — Gmail and Outlook, about every five minutes — keeps a **history** of its checks for 30 days, opened from its row's *History…*; a Microsoft Teams connection keeps one line per save, naming the Item it became, or a failure, in the same place. Managed in Settings: Gmail, Outlook and Microsoft Teams under *Connections*, for one Workspace picked at the top.
- **Connected app** — an app such as Claude that somebody signed in with Google has allowed, on Cockpit's **consent page**, to create Items in their Cockpit, to read their Items in every Workspace, and to tell Cockpit how a task sent to it is going, and to do nothing else. It belongs to that person, not to a Workspace, and acts as them only while they still have access: deleting them or taking their access away cuts it off at once. Every Item it captures shows its name. It may ask to capture 60 times a minute, a refused ask counting too, and to find Items 10 times a minute and 500 a day. Never the guest's, whose account everybody shares.
- **Dashboard** — a switchable named view inside a Workspace, holding a layout of Panels.
- **All items** — a tab after a Workspace's Dashboards, switched on from the Workspace's menu, listing every Item of the Workspace in one sortable table, finished ones included, which a Dashboard's filter bar narrows (its Status offers *Done* too) and which is drawn fifty rows at a time.
- **Panel** — a movable, titled box on a Dashboard, sharing a row with up to three others, its title unique within its Dashboard. It holds **the Items filed onto it, text you write in it, or the Items a rule gathers onto it** (a **Filter panel**, below), and which of the three is settled when the Panel is made and never after. A Panel of text is **read-only** or written in, and its words are drawn as the characters typed or as what they mean — both the Panel's own state, so the same for everybody, and neither a change to the text itself. Nothing is ever filed onto a Panel of text.
- **Filter panel** — a Panel that shows every open Item filed on a Panel of its Workspace meeting every row of its **conditions**, rather than the Items filed onto it. Never one still in the Inbox, which is what keeps triaging worth doing. A funnel beside its name marks it and reads its conditions back on hover; until it has one it says to choose what it shows from its own menu. Its rows go by its sort — until somebody chooses one, Due date, then Priority, then oldest — and they are neither reordered nor filed: nothing is ever filed onto a Filter panel. They can be grouped under a heading per Dashboard or Panel.
- **Dashboard filter** — six conditions, all of which must hold, narrowing what the Panels of items and Filter panels already on one Dashboard show: **Status**, **Priority**, **Due**, **Containing**, **Attachments** and **Agent running**. It belongs to its Dashboard and is kept in this browser; it hides every Panel with no matching Item, a Panel of text included, and never touches the Inbox. Not a Filter panel, which gathers from the whole Workspace and replaces nothing on screen.
- **Sort** — how a Panel of items draws its rows: one or more **criteria** (Title, Priority, Created, Due date), each Ascending or Descending, the next breaking the ties the one before it left. **Manual** is having none — the order you set by dragging — and is what every Panel starts as. A sorted Panel carries a **sort mark** beside its name that reads the sort back on hover.
- **Never propose** — a setting on a Panel of items, set by unticking *Suggest <Panel> for new items in the Inbox* in its Panel settings, that keeps Cockpit from suggesting it: no Inbox chip names it, and Items filed onto it are not learned from. Clearing it restores both.
- **Clear** and **Closest match** — how sure a suggested Panel is: *clear* where the note plainly belongs there, *closest* where it only fits there better than on any other Panel. A closest match's Inbox chip is dashed and fainter and files the same; a suggestion that says neither is clear.
- **Condition** — one rule a Filter panel's Items can meet. Conditions sit in rows: an Item must meet every row, and a row is met by any one of its conditions. Each field appears at most once per row, and in any number of rows. A **Due date** condition asks for a window: *overdue*, *today*, *this week* (Monday to Sunday), *this month*, *this quarter* or *not set* — the four periods widened by *or overdue* unless it is unticked — measured against the calendar of whoever is looking. A **Priority** condition takes several values and matches an Item holding any of them; an Item with no Priority never matches. An **Agent running** condition takes no values and matches an Item with an open run in any state.
- **Layout** — a Dashboard's arrangement of its Panels: which rows they are on, in what order along each, and the share of the row each takes. A Dashboard has at most one, made by its first arrangement; nobody picks one. From 480 px up the Dashboard is drawn with it, and below that one Panel to a row, in the order the Layout is read.
- **Section** — a titled row of a Dashboard's Layout holding no Panels, drawn as a band across the board to head the rows under it. Not a Panel: nothing is filed onto it and nothing counts it.
- **Inbox** — the one place in a Workspace holding what has arrived and not been dealt with: every open Item filed on no Panel. Beside the Dashboards rather than one of them, and never renamed or deleted, because it is not a Panel — it is what is left when nothing holds an Item.
- **Item** — the single object everything is stored as, whether it arrived from a source or was created in the app.
- **Agent** — retired: a name and a message template that started a Claude Code routine on an Item from the **dock** at the bottom of the screen. Each became a remembered sentence when Agents were retired, and its row is kept, since the Runs it started still name it.
- **Run** — one Item handed to Claude: sent to Cowork or Claude Code with *Send to Claude…* — *Sent*, then *on it*, *Ready* or *Needs you* as Claude reports back — or, before Agents were retired, started by an Agent, which it still names (*working*, *waiting on you*, *didn't start* or *unknown*). An Item carries at most one open run, and the Item's Status ends it — *Done* or *To do* (*In progress* leaves it open), or dismissing the Item — however the change was made, a source closing the Item included, and *Undo* after Done or Dismiss brings it back with its chip and session link; a run Claude refused is also ended by the next send. A run outlives its Agent: one whose Agent was deleted names "a deleted agent".
- **Task** — an Item representing something to do. One source Item can produce several; the *next-action label* keeps that word and is a different thing.
- **Note** — an Item created in the app as a note or idea, with no source behind it.
- **Captured message** — what arrived, or what you said, as it stood when the Item was made. Never edited afterwards, and shown only on the form's *Details* tab, so there is always a way back to what was originally captured.
- **Title** — the short name an Item goes by. Yours to edit, not required, and what a row shows when there is no next action. **Proposed** for a captured note until you edit it.
- **Description** — what you have to say about an Item: as long as it needs to be, edited on the Item's form. Formatted text, stored as Markdown, and editable as either. **Proposed** for a captured note until you edit it.
- **Attachment** — a file added to an Item: an image, a short clip or a PDF, added on the Item's own form below Description by a button, by dropping or pasting it on the form - or, for an image, by putting it in the description - or while capturing a note, by the same button, drop or paste on the Capture form, or by sharing it to the installed app from Android's share sheet, which has no description to put an image in instead. Queued as a chip until Capture is pressed, then kept with the note and uploaded once the Item is made. Drawn as a chip either way — a thumbnail for an image, an icon with its name and size for anything else — and a click opens or downloads it. Removing one takes it off the Item; the file it named is not undone by anything else about it having existed.
- **Proposed** — written for you by Cockpit rather than by you, and replaceable by Cockpit until you edit it; from your first edit it is yours and nothing overwrites it again. Today the Title and the Description of a captured note are proposed together, so editing either settles both. The same rule holds wherever Cockpit proposes something: it may replace what it proposed and never what you settled.
- **Reading** — one other way a captured note could genuinely be read, offered beside the one Cockpit proposed: its own title and description, and a few words saying what that reading takes the note to mean. Offered only where the difference would change what you do about the note, and rarely at all. Choosing one fills the two boxes, exactly as typing them in by hand would — it is Save that settles them, not the choosing.
- **What Cockpit changed** — each time Cockpit changed a captured note's proposed Title or Description or its **suggested Panel** (the Panel Cockpit proposes filing it on, which a filed Item never has), or set its priority, due date or workspace, or filed it on panels (a *Panels* line naming them as they were when filed), from what the note said: when you captured it, or after you filed another (a row kept from when a correction still re-read the Inbox says *after you edited another Item*). A time it looked and changed nothing, or one still working or failed, is not a change of its own. Each says what it changed, field by field, with what it was and what it became, naming a suggested Panel as "Dashboard ▸ Panel" (the wording the Inbox's chip and the All items table's *Where it is* column use), or as "a deleted panel" where it or its Dashboard has since been deleted. An Item lists its own as the third tab of its form, newest first, and says in one line under the tabs that Cockpit changed its title or description until you have seen or edited that change; the **What Cockpit changed** window, opened from the Inbox's own menu, lists them for the whole Inbox.
- **Association** — a link from an Item to a Person, Project or Topic; many-to-many, which is why an Item can appear in several Panels.
- **Capture** — creating an Item directly in the app instead of receiving it from a source.
- **Status** — To do, In progress or Done: which of the three an Item is, worked out from **when** it was started and **when** it was finished with rather than stored as a word of its own. Starting one and finishing with one are each a time, recorded on marking it so; going back to To do clears the start time it recorded. Undoing Done returns to In progress rather than to To do, since it kept whatever start time it already carried.
- **Priority** — low / normal / high importance, independent of the Focus horizon. Shown as a flag at the head of every row, coloured by level, and a faint grey one where none is set, so every title starts in the same place.
- **Due date** — a calendar date an Item is due by, or none. Tints its row by how close it is, warming as the deadline nears and turning solid red once it has passed.
- **Next action** — the short, always-editable label describing what to actually do about an Item.
- **Focus horizon** — Today / This Week / This Month / This Quarter, date-anchored so it escalates to overdue.
- **Possible duplicate** — a note Cockpit judges may say what another already said; its row wears a *Duplicate?* chip, and its form offers **Dismiss this one** and **Not a duplicate**.
- **Triage / process** — dealing with what arrived: associating an Item, filing it where it belongs, and finishing with it or dismissing it.
- **Undo** — putting back what the last change took away, offered briefly after it and never afterwards. One step, not a stack: what an accident needs is a way back, offered where you are looking. The bar after an Item is filed onto a Panel also offers **Show** and **Also show on…** (see the Inbox's "File it on a Panel" in `docs/product/inbox.md`).
- **Saving sign** — the small pulsing cloud in the header, left of the account menu, shown while a change made in this tab has waited 500 ms for the server (see Offline in `docs/product/offline.md`).
- **Form** — where more than one field is edited at once: a modal with a route of its own, so the back button closes it and a link opens it. **Save** writes; Cancel, Escape and pressing outside discard without asking. An Item's form is a page of its own below 768px that opens to be read, with nothing focused: **←** (closes it), *Item*, **Copy link** and **Edit** in a header. **Edit**, or a tap on the title or the description, switches to editing, with **←** (Cancel), **Copy link** and **Save** in the header, no Dock, and its status, priority and due date as chips; both return to reading. Docked, an Item's form has no Save: each field is written as it is finished, and a plain click on another row shows that Item in it instead of opening nothing, and capturing a note moves it to the new note without taking the keyboard from the capture box. It has three tabs: **Item**, the fields you edit, with an *On panels* field listing the Panels the Item is filed on and changing them; **Details**, the record — what was captured, the source, the Item's id (with a way to copy it) and when it was made and last changed; and **What Cockpit changed**, always there, listing each time Cockpit changed the Item.
- **Filing** — putting an Item on a Panel. An Item can be filed on several Panels at once, which is why one thing to do can appear on *Project Falcon* and on *Anna*; one filed nowhere is in the Inbox. Each filing carries its own place in that Panel's order, so an Item can be first on one Panel and fifth on another.
- **Offline** — working from the local copy when the connection is not there. Cockpit opens instantly and shows what it already holds; a capture made offline is kept on the device, *waiting*, until a connection gets through, and no other write made offline is queued or retried (see `docs/product/offline.md`). Nothing new arrives from a source until the connection is back.
- **User** — a person who uses this Cockpit. Each User owns one Account, which is what makes two Users' work separate. A User has a **Role**.
- **Role** — *user* or *admin*. An admin is offered **Platform settings**; everyone else is refused it, by the server rather than by not being offered the way in. It is read afresh on every request, so a role taken away applies to the next thing that person does without ending the sign-in they hold.
- **Disabled** — somebody whose access was taken away. They cannot sign in, the sign-ins they were holding are ended, and **everything they own stays exactly as it was** — which is what makes it the reversible half of removing somebody, and why deleting a User is rarely the thing to reach for. Their row stays in the list, marked, and enabling them puts them back; the sign-in they held is not revived, so they sign in afresh. Trying to sign in tells them their access was removed rather than that their Google account cannot sign in here: it can, and was stopped, and the person it happens to is a colleague who would otherwise go looking for a problem that is not theirs.
- **Platform settings** — a window over the Workspace, from the profile menu after *Settings…* and not on a phone, where an admin manages the Cockpit rather than an Account. Its sections are **Users** and **Usage**, each headed by its own name. **Users**: today, who can sign in at all, adding somebody, renaming one or changing their Role, taking somebody's access away or giving it back (**Disabled**, above), and deleting somebody. **Adding takes a name and the Google address they sign in with, and nothing else**: everyone arrives ordinary, and the Account they own is made with them rather than chosen. **A Role is changed on the person's own row afterwards, and never taken from the last admin or from yourself** — either would leave Users openable by nobody, with no way back but the bootstrap the environment was seeded from. **The same two refusals guard Disabling and deleting**, since an admin who cannot sign in, or no longer exists, is no more use than one who is not an admin. **Deleting somebody takes the Account they own with them**: their sign-ins end, everything the Account holds is destroyed, and then they leave the register — so whoever is next given their name, added here or signing in for the first time, starts with an empty Account rather than inheriting theirs, and the person deleted who signs in again is somebody new. It is asked first, naming how many Workspaces go with them, and only a Backup taken beforehand brings any of it back. It is also refused for an Account another User uses too — which only a restored register can produce — since destroying it would take their work as well. **Renaming somebody renames the Account they own**, which is named after them. The address is not editable: it is what somebody signs in by, and changing it is a question of its own. Two people may share a name — the address is what a Cockpit holds to one person — and somebody added is in the register before they ever sign in, under the name the admin gave them rather than Google's. **The one part of the app that crosses Accounts**, since who exists is the environment's question and not any one Account's.
- **Usage** — a section of **Platform settings** beside **Users**, offered to an admin only and refused to anybody else by the server. It shows each named person's latest sign-in and their sign-ins over a window of days (30 by default, 365 at most), and guest sessions per UTC day, by country and by referrer, with what guests captured and opened. Guests are never listed as people; a missing country or referrer reads as unknown. It links to Cloudflare Web Analytics for page traffic when an address is configured, and holds none of that traffic itself.
- **AI usage** — the **record** Cockpit keeps of every call it makes to Claude, one per attempt, a retried or failed one included: when, what was asked (cleaning up a note, or choosing its Panel again after a filing), what started it, for which User, on which model, paid for by which key, how it ended, how long it took and what it spent in tokens. Never the note, the answer, or the key beyond its last 4 characters. Kept 12 months, and kept when the User it names is deleted. No screen shows it.
- **Sign in** — proving which User you are, with your **Google account**, from the **logon page**. Any Google account gets in: one the register has never seen is made an ordinary User, called what Google calls them, with an Account of their own. An address the register holds for a *different* Google account is refused and told so. A sign-in lasts a set time, renews while used, and expires on its own, and Cockpit says so rather than failing at you.
- **Google account** — the account somebody signs in with. The register holds the address it recognises them by the first time and, once they have signed in, the identity Google keys them by, so a changed address neither locks them out nor lets a new owner of it in.
- **Guest** — somebody who pressed *Continue as guest* on the logon page instead of signing in with a Google account. There is **one guest account and everybody who does this shares it**, so two guests see each other's work; it is made the first time anybody asks for it, and it is offered on the deployment meant to be shown to strangers rather than on every one. **It opens on a demonstration** — three Workspaces of a contractor's week, their Dashboards, Panels, Filters and Items already there and something in each Inbox, every due date counted from the day it was put back so something is always due today, and no Panel holding more than five Items — with Gmail and Microsoft Teams shown connected in every Workspace and some Items that came from them, naming their sender, whose *Open ↗* opens a Cockpit page saying where the mail or message would open rather than the source, and some Items already sent to Cowork or Claude Code, at every stage Claude reports — rather than on the empty starter every other Account is given, so a stranger sees Cockpit working instead of a blank page. **It goes back to that demonstration every night, and whenever an operator asks**: whatever guests added, changed or deleted is gone, and nothing a guest does is kept. Ordinary in every other way: the same session, the same commands, the same rules.
- **Sign out** — ending a sign-in deliberately. Cockpit forgets it, the All items tab included, and the browser is left holding nothing of what you were looking at — including any capture still waiting to be sent, which is why it asks first when there is one.
- **Backup** — a copy of everything an environment holds, written to local files: the register, and each Account's own data in a file of its own. Taken by an operator with a command, never from inside the app, and it holds no sign-ins — those are re-made by signing in, and putting an old one back would revive a sign-in somebody ended. Taking one changes nothing about the environment it reads.
- **Restore** — putting a Backup back, a whole environment or one User's Account. It **replaces** an Account rather than merging into it: what that Account held is gone, which is why one that already holds anything is refused unless replacing it was asked for. The register is only ever added to — a User the Backup needs and the register lacks is created, one already there is left alone, and a Backup that disagrees with the register about who somebody is refuses rather than deciding.

**Menu**, **Deleting**, **Ordering**, **Live updates**, **Selection**, **Updating** and **Appearance** are shared behaviour, defined once in `docs/product/across-the-app.md` rather than here — a shared behaviour with no word is one nobody can say is tested (`tools/test-explorer/concepts.json`).

This glossary is binding on anything written to be read as a description of the product, test names included (see `docs/testing-strategy.md`, "Tests are named in the product's language"). Words that live only in `docs/architecture.md` — command, envelope, tombstone, idempotency, last-write-wins — are implementation and stay there.
