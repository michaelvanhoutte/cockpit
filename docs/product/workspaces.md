# Workspaces

Everything is built from a **container hierarchy**: **Workspace → Dashboard → Panel**.

**Workspace** — everything you want in front of you while you work in one context, and the privacy boundary. It controls **which source accounts are connected and visible**, so private email is invisible while in *Work*: scoping rather than filtering after the fact.

**What decides between a Workspace and a Dashboard is whether the whole context changes**, and the pair of cases is the definition: a contractor working for two customers wants a Workspace each — different accounts, different mail, nothing shared — while somebody at one company serving two customers wants one Workspace and a Dashboard per customer, because the context is the same and only the view of it changes. The obvious advice, *one for work, one for personal, one per customer*, gives the second of those the wrong answer, which is why the app says this in the question that makes one rather than leaving it to be inferred.

Each Workspace has a **colour** that follows it into three places only: the dashboard band, the accent (headings, buttons, focus rings, type labels, and the menus and windows opened over the page) and the dot in the logo. The page and the top bar are the same neutral in every Workspace, so a Workspace's colour never washes the screen. The tab you are on is filled with the band and runs down into it. Colours are picked as a set from a fixed palette, on the form the Workspace's own tab opens (open decision #13); the palette and its contrast rules are in `docs/design-system.md`. A Workspace is drawn in the theme its colour names, whatever else it stored; a colour the palette never had is drawn in the default theme. Nothing stored is rewritten.

**A Workspace is made from the `+` at the end of the tabs**, which opens the question a Dashboard and a Panel are named in and says what a Workspace is. **Everything else is on the tab itself, and on the header's own button for the tab you are on**: the menu holds *Edit…*, then *Show All items tab* (*Hide All items tab* once it is on) in a group of its own, since it switches a tab of the Workspace rather than editing it, with *Recurring items* beside it, and *Delete* last. It acts on the Workspace whose menu it is, so another Workspace's tab can be switched without opening it; hiding it while that Workspace's *All items* is on screen returns to its first Dashboard (see `docs/product/dashboards.md`). There is one entry for changing a Workspace rather than a *Rename* beside it, the form being what renames. **The tab opens its own menu three ways, one per input**: a right-click, a press on the tab you are already on (that press has no other job, and it is what a finger has), and the keyboard's menu key — and the Workspace you are on also carries the same menu as a visible **…** at the right of the header (see "One menu, however it opens" in `docs/product/across-the-app.md`). **The name and colour are changed together, on a form over the page**; nothing is sent until *Save*, so a colour is chosen and looked at rather than applied on the press, and *Cancel*, *Escape* and a press outside all discard both halves. ***Save* closes the form at once and shows the new name and colour on the tab, and the page in the colour, before the server has answered**; a name the server refuses goes back, and the undo bar says why in the server's words. **Adding closes the question at once and opens the new Workspace on its first Dashboard and Panel before the server has answered**, from the + and from the screen that makes the first; a name the server refuses takes it away, puts you back where you were, and the undo bar says why. A name is required, stored trimmed, one line of at most 60 characters, and unique among live Workspaces whatever the capitalization. **Deleting a Workspace keeps everything that was in it**: its Items stay filed against it, because the router learns from the decisions filed there (see [routing-learning.md](routing-learning.md)). The name becomes available again. **Deleting takes the tab away at once**, and deleting the one you are in lands on the next Workspace at once; a refused delete brings the tab back and the undo bar says why. The last Workspace can be deleted, and the app then opens on the screen that makes one.

**The Workspaces are in the order you put them in, left to right.** A new Workspace goes last, after every Workspace the account has ever had; deleting one closes the gap. A tab is moved by dragging it along the strip — the pointer's alone, absent on a touchscreen and unreachable from a keyboard. **The tabs move as the drag does**, so the strip under the hand is the order dropping would keep. A move shows itself before the server agrees, so a second move can follow a first and a dropped tab does not snap back for a round trip. If it is refused, because a Workspace was made or deleted in another tab, the strip goes back and the undo bar says why.

**Ask up front only what has to be decided up front, and teach the rest at first use.** An account arrives holding one Workspace called *Workspace 1* with one Dashboard, *Dashboard 1*, and opens on that Workspace, asking nothing. **The Dashboard holds a Panel called *Getting started* and, beside it, the empty *Panel 1***; *Getting started* holds the steps of finding your way round Cockpit, each an open Task ticked off with its row's ✓, and the Inbox holds one Task saying what the Inbox is for. They are ordinary Items and an ordinary Panel, renamed, filed and deleted like any other, and only an account nobody has used gets them — never one somebody has, and never the shared guest account. Nothing is asked at sign-in: nobody knows when they want a second Workspace or Dashboard until they have used the first for a while, so each is explained where it is made (the `+` dialogs), not before.

The full path to any box:

```
Workspace: "Work"
├── Dashboard: "Today"       ← landing dashboard
│   ├── Panel: "Focus Today"
│   ├── Panel: "Project Falcon"
│   └── Panel: "People to talk to"
├── Dashboard: "Dormant projects"
│   └── Panel: "On hold"
└── Dashboard: "Research"
    └── Panel: "To read"
```

The landing dashboard is the desk landing: a bare `/` opens the first Workspace's dashboard at a desk, and the Capture page on a phone (see `docs/product/inbox.md`).

**Settings › Connections shows each connection as a card**, under a header reading "5 connected · 1 needs attention" and an *Add connection* button that lists every source with its logo, name and sentence; with nothing connected the button gives way to a gallery of the same sources, each with its own *Connect*, and the guest is told to sign in with Google to connect their own where the button would be. Picking a source opens its Connect window, which says its sentence above its question (Teams asks nothing, so its window is only the sentence and *Connect*); Text messages asks for the number instead. A card shows the source's logo, the account in bold with the source under it, and a badge: *Healthy* for a connection Cockpit checks, *Listening* for one its source pushes to, both green, or *Needs attention* in red. Under them are a chip for what it follows and, while it files by name, "Files by label" or "Files by category", and while it gives a due date, "Due 3 days after", then "Checked 2 minutes ago" (for one that follows something) or "Worked 1 hour ago", with the exact time on hover; a pushed connection never tested says "Sends to Cockpit as it happens", and a phone says which Cockpit number to text. *Check now* (pulled connections) and *History* (those that keep one) are buttons on the card, and its ⋯ menu keeps every entry. **A disconnect, a change from *Change…* and *Check now*'s note show the moment you press**: the card is gone, or names the new choice and whether it files by name and gives a due date, within a tenth of a second and whatever is read meanwhile, and *Change…* closes at *Save*. One the server refuses puts the card back and says why in the undo bar. A failing card has a red stripe down its left edge and says why in a box that opens its history where it keeps one, with *Reconnect* beside it. A Workspace connects its sources in Settings › Connections: a Microsoft Teams account, whose saved messages land in its Inbox, Gmail accounts and Outlook mailboxes. **Save to Cockpit on a Teams message offers a *Note (optional)* box**, hinted *What to do with it, e.g. call back Friday, high priority, on Admin and money*, which Cockpit keeps with the message and reads with it as it reads a note typed into Capture, your words winning where they and the sender's disagree (see `docs/product/items.md`); left empty, the message is saved as it is. The box changes only once the app package is uploaded to Teams again. **Connecting Teams asks Microsoft for permission to read the chats and channel posts you paste links to, and asks again for less where an administrator has to approve it**: chats and channel posts first, then chats alone, then nothing, which is a connection that reads no messages. You choose nothing; a sign-in cancelled at Microsoft, or any other failure, ends the connect as it always did and asks nothing less. The card says what it got: *Reads pasted chat links* where chats were granted, with *Channel posts need your IT's approval* beside it where channel posts were not; a connection granting nothing, or holding no record of a grant, says neither. Connecting the same Teams account again, or *Reconnect* on a failing one, asks from the top, so an approval given since is picked up on the same card. Save to Cockpit works whatever was granted. Every conversation labelled `Cockpit` in a connected mailbox, whatever its age, arrives in the Workspace's Inbox within a minute of connecting, one Item per conversation ever, titled with the subject — "(no subject)" where there is none — described with the plain text of the most recent labelled message, from "Gmail · sender", opening the conversation in Gmail; the usual clean-up then runs. Each mailbox is checked about every five minutes, and its card reads "Checked …", or says why it is failing — no label called `Cockpit` in the mailbox (create it in Gmail), or Google no longer accepts the sign-in (connect again), and a failing row's menu offers *Reconnect*, which signs in again on the same row; connecting the same mailbox again finds what is already in rather than bringing it in twice. **A Gmail connection made before Gmail moved onto the connector SDK reads as failing until it is reconnected, once**: nothing it brought in is moved or removed, and its old Items stay as plain Items, so a conversation still labelled comes in again beside its old Item, which you clear by hand. The same mailbox connected to two Workspaces gives each its own Items. A conversation labelled afterwards, by hand or by a Gmail filter, arrives within about five minutes too, once; a reply in a conversation already in adds nothing. A mailbox that has not been checked for about a week is read from the start again, so nothing labelled meanwhile is missed. When the label comes off a conversation, or its mail is binned or deleted, its Item is marked done at the next check; labelled again, the same Item reopens, undismissed if it had been dismissed. A mailbox with no label called `Cockpit` marks nothing done. **The other way, within seconds:** marking a Gmail Item done or dismissing it takes the `Cockpit` label off its whole conversation, and reopening it or undoing the dismiss puts the label back. Nothing else in the mailbox is touched, though Gmail's permission would allow it. Where Gmail refuses — the sign-in expired or revoked — the Item keeps what you did, the row says it is failing, and the label follows once the connection works again; one conversation Gmail will never relabel is given up on, the Item keeping what you did, so it holds up no other. A change made in Cockpit and not yet in Gmail wins over whatever Gmail did meanwhile, since Gmail does not say when a label changed. Disconnecting stops this: a change not yet in Gmail is dropped, and the Items stay as they are. **A Gmail connection follows one mark, chosen when connecting**: *Labelled `Cockpit`*, chosen to start and followed by every connection made before there was a choice, or *Starred (flagged in Outlook)* — Outlook, its phone app included, cannot label Gmail mail, and its flag for follow-up is stored by Gmail as a star. Everything above is the label's. **By star, an email starred after connecting brings its conversation in as one Item that follows it**, within about five minutes and once, with the status of whoever wrote last in it as a message would set it (below): titled with that email's subject, described with its plain text, from its sender, and filed by the labels on any of the conversation's emails. Starring another email in the same conversation adds nothing new, and a conversation a connection following the label brought in is the same Item. An email already starred never comes in — not on connecting, nor on connecting again — because Gmail records that an email is starred but never when. Its card carries the chip *Starred (flagged in Outlook)*, and it never fails for want of a label called `Cockpit`. **Every later message in a followed conversation, starred or not, sets the Item's status by who wrote it**: one sent from this mailbox (Gmail's Sent label, whatever address it was sent as) makes it Waiting for reply, due after the Follow-ups delay (Settings › *Follow-ups*); anyone else's makes it To do, due after the connection's *Give it a due date* days, else the Follow-ups delay. Each replaces the due date, one you set included, within about five minutes of the message arriving; a status you set by hand after that check stays until the next message. A draft is no message. **Its row says who wrote last in place of "Gmail · sender"** — *Gmail · Steven replied yesterday*, *Gmail · You wrote 2 days ago*, *Gmail · Steven wrote 3 days ago* for the first message — with that message's first lines on hover, and its *Open ↗*, titled *Open the latest email in Gmail*, opens the conversation; the title stays what it was. **Done ends the following**: marking the Item done or dismissing it takes the star off every email of the conversation within seconds, so a later message in it changes nothing; starring any email in it again reopens the same Item, undismissed if it had been dismissed, with the status of whoever wrote last in it rather than the one it had, and it follows again. Unstarring every email, or binning or deleting the starred ones, marks the Item done at the next check, and reopening a followed Item in Cockpit while no email in it is starred changes nothing in Gmail: the Item stays open with its last line and stops following, so nothing in the conversation changes it, until an email in it is starred again. **An Item a star made of one email before conversations were followed stays as it was**: it never follows its conversation, it is marked done when that email's star comes off or the email is binned, marking it done or reopening it takes that email's star off or puts it back, and starring its conversation again brings the conversation in beside it as an Item of its own. Refusals, and a change made in Cockpit winning, are as for the label. A full read of what is starred, on connecting or after a week unchecked, only marks done what is not, and makes no Item. The same mailbox followed by star in two Workspaces shares each conversation's stars: done in one unstars the conversation, and the other's Item for it is done at its next check; a message in it sets the status only of the Item that follows it in each Workspace. **What a connection follows changes without connecting again**: its row's *Change…* offers the same choice, starting at the mark followed now, and the row then names the new one. A switch counts from its own moment, as connecting does — to the star, nothing starred already comes in; to the label, every conversation labelled `Cockpit` does — and a change made in Cockpit and not yet in Gmail under the old mark is dropped, as disconnecting drops it. Items that came in under the other mark stay as they are and are not kept in step until the connection follows that mark again. Switching to the mark already followed changes nothing. Only a connection following the label fails for want of a label called `Cockpit`, so a failing one switched to the star stops failing at the next check. **A connector may ask one question of a connection**, such as what to bring in: Connect then asks it first, the row names the answer, and its *Change…* asks again without signing in, dropping any change to an Item not yet passed on to the source; the connector picks the new answer up at its next check. The tick boxes under it are the connection's own, not the connector's: filing by name for a connector that reports names, and a due date for every connector Cockpit pulls from. **An Outlook mailbox** — a Microsoft 365 work or school account; a personal Outlook.com or Hotmail one is refused — is connected the same way, apart from the Teams account and asking Microsoft for permission to read and change mail. Its card says the organisation may have to approve Cockpit first, and a failing row says when that is why; Connect asks which mark to follow, *Flagged for follow-up*, chosen to start, or *Category `Cockpit`*, with one line under them: *Importance and other categories don't bring mail in.* Only the follow-up flag counts, not importance or any other category. Every message carrying the mark, whatever its age, arrives as an Item — **by category, one per message; by flag, one per conversation, which the Item follows** ("Outlook follows the flagged conversation as Gmail does", issue 1707), so two flagged messages in one thread are one Item, and a flag put on again after Done reopens it — titled with the flagged message's subject ("(no subject)" where there is none), described with its plain text, opening it in Outlook on the web. **Every later message in a followed conversation, flagged or not, in any folder, sets the Item's status by who wrote it, as for Gmail**: anyone else's makes it To do, and the row reads *Outlook · Steven replied yesterday*, *Open ↗* going to the conversation's latest message. **A message is yours when it sits in Sent Items, or was sent from an address you send from or from your Cockpit alias**, wherever it is filed, so the alias's copy of a mail you sent, delivered to your Inbox, is yours too; an address you send from is one seen on a message in Sent Items. **Yours makes the Item Waiting for reply only when someone else is on the conversation**: a sender, To or Cc recipient who is neither you nor the alias. With nobody else on it, such as a mail to the alias alone, the Item comes in or stays To do. A conversation's first read counts every message in it; a later message counts its own sender, To and Cc. Bcc'ing the alias leaves it off the recipients, so the mail waits. With no alias set, the alias counts as someone else, so a mail to it alone waits. **Connect asks for your Cockpit alias under the mark question**, *Your Cockpit alias*, optional, and *Change…* offers it again to change or clear; one that is not an email address is refused beside the field and nothing is sent. The card then names it, *Alias cockpit@example.com*. It counts from the next check, and an Item already in changes only at its conversation's next message. Gmail asks none. **Done completes the flag on every flagged message of the conversation**, a long one continuing at the next check; one reopened in Cockpit while no message in it is flagged stops following, as for Gmail. An Item made of one flagged message before conversations were followed stays as it was and follows nothing. Its card carries the chip *Flagged for follow-up*, and *Alias …* where one is set, or says why it is failing. **An Outlook or Teams card is named by the address it signed in with**, as a Gmail card is, so two mailboxes under one person's name read apart; the person's name stands only where the sign-in gives no address, and a connection made before this keeps the name it had until it is connected again. A message marked later comes in within about five minutes, or has its Item reopened. Completing or clearing the flag, taking the category off, or moving the message to Deleted Items or deleting it marks the Item done at the next check; archiving it, moving it elsewhere or a reply to it does nothing. Unlike Gmail's label, a mailbox with no category called `Cockpit` does not fail, since Outlook accepts the category on a message either way. **The other way, within seconds:** marking an Outlook Item done or dismissing it completes the flag or takes the category off its message, and reopening it or undoing the dismiss flags it again or puts the category back — that mark alone, nothing else in the mailbox — and a change made in Cockpit and not yet in Outlook wins, as for Gmail. *Change…*, *Reconnect* and the same mailbox in two Workspaces behave as for Gmail. **An Outlook connection can file what it brings in on the Panel named after its category**, skipping the Inbox: Connect asks it under the mark question, and *Change…* offers it again, as a tick box, *File it on the panel named after its category*, unticked to start (a *Reconnect* starts at the row's own value), and the card then carries the chips *Flagged for follow-up* and *Files by category*. Only a message the connection already follows is filed — a category alone brings nothing in — and the followed `Cockpit` category never names a Panel. A category names the one Panel of Items in the Workspace with that name, ignoring case, accents (`prive` names *Privé*), runs of spaces and `&` against `and`, or as *dashboard-panel*: `Cubico-Tasks` names *Tasks* on the Dashboard *Cubico*, with or without spaces around the hyphen, and only the Dashboard a Panel lives on counts, not one that shows it; one matching no Panel, Panels on two Dashboards, or more than one Dashboard–Panel pair, files nothing, and the check's history line says so, and a Filter, a Panel of text and a Section are never matched. Each category that names a Panel files the message there, so two file it on both; with none, it stays in the Inbox, and Cockpit never makes a Panel. Ticking the box counts from that moment: a category already on mail that is in files nothing. A category added later files the Item at the next check, beside wherever it is filed already; one taken off never unfiles it, and filing or moving the Item in Cockpit never changes the message. Filing by category is not learned from for proposals, and *Never propose* on a Panel does not stop it. **A Gmail connection files the same way, by label**: the box reads *File it on the panel named after its label*, and the card the chips *Labelled Cockpit* and *Files by label*. An Item is a conversation, by label or by star, so a label on any of its messages counts; an Item a star made of one email before conversations were followed counts that email's own labels alone. Accents are ignored as for categories, but a label is not read as *dashboard-panel*. Only labels you made name a Panel: Gmail's own (Inbox, Unread, Important, Starred, Sent, Draft, the inbox tabs) never do, nor the `Cockpit` label a connection follows the label by; one following the star treats `Cockpit` as any label. A nested label names a Panel only by its full name, `Clients/Atlas`. A label put on a message that has not the mark, such as a reply, is seen the next time the conversation is read for any other reason. **A Gmail or Outlook connection can give every Item it brings in a due date**, so a message you flagged, starred or labelled for Cockpit and never answered turns amber and then red like any other deadline. Connect asks it under the questions above, and *Change…* offers it again, as a tick box, *Give it a due date*, unticked to start (a *Reconnect* starts at the row's own value); ticked, it asks for a number of days from 1 to 365, starting at 3, *days after it arrives*, and a number outside that range, or none, is refused beside the field and nothing is sent. The card then carries the chip *Due 3 days after* beside the mark it follows. **The date is that many calendar days after the day the message arrives in Cockpit** — not the day it was sent, so a conversation labelled months ago is not overdue on arrival — counted on the calendar of your latest capture that had a time zone, and on UTC where none had. **It fills an empty date and nothing else**: a date the message names, which Cockpit reads as it does for any note, replaces it, and one you set or clear is never replaced; it is not listed in *Suggestions*, being the connection's rule rather than a reading. **It counts from the moment it is ticked**, as filing by name does: Items already in keep the date they have, or none, and changing the days leaves the dates given as they were — only the next message to arrive gets the new number. **A message whose mark goes back on reopens its Item with a fresh date**, counted from the day it reopened by the days the connection gives then, but only while the Item's date is still the one the connection gave it: one you set or cleared, or a message's own day that replaced it, stays, and so does the old date when the connection has been switched off. **An Outlook connection following the flag gives the flag's own day where the flag has one**, even where the connection gives no days: a message flagged *Tomorrow*, or on a picked day, makes its Item due that day, ahead of the connection's days, and a flag with no date gets the connection's days as before. With several flagged messages in a conversation, the earliest day counts. The day is the calendar day the flag names, as Outlook wrote it, with no conversion to your time zone. **If the flag's day changes in Outlook, the Item's date follows at the next check**, but only while the date is still the one the connection gave: one you set or cleared, or a message's own day that replaced it, stays. A flag whose date is removed leaves the Item's date as it is, and a flag put back after Done reopens the Item due on the flag's day (your own date, if you set one, stays). An Item brought in with no date that you never set or cleared takes a flag's day the first time a check sees one. A *Category `Cockpit`* connection ignores flag dates, and so does Gmail, whose star has no date. Teams and Text messages give none. Disconnecting forgets the sign-in and drops what was waiting; Microsoft offers no revoke for one application, so remove Cockpit at Microsoft to end the grant. **A Gmail or Outlook card's *History* button, its *History…* menu entry, and its failing reason, open its history** in place of the list, with *‹ Connections* back: each check newest first under its day, kept 30 days and as far back as its newest 500 lines, ending "Older days follow, back to" the date 30 days ago where older lines are kept than it shows, when it started and ended to the second and how long it took (*20:31:02 → 20:31:05 · Manual check · finished in 3 s · worked*, or *failed after 8 s* with the source's status and message; a line from before this shows its end time and *finished* alone), named as a kind of check: a *Scheduled check*, a *Follow-up check* to one that said there was more to fetch, or a *Manual check* (the *Check now* button's), and a failure's message, kept to 2 KB, and what it did: how many messages its complete listing returned, in the followed mark's own words — "2 flagged, nothing new", "1 with category `Cockpit`" — on scheduled and follow-up checks too, though Gmail lists in full only on connecting or after a week unchecked, so its other lines carry none, and none on a failed check, a listing that stopped between pages or a line written before this, and counts of Items brought in, closed, sent back to the source and refused by it for good, each named by its title now and opening the Item, except a dismissed one, which reads "(dismissed)" and does not open, and one no longer held, which reads "an Item no longer in Cockpit". Where a check read the categories (Gmail: labels) of a message new to it, or changed since the last report, its line also names that message and each such name with what it did — "Tasks → filed on Tasks (Privé)", or "Tasks: skipped" and why: no Panel by that name; Panels on 3 Dashboards; matches 2 Panels (a category read as more than one Dashboard–Panel pair); the followed `Cockpit` category (Outlook); already on it when filing was ticked; filing by category is off. It names the first 50 messages and counts the rest ("and 3 more"), and a message whose names are unchanged adds nothing. A check that did anything or read names keeps a line of its own, and so does a *Manual check*. **A Gmail or Outlook row's menu and the head of its history carry *Check now***, which makes that connection's check due at once: pressed while one is queued, that check takes the label; while one runs, nothing more is queued. Its line arrives in an open history when the check ends. Checks in a row that worked and did nothing fold into one line — "Checked 40 times, nothing new, 09:05–12:20", showing the newest check's count — and so do failures in a row with the same message. A day before today whose checks all worked folds into one line — "Checked 288 times · 3 brought something in · show them" — whose *show them* draws that day's lines; a day with a failure, today, a Teams connection's days, and the oldest day shown where older lines are kept than it shows, are drawn in full. **A phone is connected by confirming a code Cockpit texts to it**, in Settings › Connections › *Text messages*, which only an environment with Twilio configured offers: production. Type your mobile number and press *Send code*, then type the code back and press *Confirm*. The number is then listed under the Workspace you connected from, its row says which Cockpit number to text, and its menu offers *Disconnect* alone. Only Belgian mobile numbers (+32 4…) are accepted, written as you like (`0470 12 34 56`, `+32 470 12 34 56`) and kept as `+32470123456`, and an account may ask for five codes an hour. A number that is not accepted, a wrong or lapsed code, five codes already asked for, or Twilio failing is said under the field, with the number and code kept as typed. An account may connect several phones, and the guest connects none. **A text from a connected phone to the Cockpit number lands in the Inbox**, on *Any workspace* with the Workspace you connected from as where it was captured, and gets the same clean-up and title as any capture. Its words are the captured message; its row reads *Text message* with no sender, and its form *From Text message*. The same text delivered twice is one Item, and each delivery leaves a line on the phone's connection history, a text with no words a *Failed* one. A text from a number nobody connected, or from one since disconnected, is dropped silently, and so is any request Twilio did not sign; a phone connected in two accounts texts into the one it was connected to last. Cockpit never texts back.

Slack, Notion and the rest are not built.
