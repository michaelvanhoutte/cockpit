# Workspaces

Everything is built from a **container hierarchy**: **Workspace → Dashboard → Panel**.

**Workspace** — everything you want in front of you while you work in one context, and the privacy boundary. It controls **which source accounts are connected and visible**, so private email is invisible while in *Work*: scoping rather than filtering after the fact.

**What decides between a Workspace and a Dashboard is whether the whole context changes**, and the pair of cases is the definition: a contractor working for two customers wants a Workspace each — different accounts, different mail, nothing shared — while somebody at one company serving two customers wants one Workspace and a Dashboard per customer, because the context is the same and only the view of it changes. The obvious advice, *one for work, one for personal, one per customer*, gives the second of those the wrong answer, which is why the app says this in the question that makes one rather than leaving it to be inferred.

Each Workspace has a **colour** that follows it into three places only: the dashboard band, the accent (headings, buttons, focus rings, type labels, and the menus and windows opened over the page) and the dot in the logo. The page, the top bar and the agents' dock are the same neutral in every Workspace, so a Workspace's colour never washes the screen. The tab you are on is filled with the band and runs down into it. Colours are picked as a set from a fixed palette, on the form the Workspace's own tab opens (open decision #13); the palette and its contrast rules are in `docs/design-system.md`. A Workspace storing a set the palette does not have — every one stored before the page went neutral, or a tint it never had — is drawn in the theme its tint belongs to rather than in what it stores, and nothing stored is rewritten.

**A Workspace is made from the `+` at the end of the tabs**, which opens the question a Dashboard and a Panel are named in and says what a Workspace is. **Everything else is on the tab itself, and on the header's own button for the tab you are on**: the menu holds *Edit…* and *Delete*, and there is one entry for changing a Workspace rather than a *Rename* beside it, the form being what renames. **The tab opens its own menu three ways, one per input**: a right-click, a press on the tab you are already on (that press has no other job, and it is what a finger has), and the keyboard's menu key — and the Workspace you are on also carries the same menu as a visible **…** at the right of the header (see "One menu, however it opens" in `docs/product/across-the-app.md`). **The name and colour are changed together, on a form over the page**; nothing is sent until *Save*, so a colour is chosen and looked at rather than applied on the press, and *Cancel*, *Escape* and a press outside all discard both halves. A name is required, stored trimmed, one line of at most 60 characters, and unique among live Workspaces whatever the capitalization. **Deleting a Workspace keeps everything that was in it**: its Items stay filed against it, because the router learns from the decisions filed there (see [routing-learning.md](routing-learning.md)). The name becomes available again. The last Workspace can be deleted, and the app then opens on the screen that makes one.

**The Workspaces are in the order you put them in, left to right.** A new Workspace goes last, after every Workspace the account has ever had; deleting one closes the gap. A tab is moved by dragging it along the strip — the pointer's alone, absent on a touchscreen and unreachable from a keyboard. **The tabs move as the drag does**, so the strip under the hand is the order dropping would keep. A move shows itself before the server agrees, so a second move can follow a first and a dropped tab does not snap back for a round trip. If it is refused, because a Workspace was made or deleted in another tab, the strip goes back.

**Ask up front only what has to be decided up front, and teach the rest at first use.** An account arrives holding one Workspace called *Workspace 1*, one Dashboard and one Panel, and opens on a single question: what that Workspace is. A Workspace is the one thing somebody can answer on the day they arrive — it is the privacy boundary, there are few of them, and *work*, *personal* or a customer's name is already true. A Dashboard and a Panel are not: nobody knows when they want a second Dashboard until they have used the first for a while, so asking at sign-in would demand a decision in the one moment it cannot be made. The question **renames** rather than makes, so *Skip* and an empty box are the same answer as any other and leave an app that works.

**What the question is asked of is the account's own rows, and having been through it is the browser's.** One Workspace still wearing the name it arrived with is what "nobody has started on this" looks like — nothing is stored to know it, so an account restored from a backup is exactly as far along as its rows say — and the browser remembering the question was asked is what stops it being asked twice. A browser that refuses storage asks again, and naming the Workspace is what ends it. Only the root address opens the question; a link to a Dashboard is never diverted into it.

The full path to any box:

```
Workspace: "Work"
├── Dashboard: "Today"       ← landing dashboard
│   ├── Panel: "Focus Today"
│   ├── Panel: "Project Falcon"
│   └── Panel: "People to talk to"
├── Dashboard: "Dormant projects"
│   └── Panel: "On hold"
└── Dashboard: "Research"
    └── Panel: "To read"
```

The landing dashboard is the desk landing: a bare `/` opens the first Workspace's dashboard at a desk, and the Capture page on a phone (see `docs/product/inbox.md`).

A Workspace connects its sources in Settings › Connections: a Microsoft Teams account, whose saved messages land in its Inbox, and Gmail accounts. Every conversation labelled `Cockpit` in a connected mailbox, whatever its age, arrives in the Workspace's Inbox within a minute of connecting, one Item per conversation ever: a Task (or, where the account no longer has its Task type, what any other capture nobody chose a Type for takes), titled with the subject — "(no subject)" where there is none — described with the plain text of the most recent labelled message, from "Gmail · sender", opening the conversation in Gmail; the usual clean-up then runs. Each mailbox is checked about every five minutes, and its row reads "last checked …", or "Failing:" and why — no label called `Cockpit` in the mailbox (create it in Gmail), or Google no longer accepts the sign-in (connect again); connecting the same mailbox again checks it from the start, finding what is already in rather than bringing it in twice. The same mailbox connected to two Workspaces gives each its own Items. A conversation labelled afterwards, by hand or by a Gmail filter, arrives within about five minutes too, once; a reply in a conversation already in adds nothing. A mailbox that has not been checked for about a week is read from the start again, so nothing labelled meanwhile is missed. When the label comes off a conversation, or its mail is binned or deleted, its Item is marked done at the next check; labelled again, the same Item reopens, undismissed if it had been dismissed. Every night each mailbox is read from the start again, which corrects anything the checks between missed; a mailbox with no label called `Cockpit` marks nothing done. Marking an Item done in Cockpit does not yet take the label off in Gmail, so a done Item whose conversation is still labelled reopens at that nightly read ("Take the Cockpit label off in Gmail when its task is done in Cockpit", issue 728). Slack, Notion and the rest are not built; see "Sources to connect" in `docs/ideas.md`.
