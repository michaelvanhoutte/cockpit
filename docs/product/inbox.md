# Inbox

**The Inbox is every open Item filed on no Panel**, scoped to the current Workspace — not a Panel with rows of its own, but the absence of a filing. That is what makes filing an Item the thing that takes it out of the Inbox, and it is why an Item can be moved back there: moving an Item to the Inbox is taking it off every Panel.

**Where it sits: beside the Dashboards, not among them.** Everything else flows out of it — it is read while working on a Dashboard and dropped into while looking at something else — so it is not one more view to switch to. Where there is room, it is a column down the left of every screen inside a Workspace, about a fifth of the width with a floor and a ceiling so it stays readable at 1280px and does not swallow a very wide screen; it scrolls on its own and so does the Dashboard beside it. Every screen inside a Workspace has it, including the ones a form or Settings opens over. On a phone, where a fifth of the width is about ninety pixels, the Inbox is a tab pinned at the left of the bar opening a screen of its own — and it keeps that address at every width, so a link made on a phone still works on a desktop. **It can be collapsed.** A `«` at the left of its heading collapses the column into a chip at the left of the Dashboard bar reading its name, its count and `»`, and the Dashboard takes the whole width; pressing the chip, or `I` from anywhere but a field, a menu or a window, brings the column back at the width it had. A row held on the chip opens the Inbox so it can be dropped into it, and dropping on the chip itself does nothing. The choice is remembered in this browser for every Workspace, like the column's width, which can be dragged by hand. A phone has no column, so nothing changes there.

Its name and the number in it head the column from the Dashboard bar, in the leftmost slot of it, so the bar is a row of headings and the Inbox is the leftmost — which is what says the Inbox belongs to the Workspace rather than to a Dashboard. On a phone there is no column to head, and the screen the tab opens carries the name itself.

**Capture has one way in: the header's Capture tab, and `C`.** The Inbox has no note box of its own. At a desk both open Capture as a window over the screen you are on — a note of several lines, a row of Workspaces, and under the Capture button one line saying what was just captured, with Undo — and Escape puts you back where you were, the note already in the Inbox beside it. On a phone both open the Capture page, where capture is the primary use. `C` works anywhere inside Cockpit except while typing in a field, while a menu or window is open, or with Ctrl, Alt or ⌘ held; the tab's tooltip reads *Capture (C)*. A mouse click on the tab, the Inbox's `«` or chip, or Go to panel's `»` or strip shows a toast at the bottom centre naming the key (*Tip: press C to capture from anywhere*; likewise `I` and `G` (*Tip: press G to go to a Panel*)), gone after 5 seconds or on ✕, and held back while an undo is offered in the same place; a key press, a keyboard activation and a phone show none, and nothing is stored. **Capturing never waits on the connection**: the note is kept on this device the moment Capture is pressed, shows in the Inbox beside the form at once as it was typed (in every Workspace's Inbox under *Any workspace*, and without a connection too), and is sent once a connection gets through; a line under the form reads *1 waiting to send* until it lands, and the tab counts what has not — see `docs/product/offline.md`. Cockpit's clean-up of the note arrives with the next read, and a spoken note or one filed on Panels shows once it has landed.

**At a desk, J / K and ↓ / ↑ move the focus through the Inbox a row at a time, and the docked Item form follows it.** A click on a row puts the keyboard on it and picks it, and the pick moves with the focus (see Selection in `docs/product/across-the-app.md`). The form shows the focused Item, and opens docked on it when none is open, whichever presentation the account has chosen: docked for that opening only, the choice itself untouched, and its *Center* control still centers it. The ends stay put. The keys act only while the keyboard is on a row: in a field, with a menu or window open, or with Ctrl, Alt or ⌘ held, they do nothing (Shift extends a selection). Escape in a field of the docked form returns the keyboard to the Item's row with the form still open and what was typed written; Escape again closes it. A phone has no keys.

**At a desk, one key acts on the focused Inbox row and the focus moves on to the next row** (the last row's, to the one before it), the docked form following: **Enter** files it on its proposed Panel, **M** opens *Move to…* (the focus moves on once a Panel is chosen; Escape files nothing and leaves it on the row), **D** marks it Done, **X** dismisses it, and **Z** presses the undo bar's *Undo*, bringing back and focusing the Item acted on last. Each is the row menu's own command, so the undo bar offers the same way back. **Enter on a row with no proposal files nothing and says so** under the row, until the next key. **O opens the Item**, as Enter did here before; on a Panel's row Enter still opens it. The same keys act under the same conditions as J and K, on the one row picked as on any row, and not while two or more are picked, when Enter files the selection instead (see Selection in `docs/product/across-the-app.md`). Each key is shown where its action already lives: *Open O*, *Move to… M*, *Status ▸ Done D* and *Dismiss X* at the far end of the row menu's entries, and *Enter files it here* in the proposal chip's hover.

**After Capture the box empties and one line beside the Capture button says *Captured “…” → <Panel or Inbox>* with Undo**, until the next capture or the window closes ("Undo a capture back into the box, in place of the Just captured list", issue 1104). **Undo takes the Item back and puts the note, priority, due date, files and Panels back in the box**, to edit and capture again, and Where goes back to what it was, *Any workspace* included. Where the capture is decides how: still waiting on this device, it is deleted and nothing is sent; being sent, Undo waits for the answer and then dismisses the Item; already landed, it is dismissed, so it leaves the Inbox and every Panel, and is kept, as any dismissed Item is. With no connection to dismiss it, the line stays, says so, and Undo can be pressed again. The line and the draft are the window's memory alone: a reload or a closed window has neither, and an undone capture's entry in the decision history stays. **A note that repeats another says so above that line**: once the server pairs it, *Looks like “…” (3 days ago)* appears with **Open it**, which opens the other note's form, while Undo stays beside the *Captured* line. It goes with the next capture or the closed window, and is never shown for a capture still waiting to send or while offline, since detection runs on the server. There is no *Just captured* list, in the window or on the phone Write page; captures waiting or refused show as one line each only while there is one — *1 waiting to send*, and *1 not sent · Put back*, which fills the box with the refused note and its files, naming the server's reason under it. The Car view keeps its list of last notes. **Capture opened from a Panel's + is the exception: it closes once its one note is kept, so it has neither the line nor Undo** (see Panels in `docs/product/dashboards.md`).

**A *File on* line under the note files it on Panels as it is captured, skipping the Inbox** ("File a note on Panels from Capture, skipping the Inbox", issue 1101): a type-ahead over the Panels of Items of every Dashboard of the chosen Workspace, or of every Workspace under *Any workspace*, each naming its Workspace there. Enter adds the top match as a chip and several may be added; empty means the Inbox. Under *Any workspace* the first Panel picked settles the Workspace and the search narrows to it, and choosing another Workspace drops Panels of the one left. Filters and Panels of text are never offered. The note lands at the top of each Panel, waits offline with its Panels like any capture, and counts as the person's own filing: it is recorded in the decision history with no proposal, and no Panel is proposed for it afterwards. A Panel deleted by the time it lands is skipped, with none left it lands in the Inbox, and a capture is never refused for its filing. Offline, only Workspaces already read are searched, and the line says which are left out. The search is fetched when the line is first used. Its last option, **+ New panel “…”**, makes a Panel that does not exist yet and adds it as a chip ("Make a new Panel from Capture's File on", issue 1103): on the Dashboard you were last on in the Workspace, or on `Dashboard ▸ Name`, or on `Workspace ▸ Dashboard ▸ Name` under *Any workspace*, where Where then becomes that Workspace (`>` types the same as ▸). Under *Any workspace*, a plain name with no Dashboard to go on asks for the whole path, and a Dashboard or Workspace that matches none says so. The Panel is made when the option is chosen, so offline the option is shown unavailable and says it needs a connection; a refused name says why under the field and keeps the text. A Panel made this way stays if the capture is later undone, as one made from **Move to…** does.

**A file dropped or pasted anywhere on the form, or chosen with the **+ Add** of the *Files* line under the note, becomes an Attachment on the note** — queued as a chip on that line until Capture is pressed, then kept and sent with the note; see Attachment in the glossary for how one is added and drawn. At a desk the *Files* line says so — "or drop, or Ctrl V", ⌘V on a Mac; a phone's line stays "or drop".

**On Android, the installed app is in the share sheet for images, short clips, PDFs, text and links.** Sharing to it opens the phone Capture page on *Any workspace*, each shared file queued as an Attachment chip and shared text or a link in the note, for the person to add to and press Capture, as if they had put them there themselves; several shares made before Capture is opened land together on one note. What is shared is kept on the device, apart from captures waiting to send, and goes onto the note only once somebody is signed in, so a share made signed out or with an expired sign-in waits for the next sign-in; Sign out empties it. A file is checked as a dropped one is, so a kind Cockpit refuses is refused with the same message. A share that cannot be kept — storage refused, or about 100 MB already waiting — or that arrives before the installed app has updated to take shares, is lost and Capture says "Couldn't receive what you shared — update Cockpit and share again." once, until a capture is made or the next share arrives. iOS offers no way to share into a web app, and the sheet lists only installed apps, so a phone picks the option up when Android next refreshes the app (up to a day) or on reinstall.

**A strip inside the bottom edge of the note box sets a priority and a due date**, taking no row of its own: three flags (Low, Normal, High, the marks an Inbox row's flag wears, the level's name as the hover title), then **Due** with Today · Tmrw · +7d and a 📅 button opening the date picker. Every choice is one click and pressing the lit one again clears it, so there is no *None*. A day picked that is none of the three shows on the 📅 button with a ✕ beside it. **At a desk the strip is one row**, wrapping only where a picked day or the running time does not fit beside the rest. **On a phone it is two**: Today · Tmrw · +7d · 📅 on top with no *Due* label, and the flags at the left with Aa and the mic at the right below. A picked day shows there as day and month (*29 Oct*), with a two-digit year only when it is not this year (*29 Dec 27*), and the 📅 is hidden while one is picked; a locale whose short month still does not fit wraps the ✕ onto its own line. Both reset to none once a capture lands, while Where carries over; a refused capture keeps them with the note. The Item is made with both in the one capture. Every other front door leaves them out. Either left unset is read from the note when it says one ("urgent", "tomorrow"; [items.md](items.md)), and a note is filed where it says it goes ("for Home", "Day to day, Admin and money"). Every capture you make in the app - this form, the Car view, and the outbox that sends them - carries the time zone it was made in, so "tomorrow" is read on your calendar at the moment of capture, even for a note sent later from elsewhere.

**An Aa button at the right end of the same strip, left of the mic, writes the note formatted** — the editor and toolbar the Item's description uses, in the window and on the phone page alike, with the strip staying one row at a desk. Capture always opens plain, and the editor is downloaded only when Aa is first pressed, so a plain note never pays for it; the note stays formatted through captures until the window or page closes. Aa is lit while formatted. Switching keeps the text: the plain box shows the note's Markdown exactly as it was, unless something was done in the editor. Captures, files, priority, due date and dictation behave the same in both: Ctrl+Enter captures the Markdown, a pasted or dropped file queues as a chip and is never put inline, and dictated words land in whichever box is showing. The note box resizes at a desk when formatted, and on a phone keeps the plain box's height. If the editor cannot load, the note stays plain with its text and says so, offering the newer version as the Item form does.

**A mic at the right end of the same strip dictates into the note**, the size of a flag, on the window and on the phone page alike and drawn only where the browser can record audio and Cockpit can transcribe it. Tapping the mic records until it is tapped again, or for five minutes at most; once the recorder has started it is filled and pulsing, with a running time (*Listening · 0:12*) beside it at a desk, and on a line under the strip on a phone so the buttons never move. On the second tap *Transcribing…* shows until Whisper's words land at the end of the note after a space, still editable; nothing is kept until Capture, and anything typed meanwhile stays, the words going after it. A recording that stopped at the five-minute limit says so. Whisper hears whether it is English or Dutch, so there is no language to choose. The recording goes to Cockpit's Worker and on to Cloudflare's Workers AI, is held in memory for the call and is never stored; it needs a connection. Capture pressed while recording or transcribing waits for the words and captures them. A blocked or missing microphone, or a transcription that did not come back, is said in one line under the strip. **A recording whose words did not come back is never lost**: it waits on the device under the strip (*A recording of 0:12, not yet transcribed*), oldest first, with **Retry**, through a closed form or page, until its words are in the note, which deletes it; retried in two tabs, the words land in one. Where the device cannot keep it (a private window, storage refused) the line says to dictate it again. A page hidden mid-recording (the phone locked, another app opened) or a recorder that stops by itself ends the recording as a tap would, so what was said up to then goes the same way. Waiting recordings belong to whoever recorded them, as a waiting capture does (`offline.md`). An account is held to 20 recordings transcribed a minute, refused ones counted; past that the line says too many were sent in a minute and to try again shortly.

**A Write | Car switch in the phone Capture page's header opens the Car view**, for capturing by voice without looking: a card showing the time recorded, one large round button, a large status line, and under them a moon/sun button, a line about the screen and the last few notes captured from it. Tapping the button records, for five minutes at most (*Listening · 0:12* on the card); tapping it again, or reaching the limit, shows *Transcribing…* and then captures at once with no review — *Any workspace*, no priority, no due date — through the same outbox; the phone vibrates and the button reads ✓ *Captured* for a couple of seconds, or *Nothing heard* where Whisper heard no words, and captures nothing. Recording and transcribing are the mic's, so Whisper hears English or Dutch itself. With no connection, a transcription that does not come back, or the 20 a minute limit reached, it still captures at once: the recording waits in the outbox, listed among the last notes here, and counted in Write's *waiting to send* line, as waiting for its words, and once Cockpit is open and can reach the server it is transcribed and the Item appears with them ("Capture in the Car view without a connection, from the recording", issue 1045). One the server refuses outright shows as *not sent* there, and the **Put back** of Write's *not sent* line keeps the recording under Write's strip with Retry; so does a note spoken with no workspace to capture into, which says so. A blocked or missing microphone says why in large text and captures nothing. The screen is kept on while the view is shown and the page visible, and a line says so quietly where the browser cannot (it cannot unlock a phone already locked). The moon/sun button switches the view dark and back at any time, recording included, without touching the recording: dark covers the whole screen under the top bar (the Capture heading, the Write | Car switch, the page and its edge, and the empty band under the tabs, which takes the top bar's graphite), dimmed rather than inverted, and Write is never dark by it. The view is also dark whenever the whole app is (Settings' *Appearance*), and the moon/sun button is hidden then, since pressing it would change nothing; it returns with the view's own choice as it was once the app is light. It is one choice per device, light until first switched whatever the phone's own setting, and still switches where storage is unavailable, just unremembered; the top bar itself stays as it is. `/capture` always opens on Write, `/capture/car` on Car, and the installed app's icon menu has *Car capture* beside *Capture*; where the browser cannot record audio the view says so and the switch still goes back. The browser's own speech recognition is not used anywhere.

**Where starts on the Workspace you are in**, in the window and on the page, and on *Any workspace* only when Capture is reached from outside one — a typed `/capture`, the installed app's shortcut, or a share. A different Workspace chosen in the window holds until the window closes, so several notes for one customer cost one choice; reopening starts on the current Workspace again. A note left on *Any workspace* waits in every Workspace's Inbox until somebody says where it belongs; the Workspace it was captured *from* is recorded either way. **On a phone Cockpit opens on the Capture page**: a bare `/` — the installed app's start, a bookmark, the return from sign-in — opens it on a screen narrower than 768px, starting on *Any workspace*, and the first Workspace's dashboard anywhere wider. An account with no Workspaces still goes to the screen that makes one, and a link to a Dashboard, the Inbox or a Workspace goes where it says; deleting the Workspace you are on lands on one that is left, not on Capture. `/capture` is the page at every width, because a link and the installed app's shortcut open it; the window has no address of its own, like Settings.

Processing an Item today means one of:

- **File it on a Panel** — which is what takes it out of the Inbox, and the one thing that does. Every Item's own menu carries **Move to…**, opening a picker of the Panels in the Workspace, with the Dashboard you are on first, the five Panels most recently filed into above it, and the Inbox among the targets. A search box, focused on opening, narrows it by Panel or Dashboard name. **From an Inbox row with a proposal it opens on the proposed Panel's Dashboard, that Panel marked** (whether **M** or the row menu opened it), and **↓** from the search goes to the Panel after it, so a near miss is ↓ Enter; a row with no proposal, a proposal for a Panel set to **Never propose** or gone, and a Panel's row open as above. Each Dashboard is a heading pinned while its Panels scroll past, and the picker keeps its height while you type. **Nothing pointless is offered**: **Also show on…** leaves out every Panel the Item is on, and **Move to…** from a Panel's row leaves out every Panel the Item is on too (a move takes it off the Panel it is moved from and no other) and, for an Item already in it, the Inbox. **A move from a Panel's row takes the Item off that Panel only**, whether by the menu, the picker a swipe opens or **Move it here** on a drop; moving to the Inbox takes it off every Panel, and **Undo** puts it back on all it was on. **A selection moved from a Dashboard takes each Item off the Panels of that Dashboard**, and Panels on other Dashboards keep it; an Item shown there only by a Filter panel loses nothing and lands on the target as well, and one already on the target ends on it once. A selection in the Inbox, and any selection moved to the Inbox, takes each Item off every Panel. **A selection can also be dragged** (Selection, in `docs/product/across-the-app.md`): dropped on a Panel it files each picked Item as **Move to…** does, together at the landing line and without the move-or-add question for rows from the Inbox, and dropped on the Inbox it takes each off every Panel. Dropped from one Panel onto another it is already on, the question is still asked and **Move it here** takes it off the Panel it came from and puts it at the drop position, the question naming the Panel it leaves. **A Panel that does not exist yet can be made from it**: every Dashboard's heading carries a **+**, which opens a name field under it with **Add & move** (**Add & show** for **Also show on…**), makes a Panel of Items as **+ Panel** does and files the Item, or every selected Item, into it in the same gesture. The picker closes at the click, with the Panel drawn on its Dashboard and the Item on it before the server has answered. Escape in the field closes the field and leaves the picker open; a Panel the server refuses (a name another live Panel holds, say) takes the filing back with it, the Item where it was, and the undo bar says why; a Panel made and then refused its filing stays. Offline nothing is made, and the field says so until the name is edited. No **+** on a Dashboard filtered in this browser, where **+ Panel** is locked.
  **Filing offers its next step on the undo bar** - one Item by the menu, a proposal chip, a swipe or a drop, and a moved selection alike, covering exactly the Items that moved: **Show** switches to the Dashboard holding the Panel, scrolls to it and highlights the moved Item's row (every moved row, for a selection) for a moment, clearing a Dashboard filter that hides it as Go to panel does (an Item moved off that Panel meanwhile leaves the Panel scrolled to and nothing highlighted); **Also show on…** opens the usual picker over where you are, leaving out every Panel the Item is on now, and adds it (every moved Item, for a selection), and you stay where you were (the bar then offers Undo of that add alone, which takes every one off again). A move to the Inbox, a reorder and an **Also show on…** offer Undo alone. **An Item that belonged to no Workspace gets Show and Also show on… and no Undo**, since the filing decided its Workspace and cannot be put back; a selection holding one gets the same. The bar shortens the Item's title and never the destination: *“Look into a che…” moved to Admin & money*.
- **Mark it done** — **Status ▸ Done** in its menu (an Inbox row has no **✓**), which takes it off every list, saying when; the Workspace's **All items** tab, filtered to *Done*, is where to find it again, and reopen it.
- **Delete/Dismiss** — which is reversible for as long as the bar offering it is on screen. A dismissed Item is kept rather than erased, and undismissing it brings it back, so putting it back is the same change made the other way.

Reading, replying, flagging and associating an Item are designed, not built — reading and replying need something to read (no connector exists yet), and associating needs a view onto the Associations that already exist in the schema: see "Sources to connect" and "Associations" in `docs/ideas.md`.

Filing it, finishing it or dismissing it takes an Item out of the Inbox; it stays reachable through the Panels holding it.

**Gestures:** **swipe left dismisses, swipe right opens the picker** — the same picker **Move to…** opens, so filing is one gesture on a phone and the same question either way. One meaning per direction, in every list rather than only the Inbox: the same swipe cannot mean two things depending on which list it is in, so removing an Item from one Panel stays in the menu. **A Filter panel's row has no Move to…, so swiping it right does nothing and uncovers no strip**; swiping it left still dismisses. A swipe that stops short puts the row back, and one that is mostly vertical is the list scrolling and does nothing at all. **A swipe is a touch gesture**: on a desktop the same actions are the menu.

**A row Cockpit has proposed a Panel for shows a chip reading `→ Day to day ▸ Admin & money`**: the Dashboard, then the Panel, always both, except under a group header (below). Clicking it, or alt-double-clicking the row, files the Item there. On a row too narrow for it the Panel name is cut with an ellipsis and the Dashboard stays whole; hovering it, or its accessible name, gives the full "Dashboard ▸ Panel" and then the reason. **A proposal is *clear* or *closest***: Cockpit proposes the closest Panel wherever one plausibly fits, and none only for a note that fits nothing. A *closest* chip is drawn fainter, a dashed outline in place of the fill, its reason reading *Closest match: …*, and is filed exactly as a *clear* one; a proposal from before Cockpit said which reads as *clear*. Each filing records which kind it accepted or overrode. No chip is drawn where the Panel or its Dashboard no longer exists, or where the Panel is set to **Never propose** (see "Panels" in `docs/product/dashboards.md`).

**Rows proposed for the same Panel are grouped, wherever two or more share one**, at a desk and on a phone, under a header `→ Day to day ▸ Admin & money · 4 (1 closest)`, the count of closest ones shown only when there are any. The groups follow the order of the Dashboards and then of their Panels, whatever their counts; every other row follows them in the Inbox's own order, headerless: a proposal no other Item shares, none, a Panel set to **Never propose**, or one deleted since. Clear and closest proposals for one Panel share a group. On a narrow column the header cuts the Panel name, never the count. **Clicking the header's name selects exactly the group's rows**, replacing any selection held. Inside a group a row's chip reads `File` (dashed when closest) and files that row on the group's Panel. **The header's *File all* files every Item of the group on its Panel, closest included, with one Undo**; it is the same filing as Enter on a selection of the group, and pressed again while it is still filing sends nothing more. **At a desk, Shift+Enter on a row does the same for the row's group, or files the row alone outside a group**, the focus moving to the first row after the group (the row before it when none follows, as for the other keys); on a row with no proposal it files nothing and says *No Panel was proposed* under the row. It does nothing with a selection held, with Ctrl, Alt or ⌘ also held, or in a field. **Filing by proposal offers the full undo bar whenever everything that moved went to one Panel** (File all, Shift+Enter, or Enter on a selection): *Show*, *Also show on…* for every moved Item, and *Undo*; a selection proposed for several Panels offers Undo alone, and one holding an Item that belonged to no Workspace gets Show and Also show on… without Undo.

**When the Inbox's rows rearrange, you keep your place**: proposals arrive seconds after a capture and filing re-proposes the rest, so groups form, merge and dissolve while you look, and the list stays live while they do. Every row that was already there slides from its old place to its new one over about a quarter of a second, and a row arriving or leaving moves nothing else. The row the keyboard is on stays where it was on screen through any change to the list, a row arriving or leaving included. The focused row, every selected row and the row under the pointer flash where they land for about two seconds after a reorder, and nothing flashes otherwise. **A click, double-click or press on a row during the slide does nothing**; one after it acts as usual. With reduced motion asked for, rows jump rather than slide. A regroup that lands before the code for this has loaded, a moment after the first paint, simply jumps.

**The row names the action it would take, in the strip it uncovers**, from the first pixel it moves rather than from the threshold — which direction means what is the part a thumb cannot see, and by the time the threshold is reached the direction has already been chosen. A mark says it as soon as there is room for one and the word joins it once the strip is wide enough to hold the whole of it, a word half off the edge being worse than none; the words are the menu's own, **Move to…** and **Dismiss**, so the two ways to an action are not two things to learn. Past the threshold the strip fills with colour to say that letting go now takes it. The row **cannot name an action the release would not take**: what it says and what it does are one answer, not two.

**Dismissing a Gmail Item takes its conversation's `Cockpit` label off, and nothing more** — no archive, no delete (`docs/product/workspaces.md`); one that came in by star has its star taken off instead, and one that came in under the mark its connection no longer follows changes nothing in Gmail. **Dismissing an Outlook Item completes the flag of its message, or takes its `Cockpit` category off, and nothing more** (`docs/product/workspaces.md`). **Open question for every other source:** does "delete" mean delete only here, or also archive or delete at the source? See "One-way vs two-way sync" in `docs/functional-definition.md` — the single biggest behavioral decision.

## How an Item reaches a box — must it pass through the Inbox first? (settled for mail)

**For Outlook mail, by the source's own name.** A connection set to file by category puts a message it follows straight on the Panel its category names, skipping the Inbox, and carries no "unseen" mark: categorising it was deliberate, the active capture below. Anything matching no Panel lands in the Inbox as before. The rules are under Connections in `docs/product/workspaces.md`. Other sources are still open, as below.

The reframing that makes this tractable: **"in the Inbox" and "shown in a box" are two independent states, not two ends of one pipeline.** Associations are many-to-many and being finished with an Item is separate (see "Item + Association model" in `docs/product/items.md`), so an Item can be in either, both or neither, and "passes through the inbox first" is a per-source default rather than an architecture.

**Model A — Inbox-first (manual triage).** Everything lands in the Inbox; nothing appears in a box until deliberately filed there. *Pro:* one point of control. *Con:* obvious items still need manual routing.

**Model B — Direct routing by rule.** Rules send certain items straight to a box: "Slack messages in #customer-1 → Customer 1's board", "@mentions on a project → that project's box", "anything I star → its box". *Pro:* far less busywork. *Con:* items appear without a review step, so they need an "unseen" dot or a per-rule "also show in Inbox" toggle.

**Inside Model B, passive vs active capture:** being tagged or @mentioned is *passive* — route it **and** flag as unseen; deliberately saving a message is *active*, a strong intentional signal that should skip the Inbox entirely.

**Recommended lean (a hybrid).** Inbox by default for anything with no matching rule, plus per-source/per-channel routing rules so obvious and actively-saved items land directly in a box, optionally marked "unseen". Still open for every source but mail.

## Swipe-right — settled

**Swipe-right opens the picker**, the same one **Move to…** opens. Filing is what an Inbox row most often needs and the picker is where the decision already lives, so a phone gets to it in one gesture rather than three taps.

**One meaning per direction, at one distance.** The other candidates — a quick process sheet, a wake date, done — were going to be told apart by how far the swipe went, and that is what the gesture cannot spare: it is already competing with the list scrolling under the same thumb, so the difference between a short swipe and a long one is not a difference a hand can be relied on to make. They stay in the row's menu, where there is no threshold to miss.
