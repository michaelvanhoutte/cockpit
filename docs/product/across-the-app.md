# Across the app

**The questions that make something say what they make, and the ones that rename say only the name.** A word is worth explaining at the moment somebody is asking for it — which is when they reach for the control that makes one — and never before: nobody can decide when they want a second Dashboard until they have used the first for a while, so a screen at sign-in would demand the decision in the one moment it cannot be made. A rename is about something already in front of you and explains nothing.

**One menu, however it opens.** Every menu in the app — a row's, a tab's, a Panel's — offers the same shape: same entries, same look, and an entry that cannot be chosen right now stays visible and says why, rather than disappearing; one that never applies to that row, such as **Copy link** on an Inbox row, is left out. A row opens its by the same control every time it appears: a vertical triplet of dots, one size, one hover and focus treatment, drawn rather than typed because `···` is punctuation whose size and baseline belong to the font. Three dots mean a menu, never a link that navigates, everywhere but one. Under the pointer, an Item row also shows a **✓** just left of the dots: **Mark done**, one click, the same change as **Status ▸ Done** with the same Undo. It is not drawn on touch, while two or more rows are picked, or on an Inbox row, which is for deciding where an Item belongs; the menu keeps **Status ▸ Done** as the keyboard route and the Inbox row's only one. A row's menu also opens on a right-click anywhere on the row, at the pointer, offering exactly the same entries and moving a single pick to that row — not on touch, which has the dots and spends a long press on picking the row out. **An Item row's menu is split by dividers into groups of one kind**, in this order: *Open* (**Open**, **Open in …**, and **Copy link** on an Item that is on a Panel, never one in the Inbox), filing (**Move to this workspace**, **Move to…**, **Also show on…**, **Remove from this panel**), Claude (**Send to Claude…**, and **Open the Claude session ↗** while one is open), **Wait for reply…** (**Wait again…** on a waiting Item) and **Status**, **Not a duplicate** and **Dismiss as duplicate** where the row is flagged as a possible duplicate, and **Dismiss**, last and red. A group with nothing to offer on that row leaves its divider out. **Status** is one row naming where the Item stands, opening a submenu of To do, In progress, Waiting for reply and Done with the current one ticked; choosing the one already ticked changes nothing, and Waiting for reply asks how long to wait, as **Wait for reply…** does. A tab opens its own instead — right-click, a long press, or the keyboard's own menu key — because the tab itself is already the trigger, and a button beside every tab in a strip used all day would be chrome nobody needed. **The tab you are on is the exception**: the open Workspace's own actions are also a visible **…** at the right of the header, and the open Dashboard's at the right of its bar beside **+ Panel** — the same entries its tab's own menu offers, which on the open Dashboard include **Collapse panels** (see `docs/product/dashboards.md`); the Workspace's menu holds **Edit…**, a divider, **Show All items tab** or **Hide All items tab** for its *All items* tab, **Recurring items**, a divider and **Delete**, and that tab's own menu and **…** offer **Hide All items tab** alone, since a menu reachable only by gesture and invisible until found left an account's or a Dashboard's own actions unfound. A Panel opens the same three ways *and* carries that same vertical-triplet button on its header, since the header doubles as the panel's drag handle and a right-click or the menu key on it was easy to miss. **The header's profile is the one place the dots give way**: a circle carrying your initial opens who you are, *Settings…* and, for an admin, *Platform settings…* (the second not on a phone), *Keyboard shortcuts* with a `?` key cap (not on a phone either, which has no keys) and the way out, a glyph of its own since a second triplet beside the Workspace's **…** said nothing about which was which. **Below the way out, past a divider, the menu ends with the credit** — "© 2026 Conselit · conselit.be", the years running to the current one — which opens www.conselit.be in a new tab; the logon page carries the same line, faint, under its card. There is no footer and no About window: nobody would open one.

**Deleting anything asks first, in a dialog, and the row or tab it was asked from does not change.** The question names what is going and what goes with it — for a Workspace, how many Items stop being visible, finished ones included — and offers Cancel and Delete in one fixed order everywhere, so the control under the pointer never changes meaning between the press that asks and the press that answers. Escape and Cancel are the two ways to say no; pressing outside is deliberately not one. Focus returns to where it was asked from, and where that has gone with the thing deleted, to the tab you are now on. Nothing is sent until Delete is pressed, and a Workspace's Delete waits for the count it is asking about, though a count that could not be read at all lets it through rather than trapping you. The question closes at the click, and a refusal is said in the undo bar. **A Section is the one exception: its Delete takes effect without asking**, since only a title is lost (see "Sections: a title across the board" in `docs/product/dashboards.md`). Editing is not destructive and asks nothing, but shares the form's shape: it also opens over the page and returns the focus to what it came from.

**Editing more than one field at a time happens in a form, and a form is a modal with a route of its own**, dimmed behind. **An Item's form is a page of its own below 768px, the width Capture becomes a page at**: at the same address, **opening to be read, with nothing focused so that no keyboard comes up over the text** — a header of **←** (which closes it), *Item*, **Copy link** and **Edit**; the title as text; status, priority and due date as one line (*In progress · High priority · Due …*, changed only through Edit); the description formatted with no toolbar or box, its links opening in a new tab; and *On panels* as text (*On Errands, Admin*, or *In the Inbox*). **Copy link** is absent while the Item is in the Inbox. **Edit, or a tap on the title or the description, switches to editing**, with the field tapped focused: a header of **←**, *Item*, **Copy link** and **Save**, where Save saves and returns to reading and ← (Cancel) discards and returns to reading. Editing has the title as a large line, the four tabs, one wrapping row of chips, the description taking the height that is left, and under *On panels* the files line described below, where the Item has files. **The chips are Status, Priority, Due and + Attach**, each saying what is set and opening a picker of its own: the three statuses with the current one ticked, the three levels and *No priority*, and for Due *Today*, *Tomorrow*, *+7d*, the phone's date picker and *Clear*. A field with nothing set reads its own name, *Priority* or *Due date*, in outline; a due date wears the row's pill. A choice is held until **Save** like every other field. **A desk, docked or modal, draws status, priority and due date as one line** in place of labelled columns: a Status chip, the three priority flags (pressing the lit one clears it) and the Due chips (*Today*, *Tmrw*, *+7d*, a date button that shows any other day with a ✕), as Capture's strip draws them. Each change keeps its form's timing. **Under the description, an *On panels* field lists every Panel the Item is filed on, each with a ✕, and adds more** by the type-ahead and **+ New panel** of Capture's *File on*, within the Item's Workspace: a Filter that shows the Item is not listed, and nothing already listed is offered. Taking it off its last Panel returns it to the Inbox. It keeps its form's timing too: docked, each change is written at once and offered back through the bar; in the modal and on the phone the changes wait for **Save** and are sent after the fields, one at a time, so a refusal partway leaves the earlier ones applied, keeps the form open and names the Panel it was refused for. **An Item's files have a tab of their own, and the Item tab keeps one line for them** — at a desk and on a phone alike, under *On panels*: the first four as small icons (a thumbnail for an image, a file icon otherwise), each opening its file, then ***N attachments →***, which opens the **Attachments** tab. With no files the line is not drawn; while a file uploads it says *Attaching…* and counts it. **+ Attach** is on the status line at a desk, and among the chips on a phone. The tab lists every file as its chip, with ✕, then **+ Add** and *or drop, or paste*, and carries the count beside its name (*Attachments 2*) when there are files. A file dropped or pasted anywhere on the form outside the description text is attached whichever tab is open, and a refusal is said under the Item tab's line and under the tab's list. The page has no Dock, Cancel or bottom Save, even for an account that docks the form, and the description's toolbar is one row that scrolls sideways with *Source* in it. While you type in the description the header stays on screen. The URL says what is open, so the back button closes it and a link opens it — which is what a question does not need and does not get: a question is a small centred dialog with nothing to link to. Nothing is written until **Save**, which sends only the fields that actually changed, so leaving one untouched cannot overwrite an edit made elsewhere. Cancel, Escape and pressing outside all close the form and discard what was typed, without asking, because Cancel means cancel (on the phone page, ← while editing discards and returns to reading). Deliberately unlike the deleting rule above: a delete asks because there is one way to say yes and no way back, while a form has Save sitting in it, unpressed. **An Item's form docked to the side is the exception**, since it is meant to stay open with no session boundary for Save to be: it has no Save or Cancel, only **Close**, in its top row after Copy link and Center with nothing at its foot, and each field is written as it is finished — title and description on leaving the box, status, priority and due date the moment one is picked. Every such write can be undone right after, through the bar the other undoable changes use, and closing by any route keeps what is still in a box. **Copy link** is a text button in an Item's form header, directly left of Dock or Center, and on the page directly left of Edit or Save; it is absent while the Item is in the Inbox. It and the menu's entry copy the same link, to the Item in the workspace it is filed in rather than the view it was copied from, as a plain address and as the Item's title linked to it, so Teams, Outlook, Notion and Google Docs paste the title; the bar says *Link copied*, with no Undo, or *Couldn't copy the link* where the browser refuses. It has no keyboard shortcut. **The route is for a form opened from content** — an Item, which somebody may want to link to. A form opened from a tab is a detour from what you are already looking at, so it has no address of its own; everything else about it is the same.

**Ordering** puts rows in the order you choose by dragging one, wherever a list can be reordered — a tab strip included, which is how the Workspaces and a Workspace's Dashboards are moved. A Panel of items sorted by its fields is the exception: its rows go where the sort puts them and are not dragged to a new place, while the order you set is kept for when it goes back to Manual (see `docs/product/dashboards.md`).

**Live updates** carry a change made in one place to everywhere else it is shown, without a reload.

**Selection** picks rows out of the Inbox, or out of the Panels of the open Dashboard, to act on them, from anywhere on a row rather than a control of its own. **A plain click picks that row alone**, replacing any selection (and ending the other scope's), as a file manager does: the row is highlighted and nothing opens. From there a ctrl/cmd-click adds or removes one row at a time, and a shift-click reaches a span; a finger has a long press to pick the first row and a tap in place of ctrl/cmd, since it has no such key, and no span. **One picked row is only highlighted, however it was picked**: the bar appears from two. **Escape, a click on empty space, switching Dashboard or Workspace, or the Item leaving ends a single pick.** **One selection is held at a time, and it is the Inbox's or the open Dashboard's.** A Dashboard's spans every Panel it shows: picking on a second Panel adds to what the first holds, an Item shown on two Panels is ticked on both and counted once, and a shift-click reaches across the Panel it is in and no further. Starting one in the Inbox ends the Dashboard's, and the reverse; switching Dashboard or Workspace ends it, except the switch its own drag makes (below). A row no list of the selection's scope shows any more — hidden by a Dashboard filter, filed off the Dashboard, finished — leaves it, and is not picked again if it comes back. **Select all** is the first entry of a Panel of items' or a Filter panel's menu, adding every row it shows, and **Select all items** is on the open Dashboard's menu, adding every row its Panels show; neither reaches a row a Dashboard filter hides, and a Panel of text offers neither. The Inbox's selection has its bar at the foot of the Inbox's list; a Dashboard's has one bar for all its Panels, a card at the foot of the Dashboard's column that stays in view as it scrolls and is never over the Inbox or the Go to panel column, and on a phone either is pinned to the foot of the screen. **One picked row suspends nothing**: its menu, right-click, swipe, ✓ and the Inbox keys act on it as on any row. **Two or more suspend every row's own menu, ✓, swipe and keys**, so acting on one row cannot happen while several are picked — except a routing suggestion's own chip, which is a carve-out. **At a desk, dragging a picked row carries the whole selection**, from the Inbox or the Dashboard it is held on: the picked rows fade where they stand, the row under the pointer says how many (*3 items*), and dropped they are filed as **Move to…** files them, landing together in the order the list showed them (see Filing in `docs/product/dashboards.md`). A Dashboard's selection survives the switch its own drag makes: rested on another Dashboard's tab the drag switches to it as for one row, and the picked rows can be dropped on a Panel there, with the same question, filing and undo bar; **Move** takes each Item off the Panels of the Dashboard it was picked on. A drag cancelled after the switch ends the selection, since you are now on another Dashboard. A row that is not picked ends the selection (a single pick too) and is dragged alone; a phone has no row drag. At a desk, in the Inbox, the picked row and the focused row are one: a plain **J / K** moves the pick alone to the next or previous row, going back to one row from several, **Shift+J / K** (or Shift+↓ / ↑) extends the selection from the focused row and the opposite key shrinks it, and **Enter** files each picked Item on its own proposed Panel: those with none stay picked where they are, and the undo bar says how many moved and how many had none, or which was refused, its **Undo** putting every moved Item back in the Inbox; where all of them went to one Panel the bar also offers **Show** and **Also show on…** for the moved Items (see Inbox in `docs/product/inbox.md`). A plain click opens nothing on its own — while a form is docked open it also shows that row in the dock, marked as the one shown (a ctrl/cmd-click there picks without changing the dock); a double-click, Enter on a row that has keyboard focus (Find lands on one), or **Open** from the row's own menu, is what opens a row, and the menu key or Shift+F10 on a focused row opens its menu as a right-click does — and on an Inbox row Cockpit has proposed a Panel for, an alt-double-click files it there instead, as its chip does (alt, because ctrl/cmd and shift already pick a row out). Either bar offers **Move to…**, **Edit ▾** and **Clear**. **Edit** opens **Priority ▸**, **Due ▸** and **Status ▸**, each naming the value every picked Item shares, or *Mixed*, with a submenu ticking the shared value: High, Normal, Low and None; Today, Tomorrow, In a week, No due date and **Pick a date…**; To do, In progress and Done. A choice sets that field on every picked Item that does not hold it, is undone in one go by putting back each Item's own previous value, and keeps the selection held for a second field; an Item set to Done leaves its Panels and so the selection. What is picked is acted on one row at a time, and the bar counts the run (*Saving 12 of 50…*) with **Move to…** and **Edit** unavailable until it ends. **Filing stops at the first refusal**, leaving what did not move still picked; **an edit carries on past a refused Item**, which stays picked, and the bar says how many were not changed and why. An Undo refused part way says how far it got.

**Keyboard shortcuts** lists every key Cockpit handles, in a window opened by `?` or by *Keyboard shortcuts* on the profile menu, at a desk only. **It is grouped by where the key works** — *Anywhere*, *On an Inbox row*, *Picking several Inbox rows*, *Docked Item form*, *On a Panel's row* and *Capture* — with a line under the list saying none of them act while typing in a field; the description editor's formatting keys are left out, its toolbar already showing them. `?` has the guards `I` has (not while typing, not under a menu or window, not with Ctrl, Alt or ⌘, not held down), Shift being how most layouts type it, so the window already open is never opened a second time. **Esc, ✕ and a press outside close it**, and the keyboard goes back to where it was. **The docked Item form is not a window to a key**: `C`, `I`, `G` and `F` work while one is docked, from the page or from a button in the form; a field in it, a modal form, a menu or a dialog still holds them back.

**Updating** picks up a new version of Cockpit. A tab running one older than the server can read nothing it is told, so it stops, fetches the newer one and comes back where you were, rather than asking. Your sign-in and your work are untouched; the version is the only thing that was wrong. Where there is no newer one to fetch, it says so instead of trying again. **A new version found any other way is one message, however many deploys have landed**, and its *Continue* loads the latest once it has downloaded; a plain reload afterwards always opens Cockpit, and a click that cannot load the latest says *Could not load the new version. Try again.* and leaves the current version working. **A tab can also be behind without being told so**: Cockpit is fetched in parts, and a deploy while a tab is open takes away the parts it has not fetched yet — so a description whose formatting never arrives offers the new version rather than taking it, the description being editable and saveable in the meantime and a reload being able to throw away what you are part-way through.

Binding on anything written to be read as a description of the product, test names included (see "Tests are named in the product's language" in `docs/testing-strategy.md`).

**Appearance** is dark or light for the whole app, the logon page included. A choice stored on this device, Light or Dark, decides it; with none, which is *Match device*, the device's own setting does, and a change to either while Cockpit is open is followed without a reload, a choice made in another tab included. **It is chosen in Settings under *Appearance*** — *Match device*, *Light* or *Dark*, the current one selected — which applies at once and is remembered on this device, *Match device* clearing the stored choice; where storage is unavailable it still applies and is simply not remembered. The phone offers it too. An app opened dark paints dark from its first frame, never light first. Each Workspace keeps its band and dot, and its accent is lifted where it is text or a selected fill. Values and the contrast they hold are in `docs/design-system.md`, under *The dark appearance*.

**Dates** are stored in UTC and shown in the viewer's own time zone and region. **The zone is the device's, read when the date is drawn**; **the region is the browser's**, the first of its languages that names one, with the words kept in English: *nl-BE* reads *22 Oct 2026, 15:05* and *en-US* *Oct 22, 2026, 3:05 PM*, and with no region among the languages it reads as plain English, as does a region the platform has no English for (Brazil, China). There is no setting. **A day with no time attached** (a due date, *Next on*, a picked day) is the same day in every zone, in the region's order. It covers the admin pages: Manage users' *Last signed in* is a day on the device's calendar, and the three months after which it flags someone inactive are counted on that calendar; Usage's window and its guest day rows are counted on that calendar too (a sign-in at 00:30 in Brussels counts on that day), the device's zone going with the read. Elapsed time (*3 days ago*) names no zone and is unchanged, and dates handed to agents or exported stay ISO UTC because a machine reads them. **Every date shown goes through one formatter** (`apps/web/src/dateFormat.ts`), which the lint layer holds: a component that formats a date itself fails `pnpm lint`, so a new screen cannot pick its own zone or locale.
