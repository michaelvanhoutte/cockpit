# Dashboards

**Dashboard** — a named view *inside* a Workspace, switched between like tabs. The bar under the workspace tabs is where they live: a `+` after them adds one, and each tab carries a menu of what can be done to it. **The Inbox is not a Dashboard**; it sits beside them (see "The Inbox and the triage flow" in `docs/product/inbox.md`). Every Workspace has at least one, and a Dashboard's name is unique within its Workspace, so two Workspaces may each have a *Research*.

**The Dashboards of a Workspace are in the order you put them in, left to right along their bar**, stored per Workspace and the same on every device. A new one goes last, after every Dashboard that Workspace has ever had; deleting one closes the gap. A tab is moved by dragging it along the bar, the gesture the Workspace tabs take and with the same preview and the same answer to a refusal (see "The Workspaces are in the order you put them in, left to right" in `docs/product/workspaces.md`). The Inbox is no Dashboard, so it neither moves nor gives up its place.

**A Dashboard is changed on its own tab**, exactly as a Workspace is and opened the same three ways: *Edit…* and *Delete*, on the tab that already names it — or, for the Dashboard you are on, from the **…** at the right of the bar beside **+ Panel**, offering the same entries (see "One menu, however it opens" in `docs/product/across-the-app.md`), and on the open Dashboard's menu alone, **Select all items** in a group of its own after Collapse panels, which picks every row its Panels show (Selection, in the same document). The form opens over the Workspace rather than replacing it, because renaming a Dashboard is a detour from working on one: the panels, the Inbox and the bar stay behind it, closing puts you back with nothing to reload, and there is no address for it. Adding is not on the tab — the `+` belongs on the bar the new tab will appear on. **What it opens is the same question a Panel is named in, and it says what a Dashboard is**: pressing `+` is the moment somebody is asking, and that is the moment worth answering in. The Inbox has no such menu, being no Dashboard of the Workspace. Renaming obeys the rules adding does, so *Research* may become *RESEARCH* while a name another Dashboard of that Workspace holds is refused with a message saying which. Deleting asks first in the same dialog as everywhere else and says what the Dashboard takes with it: how many Panels go with it, or that there is nothing on it. **A Workspace's last Dashboard cannot be deleted**, the one place the app refuses to delete something: the last Workspace may go because the app can offer to make one, while a Workspace with no Dashboard has no view at all. The entry is in the menu, unavailable, with that sentence on it, rather than offered and then refused. Deleting the one you were looking at moves the Workspace on behind it, its own address deciding where you land.

**All items** is a tab after the Dashboards and before the `+`, listing every Item of the Workspace in one table. **Show All items tab** on the Workspace's menu — on its tab, or the header's **…** — switches it on, and **Hide All items tab** on that menu, or on the tab's own, switches it off; no Dashboard's menu offers it. Hiding it while you are on it returns you to the Workspace's first Dashboard. It is on or off per Workspace, remembered in this browser and forgotten at sign-out, and on a phone as on a desk. **It has an address of its own under the Workspace**, so Back, a reload and a copied link land on it, and opening that address switches the tab on if it was off; the tab is the one marked, not a Dashboard. The table holds finished Items as well as open ones and no dismissed one, with the columns *Title*, *Type*, *Status*, *Priority*, *Due*, *Where it is* — "Dashboard ▸ Panel" for each Panel the Item is filed on, *Inbox* if it never was, and for a finished Item where it was filed — and *Last changed*, the finish time of a done Item and the last edit of any other. Done rows read greyed. A header sorts by its column and pressing it again reverses; it starts with the newest change first. **It has the same funnel and filter bar as a Dashboard** (and on a phone the same summary line and sheet), its **Status** offering *Done* as well as *To do* and *In progress*, and the tab carries a filled funnel while a filter is on. Sorting and filtering run over every Item, and the table is then drawn **fifty rows at a time** with **Show more**; the heading says how many Items there are, and "*n* of *m*" while filtered, so a narrowed view never reads as the whole. With none it says "There are no items yet."; with a filter matching none, "No item matches the filter." The filter is kept in this browser and forgotten at sign-out like a Dashboard's, and is the table's own: no Dashboard's filter changes with it. A row opens the Item's form, whose Status control reopens a done Item. The Inbox stays open beside it, and the table scrolls sideways where it must. Its own **…** and tab menu offer only **Hide All items tab**.

**Panel** — a movable, titled box on a Dashboard, sharing a row with up to three others, each a **saved view of the Items filed onto it**, with a user-editable title.

Panels are added, renamed, moved and deleted **on the Dashboard itself**, not on a settings page, because dragging one Panel past another *is* the editing and has to happen where the Panels are drawn. **Add a Panel from the control at the right of the Dashboard bar**, which asks what to call it in the same question a Dashboard is named in, saying what a Panel is; each Panel carries a menu opened the three ways a Dashboard's or a Workspace's own tab opens its, plus a button on its header the same as the Dashboard or Workspace you are on now carries at the right of its own bar or header (see "One menu, however it opens" in `docs/product/across-the-app.md`), offering **Select all** (on a Panel of items or a Filter panel, as the first entry in a group of its own, unavailable and saying *This panel has no items* while it has none), rename, move to another dashboard and delete. Moving within a Dashboard is by dragging a Panel's header onto another Panel to join its row, or into the gap between two rows to take a row of its own. **The Panels move as the drag does**, so the arrangement under the hand is the one dropping will keep — the Panel in the air is drawn back in its slot, the rest step aside as it passes, and a placement that cannot happen, a row already four across, shows nothing happening rather than promising a move the drop would decline. Letting go keeps what is drawn; a drag that ends where it started changes nothing. **A Panel still has no size of its own — it fills its share of a row, and the row is what carries a height — but both are set by dragging the lines the row is drawn with.** The line **under a row** sets how tall it is, between 160 and 720 pixels so a row can neither swallow the screen nor shrink below a list worth reading, and double-clicking it puts the row back to being as tall as what is in it. The line **between two Panels** moves whole twelfths from one to the other, so the row still adds up to a whole and the Panels beside the pair keep their share. A size goes into the Layout on screen exactly as a move does — drawn as the hand goes, kept when it stops. Both lines are the pointer's, so sizing is absent from a keyboard and a touchscreen until entries that step them land instead. A title is required, stored trimmed, one line of at most 60 characters, and unique among a Dashboard's live Panels whatever the capitalization, so two Dashboards of one Workspace may each have a *Reading list*. Deleting asks first, in the same dialog as everywhere else, and says the Panel goes from every Layout of its Dashboard. **Every Dashboard arrives with one, called *Panel 1***, so a Workspace has somewhere to file an Item into from the moment it exists — the Inbox holds what no Panel holds, so a Dashboard with no Panels is an Inbox with no way out of it. That is a starting condition and not a rule: a Dashboard may end up with no Panels at all, unlike a Workspace, whose last Dashboard cannot be deleted. **A Panel holds the Items filed into it**, in an order you set, and drawing them is all it does — a rule for what *arrives* in one without being filed is designed, not built (see "Panel types and live rules" in `docs/ideas.md`).

**Layout** — a Dashboard's arrangement of its Panels, which nobody chooses: it is drawn the same on every screen from 480 px up (see "Layouts: the arrangement you draw").

**A Panel holds Items, text, or what a rule gathers**, chosen when the Panel is made and never after. A Panel of **text** holds prose written straight onto the Dashboard — a standing agenda, a note the team reads, a heading over a row — and its own menu settles two things about it. It is **read-only** or open for writing; and its words are drawn as the **characters that were typed** or as **what they mean**. Neither is a conversion: the same Markdown is stored either way, so switching back and forth loses nothing. **Characters is the default**, because drawing them costs nothing to fetch, where formatting needs a renderer and writing formatted text needs an editor — so a Dashboard costs nothing to open until somebody asks. Read-only is what a Panel of text settles into, being a thing to read; only the Panel you have just made arrives open, because an empty box refusing to be written in is nonsense one menu away from the gesture that made it. **Nothing is filed onto a Panel of text**: it is not offered when filing, it takes no drop, and an Item asked onto it directly is refused — one filed there would leave the Inbox and be drawn nowhere.

**A Dashboard is an ordered list of rows.** Each holds up to four Panels, which fill its width between them and share its height; a Panel's width is its share of its row rather than a measurement, so the Dashboard never scrolls sideways on any device. **Nothing wraps**: which Panels share a line is something a person put there, not a consequence of their widths, so it survives the screen changing under it. Moving a Panel is therefore a drop — onto another Panel to join its row, or into a gap to take a row of its own — rather than a move to a coordinate, and there are no holes to leave behind.

A **Dashboard** is a saved arrangement of Panels; there can be as many per Workspace as you like. What a Panel of Items can be filtered or classified by beyond manual filing — a Project, a Person, a Topic, Focus, an AI classification, a live query — is designed, not built: see "Panel types and live rules" in `docs/ideas.md`.

## What a Panel shows — manual filing

**Filing.** Any Item can be filed onto a Panel from its own menu, which is what takes it out of the Inbox (see `docs/product/inbox.md`). An Item filed on several Panels appears on all of them: **Also show on…** in a row's menu shows it on one more, and **Remove from this panel** stops one Panel showing it while every other carries on — an Item removed from the only Panel holding it is back in the Inbox. Dragging a row reorders it within the Panel it is already on — Ordering (see `docs/product/across-the-app.md`).

**Never propose, on a Panel of items' menu, keeps Cockpit from suggesting that Panel**, ticked while it is set: no Inbox chip names it, a chip already naming it is not drawn and cannot be taken, and Items filed onto it are not learned from — so a Panel filed into alongside another, such as a *Next up*, does not pull every new note towards it. Clearing it restores both, past filings included; a chip it hid comes back until Cockpit next suggests a Panel for that Item. A Panel of text or a Filter panel has no such entry, nothing being filed onto either.

**A Panel of items is drawn in the order you set, unless it is sorted.** **Sort…** in its menu opens a question with a **Manual / Sorted** switch; on *Sorted* it holds one row per criterion — Title, Priority, Created, Due date or Type, each offered once — each **Ascending** or **Descending**, reordered with ↑ ↓ and added with *+ Then by…*, the last row never removable. Rows go by the first criterion, then the next wherever two tie, then the order you set. Ascending is A→Z ignoring case, Low→High, oldest first, soonest first and your Types' order, which hovering the direction says; a new Priority row starts on Descending, every other on Ascending. **An Item with no value for a criterion** — no title, Priority, due date or Type, or a Type since deleted — **goes last in either direction.** Flipping to Manual and back inside the question keeps the rows until Save; Cancel and Escape discard.

The sort is **kept on the Panel**, the same on every device, and **Manual is having none**: switching back gives the order you set, untouched. While sorted, **a row cannot be dragged to a new place in that Panel** — the pointer says no and no landing line is drawn — but Items are still filed onto it, going to the top of the order you set and drawn where the sort puts them. A **sort mark** beside the Panel's name, only while sorted, reads the sort back on hover — *Sorted: Due date ascending, then Priority descending*. A change of sort has no undo, as *Filter…* has none. Neither a Panel of text nor the Inbox is sorted; a Filter panel is sorted its own way, below.

**A drag held near the top or bottom edge of a box scrolls that box**, for an Item and a Panel alike, on a desktop. The speed eases in from the inner edge of the band and tops out around 480 pixels a second right at the edge — a speed, not a rate per screen refresh, so it reads the same on every screen. One box scrolls at a time: a Panel that runs out of room hands the scroll to the Dashboard around it, but a scrolling Dashboard is never taken over by the Panels sliding past under the pointer; to scroll a Panel's own list, move into that Panel and then to its edge, before it runs out. The band sits inside the box's edge, never past it, so the Dashboard bar above — where resting a drag on a tab switches Dashboards — is not part of it. The Inbox column never scrolls under a drag, being sorted by age with nowhere in it to aim. A Panel drag scrolls only the Dashboard, and the arrangement drawn under the hand follows the scroll while the pointer is still.

## What a Filter panel shows — gathered by rule

**A Filter panel shows every open Item filed on a Panel of its Workspace that meets all its conditions, or any of them where it is set to *any***, wherever it is filed, so seeing everything due today no longer means opening every Dashboard in turn. **Never an Item still in the Inbox**, which is what keeps triaging worth doing, and an Item filed on two Panels appears once.

**Five conditions: Due date, Priority, Type, Panel and Status**, each offered once per Filter panel. A Due date condition asks for a window — *overdue*, *today*, *this week* (Monday to Sunday), *this month*, *this quarter* or *not set* — the four periods carrying **or overdue**, ticked unless you untick it, because "due today" without it hides exactly the work most in need of looking at, measured against the calendar of whoever is looking. A Priority or Type condition takes several values and matches an Item holding any of them — *Priority is High or Normal*, *Type is OKR* — and an Item with no Priority, or no Type, matches neither. A Panel condition takes several Panels of items and matches an Item filed on any of them. A Type or Panel condition's values ignore one since deleted; left with none live, it matches nothing rather than every Type or Panel. A Status condition takes To do and In progress — *Status is To do* — and never Done, since a Filter panel never draws a Done Item to begin with; one saved before it took values reads as *Status is In progress*.

**Values are chosen from a list, and shown as chips.** Type, Priority, Panel and Status each show their chosen values as chips, each with its own ×, struck through in red where the card says *is not*. After the chips, *+ Type*, *+ Priority*, *+ Panel* or *+ Status* opens a checkbox list, and ticking or unticking there adds or removes the chip. The list gets a search box once it has more than six options. **Panels are listed under their Dashboard**, Dashboards in tab order and each one's Panels as its Layout draws them, and the search matches a Dashboard's name too, keeping all its Panels; a Filter or a Panel of text is never offered. A Panel's chip names both — *Errands · Day to day*. A chosen Type or Panel since deleted shows no chip.

**Is or is not.** Type, Priority, Panel and Status each open on *is* and can be set to *is not*, which matches an Item holding none of the chosen values: an Item with no Priority or no Type passes *Priority is not High*, and an Item filed on any excluded Panel fails *Panel is not Archive*. An *is not* card with nothing chosen, or whose chosen Types or Panels have all been deleted, filters nothing: it lets every Item through under *All* and adds none under *Any*, where an *is* card in the same state empties the Filter panel under *All*. The funnel reads them *Type is not Note or OKR*, *Priority is not Low*, *Not filed on Archive*, *Status is not In progress*, and leaves out an *is not* card that filters nothing. A Filter panel open in a tab still running the previous release reads an *is not* as an *is*, and a save from it writes that back.

**All or any.** A Filter panel joins its conditions with *and* unless somebody chooses *or*, which shows an Item meeting one condition where *All* asks for every one — *due this week or overdue, and High priority* in one list. The rows are joined in a left gutter — *Where* on the first, then *and* or *or* on each row after it — and the second row's joiner is a dropdown that switches the whole Filter between the two; the rows after it only repeat its word. With one condition there is nothing to choose. On *or* the description says so. The choice is kept when conditions are removed down to one. A condition with nothing ticked adds nothing under *Any*, where under *All* it empties the Filter panel. Every Filter panel that was never asked means *All*, and so does a save that carries no setting — a stale tab saving over an *Any* Filter panel puts it back to *All*, and one tick undoes that. Mixes such as *(A and B) or C*, sets of conditions joined by *or*, are a decided design and not built (see "Panel types and live rules" in `docs/ideas.md`).

**Its own menu is where it is told what to show.** *Filter…* opens a question with one box per condition, on a grid whose columns line up from box to box — the field in bold, the operator or window, the values, and a × that removes it — and *+ Add a condition* under them; saving with none left puts it back to saying it has nothing chosen. A funnel beside the Panel's name marks it, and hovering the funnel reads the conditions back as a sentence — *Any of: Due this week or overdue; Priority is High* under *Any*.

**Group by.** *Filter…* ends with a *Display* section, set apart from the conditions by a divider, holding a **None / Dashboard / Panel** choice and one line saying what it does — *One list.*, *A heading per Dashboard, its matching items under it.*, or *A heading per Panel, such as "Errands · Day to day". An item on two Panels appears under each.* The choice is saved with the conditions, kept on the Filter panel and the same on every device. *None* is the one list a Filter panel has always drawn, and what every Filter panel starts on — a save carrying no grouping puts it back to *None*, as with *All*. Grouped, the list is a heading per Dashboard, or per Panel of items, with its own count, sticking to the top of the Panel while its rows scroll under it; a group with no matching row is not drawn, and rows within a group keep the sort. **Groups go in board order**: Dashboards in tab order, a Dashboard's Panels as its Layout draws them, row by row and left to right. **An Item filed on two Panels appears under each**, and under each Dashboard once, while the count beside the Filter panel's name still counts distinct Items. A Panel heading reads the Panel's name first and its Dashboard's after, fainter — *Getting started · Dashboard 1* — so a narrow Panel truncates the Dashboard first. Grouped by Panel, a row's *also in* names only the other Panels it is filed on, and an Item on that Panel alone shows none; the funnel's sentence ends *; grouped by Panel*. The Dashboard filter narrows each group, and a group it empties is not drawn.

**A Filter panel's rows go by its sort, and a Filter panel nobody has sorted goes by Due date ascending, then Priority descending, then Created ascending**, so a row with no due date is last and nothing changes until somebody chooses otherwise. **Sort…** in its menu asks the question a Panel of items gets, without the Manual / Sorted switch — nothing is filed onto a Filter panel, so it has no order of its own to go back to — and with its last row never removable. A final tie goes oldest first. The sort mark shows on a Filter panel whenever it has a sort of its own, beside the funnel; the same rules for ties and for Items with no value apply, and the sort is kept on the Filter panel like a Panel's.

**Nothing is filed onto a Filter panel**, exactly as nothing is filed onto a Panel of text: it is not offered when filing, it takes no drop, and an Item asked onto it directly is refused. A row's menu is the usual one without *Remove from this panel*, and rows are not reordered — where a row sits is a consequence of the Item rather than a filing anybody made.

## Narrowing a Dashboard — the Dashboard filter

**A Dashboard filter narrows the Panels already on a Dashboard**, where a Filter panel gathers from the whole Workspace and replaces nothing. A **funnel on the tab you are on** opens a bar under the dashboard bar holding six conditions that must all hold, in this order:

| Condition | Takes |
|---|---|
| **Status** | *To do* and *In progress*, either or both; *Done* is not offered, since a finished Item is on no Dashboard (the **All items** tab's bar does offer it) |
| **Priority** | several values, *No priority* among them |
| **Due** | the windows a Filter panel offers, with *or overdue* ticked by default |
| **Containing** | case-insensitive, over an Item's title, description and next action — not its sender |
| **Attachments** | *With* or *Without*; neither pressed is no condition |
| **Agent running** | off by default; on, only Items with an open run, whatever its status — a refused start included, which still shows its *didn't start* chip |

Status, Priority and Attachments are each drawn as one joined group of buttons inside a single outline, labelled only for *Attachments*; *Agent running* is a single button. **A pressed button is filled with the Workspace's accent, so a pressed button always means the Dashboard is narrowed**, and pressing it again turns it off, *With* and *Without* included. A *Due* that is not *Any time*, and a *Containing* that holds text, wear a 2px accent border. The bar is the same on the **All items** tab, and from 768 px up at every width; below 768 px, a phone, it is a summary line and a sheet (below). While any is set, every Panel of items and every Filter panel on that Dashboard shows only the Items that meet them: a Filter panel shows the intersection of its own conditions and the Dashboard filter's, a Panel's count counts the matching Items only, and **a Panel with no matching Item is not drawn** — a Panel of items with nothing on it, and a Panel of text, which has no Item to match, included. The Panels left in a row share it, a row with none left is not drawn, and the bar says how many it hid (*2 panels hidden*, nothing where it hid none); if it hid every Panel the board says *No panel has an item matching the filter*. The Inbox is untouched. An Item that stops matching leaves its Panel at once, taking the Panel with it if it was the last, and its open form stays open; an Item that starts to match brings its Panel back.

**It belongs to its Dashboard alone and is kept in this browser**, surviving a switch and a reload, and forgotten at sign-out; it is never written to the server, so it does not follow you to another device or person.

**A filter is never on out of sight.** A filtered Dashboard always shows its bar, and its tab carries a filled funnel — on a Dashboard you are not on too, where pressing the tab or the funnel switches to it. **×** in the bar clears the conditions and keeps the bar open; the funnel on the open tab clears the filter and closes the bar. **`F` opens the bar and puts the cursor in *Containing…*** (on *All items* too, on its own bar), so `F` and typing filters at once; on a filtered Dashboard it only puts the cursor there and never clears, and with the bar open and nothing set it closes the bar. **Escape in *Containing…*** hands the keys back to the page and keeps the text, the filter and the bar. `F` has the guards `C` has and does nothing on a phone, whose summary line and sheet have no keyboard; the funnel's tooltip names it, and a mouse click on the funnel shows *Tip: press F to filter*.

**Below 768 px the bar is replaced by a summary line and a bottom sheet**, with no change at 768 px and wider. The line is pinned in the bar's place, **only while a filter is on**, so it too shows after a reload. It is one row inside the bar's outline: a pill per set condition in the pressed style, in the bar's order, as many as fit and then *+n*; *· n hidden* where Panels are hidden; **Edit**; and **×**, which clears the filter and takes the line with it. Tapping the line opens the sheet. The sheet slides up over a dimmed scrim: *Filter · n panels hidden* with **Clear** at the right, one row per condition with its label in a fixed column, and a full-width **Done**. Each tap filters at once, the Panels updating behind it; **Done**, the scrim, Escape or a swipe down on the handle only close it, with focus back on the line. **Clear** clears and keeps the sheet open. The funnel on the tab opens the sheet on an unfiltered Dashboard, where closing it with nothing set leaves no line, and clears the filter on a filtered one. Crossing 768 px keeps the filter and closes the sheet.

**While filtered, every row is as tall as what is in it**, below the 160-pixel floor and past the 720-pixel cap where the Items need it, and **nothing about the arrangement can change**: no Panel, row line or divider can be dragged or double-clicked, the Panels left in a row keep their proportions to one another, *Move to another dashboard* and **+ Panel** are unavailable and say to clear the filter, and an Item is not reordered within a Panel. Filing, editing an Item and adding one on a Panel still work. Clearing brings every Panel back at its stored size, because nothing was written.

## Layouts: the arrangement you draw

A Dashboard has one arrangement, its Layout, and **nothing on screen offers a choice of one**: no control on the Dashboard bar, and no screen sizes anywhere in the account. The bar holds **+ Panel** and the Dashboard's **…** menu, both drawn only where a Dashboard is open, since the bar is also the Inbox's.

**From 480 px up a Dashboard is drawn with its Layout, whatever the screen**, so a laptop and a 4K screen show the same arrangement.

**Below 480 px, a phone, the Layout is read only for its order**: the Dashboard is drawn one Panel across, stacked as a wider screen reads the Layout — rows top to bottom, each row left to right — and not at the Layout's row heights. A Panel the Layout does not name yet comes after the placed ones, and a deleted one is skipped. Every gesture that rearranges (drag, row and divider sizing) is absent, and the Layout is kept for a wider screen.

**On a phone, a Dashboard collapses to its Panels' headers**, so a Panel far down it is in view at once and one tap away. A double-tap on a header, or **Collapse panels** on the Dashboard's **…**, draws every Panel as its header alone — as during a Panel drag — and the **…** then offers **Open panels**. While collapsed, a tap on a header outside its menu button opens every Panel and scrolls so that Panel's header sits at the top of the screen, as far as the page can scroll; **Open panels** opens them without scrolling. A Panel's menu still opens from its button, a long press or a right-click. Collapsed is not remembered: switching Dashboard, going to the Inbox, widening past 480 px or reloading opens every Panel. From 480 px up a double-click does nothing, and **Collapse panels** stays on the menu, unavailable, saying it is for a phone; on a Dashboard with no Panels it says there are none.

**Where the Inbox has room, a Dashboard has a *Go to panel* column at its right**, the Inbox's counterpart: a full-height well below the tab bar, headed *GO TO PANEL* with the key **G** and **»**, 224 pixels wide until its left edge is dragged, up to a third of the row it shares with the Dashboard (a double-click on the edge puts it back; the width is remembered in this browser and forgotten at sign-out, and the hidden strip keeps its own), and standing left of a docked Item form. It is a search box over the Dashboard's Panels, each a title and the Item count its header shows (none on a Panel of text), in reading order with a hairline between the Dashboard's rows, and a legend of its keys at its foot. **Every Panel is listed, whatever a Dashboard filter hides**: a hidden one reads faint as *filtered*, and going to it clears the Dashboard's filter first. **G opens it on this Dashboard and Shift+G on its Workspace, the cursor already in the search box**; Shift+G is G where the Workspace has one Dashboard. The same key again hides it and the other switches scope, keeping what is typed. Both are heard from the page, so inside the box they are letters. **Typing narrows the list by Panel name at once, with nothing highlighted**: the first **↓** highlights the first entry, **↑** and **↓** move the highlight and scroll the Dashboard to that Panel, and **Enter** goes to the highlight, or to the top match with nothing highlighted, and does nothing where nothing matches. Going, by **Enter** or a click, puts the Panel's header at the top of the Dashboard (as far as the page can scroll), outlines the Panel for a moment, hides the column and leaves the Dashboard where it is. **Esc**, in the box or the list, puts the Dashboard back where the mode began and hides the column; Esc and going hide it whatever opened it (a key, the strip or **«**). Every opening by a key or the strip starts with an empty search, on the key's scope (the strip's is *Dashboard*); a click back into the box keeps the search and the scope. There are three scopes under the box: a click on *Dashboard · Workspace · All* (the chosen one on tinted) searches this Dashboard, every Dashboard of its Workspace, or every Workspace, and the tooltips of the first two name **G** and **Shift+G**; *All* has no key. One that would add nothing (a single Dashboard, a single Workspace) stays visible, unavailable and saying why. In *Workspace* each Dashboard is a pinned accent sub-heading over its Panels, indented; in *All* each Workspace is a pinned uppercase heading with its colour dot above its Dashboards, both in tab order and with each Panel's own count. The search also matches a Dashboard's or Workspace's name, which keeps all its Panels. **The screen follows the highlight**: onto another Dashboard's or Workspace's Panel it switches there and scrolls to it; **Enter** keeps it, **Esc** puts you back on the Dashboard and scroll where the mode began. Moving adds no history entry and going adds one, so Back returns to where it began. Another Workspace's Panels are read from its snapshot only once *Workspace* or *All* is chosen, and a Workspace still loading or that failed to load says so under its heading. **G** works under the guards **I** has for the Inbox (not while typing, not under a menu or window, not with Ctrl, Alt or ⌘). Hidden, the column is a slim strip (**«**, *GO TO PANEL* written vertically, the key caps **G** and **⇧G**) that a click opens, and **»** hides it again. It is hidden until first shown, then remembered in this browser and forgotten at sign-out. Entries cannot be picked up: dragging a Panel on the Dashboard itself is the one way to rearrange it. All items, which has no Panels, and a phone, which has collapse-and-jump above, draw none.

**Changing the arrangement changes the Layout drawn, and asks nothing.** Dragging a Panel goes into it, whatever screen you are on.

**A Dashboard that has never been arranged is drawn to fit the screen it is on**: the Panels in the order they were added, on rows of a count that keeps a Panel worth reading — one across on a phone, three on a laptop, four on anything wider; a phone stacks them in that order too. It has no Layout at all, and the first move records one from what was drawn.

## Sections: a title across the board

**A Section is a row of its own in a Dashboard's Layout, holding only a title**, drawn full width as a band tinted in the Workspace's accent with its title in small uppercase accent letters: no count, no box, and a height nobody sets.

- **Adding.** *Section* is the fourth choice in the question **+ Panel** asks, after Items, Text and Filter; chosen, the question reads *What is the new section called?* It lands at the foot of the board, where a new Panel lands, and the first Section on a Dashboard nobody has arranged records its Layout from what was drawn.
- **Moving.** A Section is dragged by its band, with a mouse's primary button and not while it is being renamed or from its menu button, into the gap between two rows, and is placed by the pointer's height alone, as a row taken by its grip is. **Nothing shares its line**: a Panel dropped on a Section's line shows nothing happening, one dropped in the gap under it takes a row of its own there, and a Section released where it started changes nothing. A Section's row has a fixed height, so the line under it sizes nothing; the line under the row above it still sizes that row. Like every rearranging gesture it is absent while a Dashboard filter is on and on a phone.
- **Its title** is required, trimmed, one line of at most 60 characters, and need not be unique, even against a Panel's.
- **Its menu** offers Rename and Delete only. **Delete takes effect without asking**, since only a title is lost.
- **It is not a Panel**: never offered for filing, in *Move to*, in a Filter panel's Panel condition or in Group by Panel, never listed in *Go to panel*, and never counted where Panels are.
- **It stays** with nothing under it, beside another Section, and when the Panel under it is deleted. On a phone it is drawn in its place in the column.
- **While a Dashboard filter is on, no Section is drawn.**
