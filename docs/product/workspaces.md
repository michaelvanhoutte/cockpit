# Workspaces

Everything is built from a **container hierarchy**: **Workspace → Dashboard → Panel**.

**Workspace** — everything you want in front of you while you work in one context, and the privacy boundary. It controls **which source accounts are connected and visible**, so private email is invisible while in *Work*: scoping rather than filtering after the fact.

**What decides between a Workspace and a Dashboard is whether the whole context changes**, and the pair of cases is the definition: a contractor working for two customers wants a Workspace each — different accounts, different mail, nothing shared — while somebody at one company serving two customers wants one Workspace and a Dashboard per customer, because the context is the same and only the view of it changes. The obvious advice, *one for work, one for personal, one per customer*, gives the second of those the wrong answer, which is why the app says this in the question that makes one rather than leaving it to be inferred.

Each Workspace has a **colour** that follows it into three places only: the dashboard band, the accent (headings, buttons, focus rings, type labels, and the menus and windows opened over the page) and the dot in the logo. The page and the top bar are the same neutral in every Workspace, so a Workspace's colour never washes the screen. The tab you are on is filled with the band and runs down into it. Colours are picked as a set from a fixed palette, on the form the Workspace's own tab opens (open decision #13); the palette and its contrast rules are in `docs/design-system.md`. A Workspace is drawn in the theme its colour names, whatever else it stored; a colour the palette never had is drawn in the default theme. Nothing stored is rewritten.

**A Workspace is made from the `+` at the end of the tabs**, which opens the question a Dashboard and a Panel are named in and says what a Workspace is. **Everything else is on the tab itself, and on the header's own button for the tab you are on**: the menu holds *Edit…*, then *Show All items tab* (*Hide All items tab* once it is on) in a group of its own, since it switches a tab of the Workspace rather than editing it, and *Delete* last. It acts on the Workspace whose menu it is, so another Workspace's tab can be switched without opening it; hiding it while that Workspace's *All items* is on screen returns to its first Dashboard (see `docs/product/dashboards.md`). There is one entry for changing a Workspace rather than a *Rename* beside it, the form being what renames. **The tab opens its own menu three ways, one per input**: a right-click, a press on the tab you are already on (that press has no other job, and it is what a finger has), and the keyboard's menu key — and the Workspace you are on also carries the same menu as a visible **…** at the right of the header (see "One menu, however it opens" in `docs/product/across-the-app.md`). **The name and colour are changed together, on a form over the page**; nothing is sent until *Save*, so a colour is chosen and looked at rather than applied on the press, and *Cancel*, *Escape* and a press outside all discard both halves. A name is required, stored trimmed, one line of at most 60 characters, and unique among live Workspaces whatever the capitalization. **Deleting a Workspace keeps everything that was in it**: its Items stay filed against it, because the router learns from the decisions filed there (see [routing-learning.md](routing-learning.md)). The name becomes available again. The last Workspace can be deleted, and the app then opens on the screen that makes one.

**The Workspaces are in the order you put them in, left to right.** A new Workspace goes last, after every Workspace the account has ever had; deleting one closes the gap. A tab is moved by dragging it along the strip — the pointer's alone, absent on a touchscreen and unreachable from a keyboard. **The tabs move as the drag does**, so the strip under the hand is the order dropping would keep. A move shows itself before the server agrees, so a second move can follow a first and a dropped tab does not snap back for a round trip. If it is refused, because a Workspace was made or deleted in another tab, the strip goes back.

**Ask up front only what has to be decided up front, and teach the rest at first use.** An account arrives holding one Workspace called *Workspace 1* with one Dashboard, *Dashboard 1*, and opens on that Workspace, asking nothing. **The Dashboard holds a Panel called *Getting started* and, beside it, the empty *Panel 1***; *Getting started* holds the steps of finding your way round Cockpit, each an open Task ticked off with its row's ✓, and the Inbox holds one Task saying what the Inbox is for. They are ordinary Items and an ordinary Panel, renamed, filed and deleted like any other, and only an account nobody has used gets them — never one somebody has, and never the shared guest account. Nothing is asked at sign-in: nobody knows when they want a second Workspace or Dashboard until they have used the first for a while, so each is explained where it is made (the `+` dialogs), not before.

The full path to any box:

```
Workspace: "Work"
├── Dashboard: "Today"       ← landing dashboard
│   ├── Panel: "Focus Today"
│   ├── Panel: "Project Falcon"
│   └── Panel: "People to talk to"
├── Dashboard: "Dormant projects"
│   └── Panel: "On hold"
└── Dashboard: "Research"
    └── Panel: "To read"
```

The landing dashboard is the desk landing: a bare `/` opens the first Workspace's dashboard at a desk, and the Capture page on a phone (see `docs/product/inbox.md`).

A Workspace connects its sources in Settings › Connections: a Microsoft Teams account, whose saved messages land in its Inbox, Gmail accounts and Outlook mailboxes. Every conversation labelled `Cockpit` in a connected mailbox, whatever its age, arrives in the Workspace's Inbox within a minute of connecting, one Item per conversation ever, titled with the subject — "(no subject)" where there is none — described with the plain text of the most recent labelled message, from "Gmail · sender", opening the conversation in Gmail; the usual clean-up then runs. Each mailbox is checked about every five minutes, and its row reads "last checked …", or "Failing:" and why — no label called `Cockpit` in the mailbox (create it in Gmail), or Google no longer accepts the sign-in (connect again), and a failing row's menu offers *Reconnect*, which signs in again on the same row; connecting the same mailbox again finds what is already in rather than bringing it in twice. **A Gmail connection made before Gmail moved onto the connector SDK reads as failing until it is reconnected, once**: nothing it brought in is moved or removed, and its old Items stay as plain Items, so a conversation still labelled comes in again beside its old Item, which you clear by hand. The same mailbox connected to two Workspaces gives each its own Items. A conversation labelled afterwards, by hand or by a Gmail filter, arrives within about five minutes too, once; a reply in a conversation already in adds nothing. A mailbox that has not been checked for about a week is read from the start again, so nothing labelled meanwhile is missed. When the label comes off a conversation, or its mail is binned or deleted, its Item is marked done at the next check; labelled again, the same Item reopens, undismissed if it had been dismissed. A mailbox with no label called `Cockpit` marks nothing done. **The other way, within seconds:** marking a Gmail Item done or dismissing it takes the `Cockpit` label off its whole conversation, and reopening it or undoing the dismiss puts the label back. Nothing else in the mailbox is touched, though Gmail's permission would allow it. Where Gmail refuses — the sign-in expired or revoked — the Item keeps what you did, the row says it is failing, and the label follows once the connection works again; one conversation Gmail will never relabel is given up on, the Item keeping what you did, so it holds up no other. A change made in Cockpit and not yet in Gmail wins over whatever Gmail did meanwhile, since Gmail does not say when a label changed. Disconnecting stops this: a change not yet in Gmail is dropped, and the Items stay as they are. **A Gmail connection follows one mark, chosen when connecting**: *Labelled `Cockpit`*, chosen to start and followed by every connection made before there was a choice, or *Starred (flagged in Outlook)* — Outlook, its phone app included, cannot label Gmail mail, and its flag for follow-up is stored by Gmail as a star. Everything above is the label's. **By star, only a conversation starred after connecting comes in**, within about five minutes and once, as the label's Items are made; one already starred never does — not on connecting, nor on connecting again — because Gmail records that a conversation is starred but never when. Its row reads "Gmail · starred · last checked …", and it never fails for want of a label called `Cockpit`. **Its Item is kept in step with the star both ways, as the label's is**: unstarring the conversation, or binning or deleting its mail, marks the Item done at the next check, and starring it again reopens the same Item, undismissed if it had been dismissed; marking the Item done or dismissing it takes the star off the whole conversation within seconds, and reopening it or undoing the dismiss puts the star back — the star alone, and nothing else. Refusals, and a change made in Cockpit winning, are as for the label. A full read of what is starred, on connecting or after a week unchecked, only marks done what is not, and makes no Item. The same mailbox followed by star in two Workspaces shares one star: done in one unstars it, and the other's Item is done at its next check. **What a connection follows changes without connecting again**: its row's *Change…* offers the same choice, starting at the mark followed now, and the row then names the new one. A switch counts from its own moment, as connecting does — to the star, nothing starred already comes in; to the label, every conversation labelled `Cockpit` does — and a change made in Cockpit and not yet in Gmail under the old mark is dropped, as disconnecting drops it. Items that came in under the other mark stay as they are and are not kept in step until the connection follows that mark again. Switching to the mark already followed changes nothing. Only a connection following the label fails for want of a label called `Cockpit`, so a failing one switched to the star stops failing at the next check. **A connector may ask one question of a connection**, such as what to bring in: Connect then asks it first, the row names the answer, and its *Change…* asks again without signing in, dropping any change to an Item not yet passed on to the source; the connector picks the new answer up at its next check. **An Outlook mailbox** — a Microsoft 365 work or school account; a personal Outlook.com or Hotmail one is refused — is connected the same way, apart from the Teams account and asking Microsoft for permission to read and change mail. Its card says the organisation may have to approve Cockpit first, and a failing row says when that is why; Connect asks which mark to follow, *Flagged*, chosen to start, or *Category `Cockpit`*. Every message carrying the mark, whatever its age, arrives as an Item of its own — one per message, not per conversation, so two flagged messages in one thread are two Items — titled with the subject ("(no subject)" where there is none), described with its plain text, from "Outlook · sender", opening it in Outlook on the web. Its row reads "Outlook · Flagged · last checked …", or "Failing:" and why. A message marked later comes in within about five minutes, or has its Item reopened. Completing or clearing the flag, taking the category off, or moving the message to Deleted Items or deleting it marks the Item done at the next check; archiving it, moving it elsewhere or a reply to it does nothing. Unlike Gmail's label, a mailbox with no category called `Cockpit` does not fail, since Outlook accepts the category on a message either way. **The other way, within seconds:** marking an Outlook Item done or dismissing it completes the flag or takes the category off its message, and reopening it or undoing the dismiss flags it again or puts the category back — that mark alone, nothing else in the mailbox — and a change made in Cockpit and not yet in Outlook wins, as for Gmail. *Change…*, *Reconnect* and the same mailbox in two Workspaces behave as for Gmail. **An Outlook connection can file what it brings in on the Panel named after its category**, skipping the Inbox: Connect asks it under the mark question, and *Change…* offers it again, as a tick box, *File it on the panel named after its category*, unticked to start (a *Reconnect* starts at the row's own value), and the row then reads "Outlook · Flagged · files by category · last checked …". Only a message the connection already follows is filed — a category alone brings nothing in — and the followed `Cockpit` category never names a Panel. A category names the one Panel of Items in the Workspace with that name, ignoring case, accents (`prive` names *Privé*), runs of spaces and `&` against `and`, or as *dashboard-panel*: `Cubico-Tasks` names *Tasks* on the Dashboard *Cubico*, with or without spaces around the hyphen, and only the Dashboard a Panel lives on counts, not one that shows it; one matching no Panel, Panels on two Dashboards, or more than one Dashboard–Panel pair, files nothing, and the check's history line says so, and a Filter, a Panel of text and a Section are never matched. Each category that names a Panel files the message there, so two file it on both; with none, it stays in the Inbox, and Cockpit never makes a Panel. Ticking the box counts from that moment: a category already on mail that is in files nothing. A category added later files the Item at the next check, beside wherever it is filed already; one taken off never unfiles it, and filing or moving the Item in Cockpit never changes the message. Filing by category is not learned from for proposals, and *Never propose* on a Panel does not stop it. **A Gmail connection files the same way, by label**: the box reads *File it on the panel named after its label*, and the row "Gmail · Labelled `Cockpit` · files by label · last checked …". An Item is a conversation, so a label on any of its messages counts. Accents are ignored as for categories, but a label is not read as *dashboard-panel*. Only labels you made name a Panel: Gmail's own (Inbox, Unread, Important, Starred, Sent, Draft, the inbox tabs) never do, nor the `Cockpit` label a connection follows the label by; one following the star treats `Cockpit` as any label. A nested label names a Panel only by its full name, `Clients/Atlas`. A label put on a message that has not the mark, such as a reply, is seen the next time the conversation is read for any other reason. Disconnecting forgets the sign-in and drops what was waiting; Microsoft offers no revoke for one application, so remove Cockpit at Microsoft to end the grant. **A Gmail or Outlook row's *History…*, and its *Failing:* line, open its history** in place of the list, with *‹ Connections* back: each check newest first under its day, kept 30 days and as far back as its newest 500 lines, ending "Older days follow, back to" the date 30 days ago where older lines are kept than it shows, when it ran, *Worked* or *Failed* in words, whether it was scheduled, a follow-up to one that said there was more to fetch, or a *Check now*, and a failure's message, kept to 2 KB, and what it did: how many messages its complete listing returned, in the followed mark's own words — "2 flagged, nothing new", "1 with category `Cockpit`" — on scheduled and follow-up checks too, though Gmail lists in full only on connecting or after a week unchecked, so its other lines carry none, and none on a failed check, a listing that stopped between pages or a line written before this, and counts of Items brought in, closed, sent back to the source and refused by it for good, each named by its title now and opening the Item, except a dismissed one, which reads "(dismissed)" and does not open, and one no longer held, which reads "an Item no longer in Cockpit". Where a check read the categories (Gmail: labels) of a message new to it, or changed since the last report, its line also names that message and each such name with what it did — "Tasks → filed on Tasks (Privé)", or "Tasks: skipped" and why: no Panel by that name; Panels on 3 Dashboards; matches 2 Panels (a category read as more than one Dashboard–Panel pair); the followed `Cockpit` category (Outlook); already on it when filing was ticked; filing by category is off. It names the first 50 messages and counts the rest ("and 3 more"), and a message whose names are unchanged adds nothing. A check that did anything or read names keeps a line of its own, and so does a *Check now*. **A Gmail or Outlook row's menu and the head of its history carry *Check now***, which makes that connection's check due at once: pressed while one is queued, that check takes the label; while one runs, nothing more is queued. Its line arrives in an open history when the check ends. Checks in a row that worked and did nothing fold into one line — "Checked 40 times, nothing new, 09:05–12:20", showing the newest check's count — and so do failures in a row with the same message. A day before today whose checks all worked folds into one line — "Checked 288 times · 3 brought something in · show them" — whose *show them* draws that day's lines; a day with a failure, today, a Teams connection's days, and the oldest day shown where older lines are kept than it shows, are drawn in full. Slack, Notion and the rest are not built; see "Sources to connect" in `docs/ideas.md`.
