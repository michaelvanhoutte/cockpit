# Inbox

**The Inbox is every open Item filed on no Panel**, scoped to the current Workspace — not a Panel with rows of its own, but the absence of a filing. That is what makes filing an Item the thing that takes it out of the Inbox, and it is why an Item can be moved back there: moving an Item to the Inbox is taking it off every Panel.

**Where it sits: beside the Dashboards, not among them.** Everything else flows out of it — it is read while working on a Dashboard and dropped into while looking at something else — so it is not one more view to switch to. Where there is room, it is a column down the left of every screen inside a Workspace, about a fifth of the width with a floor and a ceiling so it stays readable at 1280px and does not swallow a very wide screen; it scrolls on its own and so does the Dashboard beside it. Every screen inside a Workspace has it, including the ones a form or Settings opens over. On a phone, where a fifth of the width is about ninety pixels, the Inbox is a tab pinned at the left of the bar opening a screen of its own — and it keeps that address at every width, so a link made on a phone still works on a desktop. **It can be collapsed.** A `«` at the left of its heading collapses the column into a chip at the left of the Dashboard bar reading its name, its count and `»`, and the Dashboard takes the whole width; pressing the chip, or `I` from anywhere but a field, a menu or a window, brings the column back at the width it had. A row held on the chip opens the Inbox so it can be dropped into it, and dropping on the chip itself does nothing. The choice is remembered in this browser for every Workspace, like the column's width, which can be dragged by hand. A phone has no column, so nothing changes there.

Its name and the number in it head the column from the Dashboard bar, in the leftmost slot of it, so the bar is a row of headings and the Inbox is the leftmost — which is what says the Inbox belongs to the Workspace rather than to a Dashboard. On a phone there is no column to head, and the screen the tab opens carries the name itself.

**Capture has one way in: the header's Capture tab, and `C`.** The Inbox has no note box of its own. At a desk both open Capture as a window over the screen you are on — a note of several lines, the Types as chips, a row of Workspaces, and what has just been captured under it — and Escape puts you back where you were, the note already in the Inbox beside it. On a phone both open the Capture page, where capture is the primary use. `C` works anywhere inside Cockpit except while typing in a field, while a menu or window is open, or with Ctrl, Alt or ⌘ held; the tab's tooltip reads *Capture (C)*. A mouse click on the tab, the Inbox's `«` or chip, Go to panel's `»` or strip, the agents' dock's hide control or strip, or the funnel on a Dashboard's tab shows a toast at the bottom centre naming the key (*Tip: press C to capture from anywhere*; likewise `I`, `G`, `A` and `F`; for Go to panel, *Tip: press G to go to a Panel*), gone after 5 seconds or on ✕, and held back while an undo is offered in the same place; a key press, a keyboard activation and a phone show none, and nothing is stored. **Capturing never waits on the connection**: the note is kept on this device the moment Capture is pressed and sent once a connection gets through, *Just captured* reads *Waiting to send* until it lands, and the tab counts what has not — see `docs/product/offline.md`.

**A file dropped or pasted anywhere on the form, or chosen with its own Add button, becomes an Attachment on the note** — queued as a chip until Capture is pressed, then kept and sent with the note; see Attachment in the glossary for how one is added and drawn. At a desk the attachment line says so — "paste an image with Ctrl V", ⌘V on a Mac; a phone's line stays "Drag a file here, or".

**On Android, the installed app is in the share sheet for images, short clips, PDFs, text and links.** Sharing to it opens the phone Capture page on *Any workspace*, each shared file queued as an Attachment chip and shared text or a link in the note, for the person to add to and press Capture, as if they had put them there themselves; several shares made before Capture is opened land together on one note. What is shared is kept on the device, apart from captures waiting to send, and goes onto the note only once somebody is signed in, so a share made signed out or with an expired sign-in waits for the next sign-in; Sign out empties it. A file is checked as a dropped one is, so a kind Cockpit refuses is refused with the same message. A share that cannot be kept — storage refused, or about 100 MB already waiting — or that arrives before the installed app has updated to take shares, is lost and Capture says "Couldn't receive what you shared — update Cockpit and share again." once, until a capture is made or the next share arrives. iOS offers no way to share into a web app, and the sheet lists only installed apps, so a phone picks the option up when Android next refreshes the app (up to a day) or on reinstall.

**A strip inside the bottom edge of the note box sets a priority and a due date**, taking no row of its own: three flags (Low, Normal, High, the marks an Inbox row's flag wears, the level's name as the hover title), then **Due** with Today · Tmrw · +7d and a 📅 button opening the date picker. Every choice is one click and pressing the lit one again clears it, so there is no *None*. A day picked that is none of the three shows on the 📅 button with a ✕ beside it. Both reset to none once a capture lands, while Type and Where carry over; a refused capture keeps them with the note. The Item is made with both in the one capture. Every other front door leaves them out and captures with neither.

**An Aa button at the right end of the same strip, left of the mic, writes the note formatted** — the editor and toolbar the Item's description uses, in the window and on the phone page alike, with the strip staying one row at a desk. Capture always opens plain, and the editor is downloaded only when Aa is first pressed, so a plain note never pays for it; the note stays formatted through captures until the window or page closes. Aa is lit while formatted. Switching keeps the text: the plain box shows the note's Markdown exactly as it was, unless something was done in the editor. Captures, files, priority, due date and dictation behave the same in both: Ctrl+Enter captures the Markdown, a pasted or dropped file queues as a chip and is never put inline, and dictated words land in whichever box is showing. The note box resizes at a desk when formatted, and on a phone keeps the plain box's height. If the editor cannot load, the note stays plain with its text and says so, offering the newer version as the Item form does.

**A mic at the right end of the same strip dictates into the note**, the size of a flag, with an `EN` or `NL` tag beside it, on the window and on the phone page alike and drawn only where the browser has speech recognition. Tapping the mic listens until it is tapped again, through pauses; what is said is added to the note after a space and stays editable, and nothing is kept until Capture. The mic is filled and pulsing only once the engine has started. The tag switches English and Dutch, is off while listening, starts on English whatever the browser's language, and is remembered on this device. The browser's own engine does the listening, so it costs nothing, sends audio to the browser's vendor and needs a connection; a blocked or missing microphone, or no connection, is said in one line under the strip.

**A Write | Car switch in the phone Capture page's header opens the Car view**, for capturing by voice without looking: a card showing the words as they are recognised, one large round button, a large status line, and under them the `EN`/`NL` tag, a moon/sun button as large as it, a line about the screen and the last few notes captured from it. Tapping the button listens, through pauses; tapping it again captures at once with no review — *Any workspace*, the Type Capture starts on, no priority, no due date — through the same outbox, so it is kept offline; the phone vibrates and the button reads ✓ *Captured* for a couple of seconds, or *Nothing heard* and captures nothing. Dictation that fails after words were heard captures those words and says why it stopped; with none heard it says why in large text. The screen is kept on while the view is shown and the page visible, and a line says so quietly where the browser cannot (it cannot unlock a phone already locked). The language is the mic's: one choice per device, English to start with. The moon/sun button switches the view dark and back at any time, listening included, without touching the speech engine: dark covers the whole screen under the top bar (the Capture heading, the Write | Car switch, the page and its edge, and the empty band under the tabs, which takes the top bar's graphite), dimmed rather than inverted, and Write is never dark by it. The view is also dark whenever the whole app is (Settings' *Appearance*), and the moon/sun button is hidden then, since pressing it would change nothing; it returns with the view's own choice as it was once the app is light. It is one choice per device, light until first switched whatever the phone's own setting, and still switches where storage is unavailable, just unremembered; the top bar itself stays as it is. `/capture` always opens on Write, `/capture/car` on Car, and the installed app's icon menu has *Car capture* beside *Capture*; where the browser has no speech recognition the view says so and the switch still goes back.

**Where starts on the Workspace you are in**, in the window and on the page, and on *Any workspace* only when Capture is reached from outside one — a typed `/capture`, the installed app's shortcut, or a share. A different Workspace chosen in the window holds until the window closes, so several notes for one customer cost one choice; reopening starts on the current Workspace again. A note left on *Any workspace* waits in every Workspace's Inbox until somebody says where it belongs; the Workspace it was captured *from* is recorded either way. **On a phone Cockpit opens on the Capture page**: a bare `/` — the installed app's start, a bookmark, the return from sign-in — opens it on a screen narrower than 768px, starting on *Any workspace*, and the first Workspace's dashboard anywhere wider. An account with no Workspaces still goes to the screen that makes one, and a link to a Dashboard, the Inbox or a Workspace goes where it says; deleting the Workspace you are on lands on one that is left, not on Capture. `/capture` is the page at every width, because a link and the installed app's shortcut open it; the window has no address of its own, like Settings.

Processing an Item today means one of:

- **Mark it done** — with the **✓** that appears on the row under the pointer, or **Status ▸ Done** in its menu, which takes it off every list, saying when; the Workspace's **All items** tab, filtered to *Done*, is where to find it again, and reopen it.
- **File it on a Panel** — which is what takes it out of the Inbox, and the one thing that does. Every Item's own menu carries **Move to…**, opening a picker of the Panels in the Workspace, with the Dashboard you are on first, the five Panels most recently filed into above it, and the Inbox among the targets. A search box, focused on opening, narrows it by Panel or Dashboard name. Each Dashboard is a heading pinned while its Panels scroll past, and the picker keeps its height while you type. **Nothing pointless is offered**: **Also show on…** leaves out every Panel the Item is on, and **Move to…** leaves out its only Panel (one of several stays, since moving there takes it off the rest) and, for an Item already in it, the Inbox. **A Panel that does not exist yet can be made from it**: every Dashboard's heading carries a **+**, which opens a name field under it with **Add & move** (**Add & show** for **Also show on…**), makes a Panel of Items as **+ Panel** does and files the Item, or every selected Item, into it in the same gesture. Escape in the field closes the field and leaves the picker open; a refused name shows under the field until it is edited; a Panel made and then refused its filing stays. No **+** on a Dashboard filtered in this browser, where **+ Panel** is locked.
  **Filing offers its next step on the undo bar** - one Item by the menu, a proposal chip, a swipe or a drop, and a moved selection alike, covering exactly the Items that moved: **Show** switches to the Dashboard holding the Panel, scrolls to it and highlights the moved Item's row (every moved row, for a selection) for a moment, clearing a Dashboard filter that hides it as Go to panel does (an Item moved off that Panel meanwhile leaves the Panel scrolled to and nothing highlighted); **Also show on…** opens the usual picker over where you are, leaving out every Panel the Item is on now, and adds it (every moved Item, for a selection), and you stay where you were (the bar then offers Undo of that add alone, which takes every one off again). A move to the Inbox, a reorder and an **Also show on…** offer Undo alone. **An Item that belonged to no Workspace gets Show and Also show on… and no Undo**, since the filing decided its Workspace and cannot be put back; a selection holding one gets the same. The bar shortens the Item's title and never the destination: *“Look into a che…” moved to Admin & money*.
- **Delete/Dismiss** — which is reversible for as long as the bar offering it is on screen. A dismissed Item is kept rather than erased, and undismissing it brings it back, so putting it back is the same change made the other way.

Reading, replying, flagging and associating an Item are designed, not built — reading and replying need something to read (no connector exists yet), and associating needs a view onto the Associations that already exist in the schema: see "Sources to connect" and "Associations" in `docs/ideas.md`.

Filing it, finishing it or dismissing it takes an Item out of the Inbox; it stays reachable through the Panels holding it.

**Gestures:** **swipe left dismisses, swipe right opens the picker** — the same picker **Move to…** opens, so filing is one gesture on a phone and the same question either way. One meaning per direction, in every list rather than only the Inbox: the same swipe cannot mean two things depending on which list it is in, so removing an Item from one Panel stays in the menu. A swipe that stops short puts the row back, and one that is mostly vertical is the list scrolling and does nothing at all. **A swipe is a touch gesture**: on a desktop the same actions are the menu.

**A row Cockpit has proposed a Panel for shows a chip reading `→ Day to day ▸ Admin & money`**: the Dashboard, then the Panel, always both. Clicking it, or alt-double-clicking the row, files the Item there. On a row too narrow for it the Panel name is cut with an ellipsis and the Dashboard stays whole; hovering it, or its accessible name, gives the full "Dashboard ▸ Panel" and then the reason. No chip is drawn where the Panel or its Dashboard no longer exists, or where the Panel is set to **Never propose** (see "Panels" in `docs/product/dashboards.md`).

**The row names the action it would take, in the strip it uncovers**, from the first pixel it moves rather than from the threshold — which direction means what is the part a thumb cannot see, and by the time the threshold is reached the direction has already been chosen. A mark says it as soon as there is room for one and the word joins it once the strip is wide enough to hold the whole of it, a word half off the edge being worse than none; the words are the menu's own, **Move to…** and **Dismiss**, so the two ways to an action are not two things to learn. Past the threshold the strip fills with colour to say that letting go now takes it. The row **cannot name an action the release would not take**: what it says and what it does are one answer, not two.

**Dismissing a Gmail Item takes its conversation's `Cockpit` label off, and nothing more** — no archive, no delete (`docs/product/workspaces.md`); one that came in by star has its star taken off instead, and one that came in under the mark its connection no longer follows changes nothing in Gmail. **Open question for every other source:** does "delete" mean delete only here, or also archive or delete at the source? See "One-way vs two-way sync" in `docs/functional-definition.md` — the single biggest behavioral decision.

## How an Item reaches a box — must it pass through the Inbox first? (undecided)

The reframing that makes this tractable: **"in the Inbox" and "shown in a box" are two independent states, not two ends of one pipeline.** Associations are many-to-many and being finished with an Item is separate (see "Item + Association model" in `docs/product/items.md`), so an Item can be in either, both or neither, and "passes through the inbox first" is a per-source default rather than an architecture.

**Model A — Inbox-first (manual triage).** Everything lands in the Inbox; nothing appears in a box until deliberately filed there. *Pro:* one point of control. *Con:* obvious items still need manual routing.

**Model B — Direct routing by rule.** Rules send certain items straight to a box: "Slack messages in #customer-1 → Customer 1's board", "@mentions on a project → that project's box", "anything I star → its box". *Pro:* far less busywork. *Con:* items appear without a review step, so they need an "unseen" dot or a per-rule "also show in Inbox" toggle.

**Inside Model B, passive vs active capture:** being tagged or @mentioned is *passive* — route it **and** flag as unseen; deliberately saving a message is *active*, a strong intentional signal that should skip the Inbox entirely.

**Recommended lean (a hybrid).** Inbox by default for anything with no matching rule, plus per-source/per-channel routing rules so obvious and actively-saved items land directly in a box, optionally marked "unseen". Decision still open, and moot until a connector exists to route from.

## Swipe-right — settled

**Swipe-right opens the picker**, the same one **Move to…** opens. Filing is what an Inbox row most often needs and the picker is where the decision already lives, so a phone gets to it in one gesture rather than three taps.

**One meaning per direction, at one distance.** The other candidates — a quick process sheet, a wake date, done — were going to be told apart by how far the swipe went, and that is what the gesture cannot spare: it is already competing with the list scrolling under the same thumb, so the difference between a short swipe and a long one is not a difference a hand can be relied on to make. They stay in the row's menu, where there is no threshold to miss.
