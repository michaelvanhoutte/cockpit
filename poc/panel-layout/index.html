What this is testing

  • A dashboard is a list of rows. A row holds up to four panels, they always fill the whole width, and they always share one height — so there are no holes, no slivers and nothing to align by hand.
  • Every gap is somewhere a panel goes. Hover between two rows for + Add a row; hover a seam inside a row — either end, or between two panels — for a + that puts one in that row. A row never exists empty, so adding a row and putting something in it are one press.
  • Three gestures move and size things, and that is all there is. Drag a panel into another row, or into the gap between two rows to make a row of its own. Drag a row's bottom edge to set its height — double-click it to fit the row to what is in it. Drag the divider between two panels to give one more of the row than the other: a row is twelve units, so two panels can sit 6/6, 8/4 or 9/3.
  • The layout is a thing you pick, not a side effect. The dropdown on the right of the dashboard bar names the arrangement you are in, and New layout… is how one gets made — so nothing has to ask "this layout was made for a 1463 px screen, keep the change where?"
  • Automatic is one visible entry, not the invisible default. Pick it and the button reads Auto · Wide; change the screen and watch which layout it follows.
  • Where "Add a panel" goes — in the dashboard's own bar, or as a tile at the end of the board. Switch between them above.

Still open: whether a phone gets a layout at all or just stacks every panel; whether a row's height should default to fitting its content; and whether the divider should snap to twelfths or to a coarser set.
