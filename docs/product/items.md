# Items

Everything that flows in — email, Slack message, Notion page, later a calendar event or bookmark — normalizes into a single **Item**, storing: source app (or *internal*), source ID, a deep link back, sender/author, timestamp, its three texts, optional priority, optional due date, and whether it has been started or finished with. Native notes and to-dos created in the app have no source app and open in the app rather than deep-linking out, except that one carrying a Teams message link in its description or captured words offers Open in Microsoft Teams with it, as does an app's capture with no link of its own. **A note you type with a Teams message link in it is read from Teams** ("Read pasted Teams links", issue 1755): a few seconds after capture it becomes what Save to Cockpit would have made, its row reading *Microsoft Teams · Els Maes*, *Details* showing her message with the words you typed beside the link under it as your note (read as a Teams save with a note is, below), and the usual reading titling, describing and dating it; a title or description you edited before then is kept. It is read with a Teams connection of the Workspace you captured in, else any other in your account: a chat link (1:1, group or meeting) where one was granted chats, a channel post where one was granted channel posts, and a personal Teams link (`teams.live.com`) never. Anything it cannot read stays as typed, with Open in Microsoft Teams, and the connection is not marked failing. An app's capture is never read. An Item with a deep link offers to open at its source, in a new tab, from its row and from its "..." menu ("Open in Microsoft Teams"); an Item on a Panel also offers **Copy link** there and in its form (see `docs/product/across-the-app.md`); every Item not made in the app also names its source on a "From <source> - <sender>" line in its form, which is where the same link sits, and as "<source> · <sender>" on its row. The source goes by its product's name — "Gmail", "Microsoft Teams" — never by what is stored.

**An Item carries three texts, answering three different questions.** The **captured message** is what arrived, or what you said: written once when the Item is made and never changed by anything afterwards, because it is the record of what was actually captured, except once, when a note holding a pasted Teams link is read from Teams and becomes her message with your words as its note (above). The **title** names the Item and the **description** is what you have to say about it; both are yours to edit and neither is required. **The description is formatted text, stored as Markdown**: bold looks bold as it is written, and the same description can be worked on as its source. Bold, italic, links and both kinds of list have a button and a keyboard shortcut; a heading, a table, a code block or strikethrough has neither and is kept all the same, because a description pasted out of somewhere else must arrive whole.

**An image goes into the description by its own button, or by pasting or dropping it into the text.** It is in the text at once, where it was put — the cursor, or the drop point — drawn from the file on the device until it has uploaded; it is also one of the Item's Attachments, listed with the rest. An upload that fails takes the image back out of the text and says why in the undo bar. Saved before it has uploaded, the description shows a missing image on another device until it lands. Only a PNG, JPEG, GIF or WebP goes in the text; any other file is refused there, naming Attachments as where it can go.

**Only two of the three are ever shown.** The captured message is a record, not a name, and it is what the title and description are read back against. It stood in as a label until an Item had two names — the one its row showed and the empty one its form offered under it — which is what **capture writing the title** fixed: what you capture becomes the title, and where it does not fit one line of 200 characters the title takes the first 200 and the whole of it goes into the description, so nothing you typed is only in a text you cannot edit.

**A note added to an Item later never rewrites its captured message; each Addition sits beside it** ("Capture append: add to the Item you just captured with an Add to this button", issue 1640). The *Details* tab lists the captured message, then each addition under it, oldest first, headed with when it was added (*Added 14:32*, with the date where it was another day). Adding a note changes neither the title nor the description; a priority or due date picked in Capture's strip for it is the one thing it can change. See Capture in `docs/product/inbox.md` for how one is made.

**And a moment later Cockpit rewrites both of them, having read the note.** Capture is meant to be fast, so what arrives is clipped, abbreviated, half-typed and often a mix of English and Dutch — and two weeks later the row says `part 11 audit trail q for validation protocol, who signs off eod` with none of the context around it left. So the note is read and given a **title** as short as it can be while still naming this note and no other, and a **description** that writes out what was said so it still makes sense in two weeks.

- **It adds nothing the note does not contain.** Abbreviations are expanded, spelling corrected, a clipped sentence finished and a dictated one reordered; a fact, a name, a date, a reason or a next step the note does not state is never supplied. Where the note refers to something it never states, the description says the note does not say.
- **It proposes a Next action only where the note states a next step.** `dinner w steven, confirm 4 feb` reads as title *Dinner with Steven* and Next action *Confirm 4 Feb to Steven*; `kitchen renovation quote` states none and gets none. It is one short action in the note's language, never made up from what the work usually needs. An answer with a blank Next action, one over 200 characters or one that is not text proposes none, and the title and description still land. A note holding a pasted Teams link is read the same way.
- **It never invents a priority, and takes one you stated.** "High priority", "urgent" or "ASAP" sets High, and "low priority" or "no rush" sets Low; tone never counts. The words it read come out of the title and stay in the description. A priority you chose in the Capture strip, or set or cleared before the reading lands, is never replaced, and *Suggestions* lists the one it set.
- **It never invents a due date, and takes a day you named.** "Tomorrow", "Friday" or "the 24th" sets that day, counted on your calendar from the moment you captured the note, so a note kept offline and sent the next day still means the day after you said it. A time is dropped, since a due date has none, and an unclear day ("end of the month", "soon") is left unset. A note captured with no time zone - from Claude or a connector - is read in the time zone of your latest capture that had one, and with none no date is read. As with the priority, a due date you chose, set or cleared is never replaced, and *Suggestions* lists the one it set. The one date it does replace is a date a Gmail or Outlook connection gave the Item (see "Settings › Connections" in `docs/product/workspaces.md`), which is not yours until you set it.
- **It files the note where you said it goes, and only where that points to one place.** Say any part of the path — workspace, dashboard, panels: "for Home", "Day to day, Admin and money", "on Admin and money and on This week". Names are matched to yours however you say them, so "admin and money" finds *Admin & money*; a name that matches nothing is ignored, and nothing is ever created.
  - **A panel you name gets the note at the top**, skipping the Inbox, as if you had filed it yourself, and several named get it on each. A dashboard only narrows, so two panels of one name are told apart by saying the dashboard, and two in different workspaces by saying the workspace. A name that still points to more than one place files nothing, and the note waits in the Inbox with its usual suggestion. A panel set to Never propose is still filed on; a Filter or a panel of text never is.
  - **An *Any workspace* note is settled in the workspace the path points to**: the one you named, the one holding the panels it was filed on, or the one holding the dashboard you named when only one workspace has a dashboard of that name. It leaves the other workspaces' Inboxes, and where it is not filed, the panel suggested among the old workspace's panels is withdrawn and one suggested among the new workspace's.
  - **A note captured inside a workspace only matches within it**, and one you filed, dismissed or finished before the reading landed is left where you put it.
  - **Only names are shown, and only when you say a place**: the reading lifts the words out, and a second, cheaper reading matches them against your workspace, dashboard and panel names — never an Item. The words leave the title and stay in the description, and *Suggestions* lists the workspace it set and, in a *Panels* line, the panels it filed the note on, named as they were when filed.
- **A Teams message saved with a note is read as both, and your words win.** The note you type when saving it sits in the Item's captured message after the message, marked as yours, so it is still there without a reading. Where it and the sender disagree about a priority, a due date or a place ("urgent, call me today" against your "no rush, call her Friday": Low, due Friday) yours decides it, and a field your note leaves unsaid is still read from the sender's words.
- **A note's formatting stays on the same words.** Bold, italics, links and lists in the note come back in the description it proposes; a note without any gets none, and no headings or bullets unless it was a list.
- **The title, the description and the Next action are *proposed*, and yours the moment you edit any of them.** Editing one takes all three over for good, and nothing overwrites any of them again.
- **The answer is in the language the note was written in**, whichever of the two that is, and a note mixing them stays mixed.
- **Capture never waits for it and never fails because of it.** The Item is written and answered first; the reading follows. Where nothing is set up to read a note, where the reading fails, where what comes back is not usable, or in the shared guest account, which never calls the model, the Item simply keeps the title capture wrote.
- **Only notes captured after this shipped are read.** Nothing sweeps what is already there, so a title you wrote by hand is out of reach by construction rather than by a rule.

**The same reading may say the note genuinely takes two meanings, and offer both.** `bel jan` is *call Jan* or *call in January*; picking the wrong one silently is a wrong answer nothing afterwards can spot. Where that is true, Cockpit lists the other **Readings** beside the one it wrote onto the Item — each its own title and description, and a few words saying what that reading takes the note to mean — and choosing one puts its title and description in the two boxes, exactly as typing them in by hand would; a reading carries no Next action, so the Next action stays as it is. **Reporting none is the common case and the right one:** a note that is merely terse has one reading, and only a difference that would change what you do about the note is worth naming a second time. The row carries one quiet mark saying a note reads more than one way; the readings themselves wait for the form, the way the captured message does.

**A note that may repeat another says so, and offers both answers.** Cockpit pairs notes that seem to say the same thing. A flagged row wears a short chip, *Duplicate?*, whose hover names the other note; the Item form asks *Possible duplicate · keep which one?* and shows the two notes side by side (stacked on a phone, this one first): *This note (open)* and *The other note*, each with its title, where it came from and when, and the first line of its description, so two notes with one title can be told apart. **Keep this one** on a card keeps that note and dismisses the other, with the usual Undo: on the other note's card it also opens the other note's form, and on this note's card the form stays. **Keep both: not a duplicate** under the cards settles the pair, and **Open** on the other note's card opens it without deciding anything. The row's menu offers **Dismiss as duplicate** and **Not a duplicate**, where which note each acts on is clear. Nothing is merged: the note kept stays as it is. The chip goes once either note is dismissed, done, or the pair settled, the last at the click and back at the Undo's; a refusal brings it back with the reason in the bar from the row, or beside the warning in the form.

**A note that lands in the description is Markdown, not escaped text**, decided when capture started writing there: somebody who captured a `- ` list meant a list, and the source view and the *Details* tab both hold the note exactly as typed for the times they did not. The cost is that a note written over several lines reads as one paragraph, a single line break being a soft break in CommonMark; making capture propose formatting rather than inherit it belongs with the rest of the smarter capture.

**Each Item can also carry any number of Attachments** — an image, a short clip, a PDF, or a Markdown or plain text file (`.md`, `.txt`), added on the Item's own form by **+ Attach** (at a desk on the status line, on a phone among the chips) or **+ Add** on its Attachments tab, by dropping it anywhere on the form outside the description text, or by pasting it with the cursor in no text box, whichever tab is open. **The files have a tab of their own, *Attachments*, with their count beside its name.** It lists each as a chip: a thumbnail for an image, an icon with its name and size for anything else; a click opens or downloads the real file, and a Markdown or plain text file opens in a new tab as plain text, the raw Markdown unformatted. The Item tab keeps one line for them under *On panels*: the first four as small icons, each opening its file, then *N attachments →*, which opens the tab; with no files there is no line.  Removing one only takes it off the Item — nothing else about it having existed is undone by that. **A file chosen shows at once, marked *Attaching…* until it has uploaded and then as its chip, and a file removed is gone at once**; a file the server refuses leaves, and a removal it refuses brings the chip back, with its reason under the files (on the Item tab, under its line).

**The Panels an Item is filed on are listed and changed in its form**, in an *On panels* field under the description, the same search and **+ New panel** as Capture's *File on* and within the Item's own Workspace. **A Panel made there shows at once**, on its Dashboard and as the Item's chip, before the server has answered; if the Panel is refused, both go back and the undo bar says why. Removing the last returns the Item to the Inbox. A Filter panel that happens to show the Item is listed nowhere on the form: the Item is not filed on it.

**A row shows the title**, and *Untitled* where an Item has none — the label every place that names an Item uses (the undo bar, a drag, Find item), worked out where the row is drawn. **Where the row is too narrow to draw the whole label, hovering it spells the label out**, and where the row drew all of it, hovering says nothing: a tooltip repeating what you can already read is noise on every row of a list you scan. The cut is in the drawing alone, so a screen reader has had the whole label the entire time; a finger, which cannot hover, reaches **Open** from the row's own menu instead, and the form shows it whole.

**A row shows an Item's Next action on a line of its own under the title**: *Next:* in the accent's deep shade, then the text in body ink, on one line cut off with “…”. On a phone the line sits between the title and the line holding the pills and the source. An Item with no Next action, or one of only spaces, has no such line and its row looks as it always did. Hovering the line spells it out where it is cut off and says nothing where it is drawn whole. Find item and a Dashboard filter's *Containing* still match it, but nothing else names an Item by it. **The Item's form has a one-line Next action field directly under the title**, at most 200 characters, written by the form's usual rule (at once when docked, on Save otherwise); emptying it clears it, and a finished Item keeps it. Setting it takes the title and description over from Cockpit, as editing either does, without Cockpit learning anything from it. It can also be given when an Item is captured, and a Next action over 200 characters is refused there too.

Each Item can carry any number of **Associations**, to one or more **People**, **Projects** or **Topics/Areas** — stored today (`packages/shared/src/domain/item.ts`), with no view yet that adds, shows or filters by one. What Panels do with associations once there is one is not built.

**An Item is To do, In progress, Waiting for reply or Done.** Starting it, waiting and finishing with it are each a time, so each says *when* — an Item in progress wears a pill saying so on its row. **The Item's form and its row's menu both offer all four**, the menu as a **Status** row opening a submenu. The form commits the choice at once when docked and saves it with the rest when not; In progress keeps a start time the Item still carries, and To do clears it. Undoing Done returns to In progress rather than to To do where it was started before being finished with, and returns the Item to where it was filed. **A Done Item leaves every list but is not lost:** the Workspace's **All items** tab lists it — and any Item not yet assigned to a Workspace, as the Inbox does — with *Done* chosen in its **Status** filter to see only the finished ones, each row saying where it was filed ("Day to day ▸ Admin & money", each Panel named when there are several, *Inbox* when it was filed nowhere or its Panel has been deleted) and when it last changed. Opening a row opens its form, where changing the status reopens it. **Reopened, it takes back its place on each Panel it was filed on**, however long it was done, among the Items moved there since, in the same order on every device. Dismissed Items have no such list yet. dismissing one is a separate tombstone, the one that makes it reversible. A due date is a field.

**An Item waiting on someone else is Waiting for reply** ("Set an Item Waiting for reply, and wait again after chasing", issue 1463). *Wait for reply…* on its row menu and its form, or *Waiting for reply* from either's Status, asks *Remind me in [N] days* — *1 · 3 · 7 · 14*, or a whole number from 1 to 365 that Enter accepts, starting on the number chosen under Settings › *Follow-ups* (`accounts.md`) — and makes the Item due N days from today, replacing any due date. On a waiting Item it reads *Wait again…*: the due date moves, and *Waiting since* stays on the first day. A waiting Item stays where it is, wearing *Waiting since <date>* beside its deadline pill — on the title line at a desk, under the title on a phone — and is open everywhere else: a Gmail label or Outlook flag stays, and a repeating one brings no next copy. Done ends the wait and undoing Done brings it back; To do or In progress clears it. The form writes a wait by its usual rule, at once docked and on Save otherwise.

**An Item following a conversation sets its own status by who wrote last** ("A Gmail starred conversation is one Item whose status follows who wrote last", issue 1705): a message from you makes it Waiting for reply as *Wait for reply…* would, and one from anyone else makes it To do, each replacing the due date. So far a Gmail conversation brought in by star, an Outlook conversation brought in by flag (yours is a message in Sent Items or sent from an address of yours or your Cockpit alias, and it waits only when someone else is on the conversation; "Outlook follows the flagged conversation as Gmail does", issue 1707, and "Outlook capture: a mail you Cc to your Cockpit alias arrives To do…", issue 1760), and a LinkedIn conversation told of by a starred notification email, are the only ones an Item follows (`workspaces.md`); its row says who wrote last and when in place of the source and sender.

**Email… on an Item's row menu and form starts a Gmail conversation the Item then follows** ("Email… makes a Gmail draft the Item then follows", issue 1708): it makes a draft in the Workspace's Gmail connection following the star, with the Item's title as subject and nobody in To, and opens it in a new tab. On an Item already following a Gmail conversation the entry reads **Reply…**, on its form's Conversation section too, and makes a reply draft in that conversation. From the click the Item is In progress and its row reads *Gmail · Draft started*; once the draft is sent, the next check makes it Waiting for reply; a draft deleted unsent puts back the status it had and the Item follows nothing. One such connection is used as it is; with several, a small menu asks which, headed *From* (small, grey, not a choice) over one entry for each, the one you used last first, each the connection's name over its source in small grey text (*Gmail*, *Outlook*), as the Connections list shows it; the menu is as wide as its longest entry up to about 320px, and a longer name ends in "…" with the whole name on hover ("Email…'s mailbox menu does not say it is the From", issue 1723); with none, the entry is not shown. A draft Gmail would not make says why, and the Item is put back. A click sent twice makes one draft.

**Email… and Reply… work with an Outlook connection as they do with Gmail** ("Email… makes an Outlook draft the Item then follows", issue 1709): with a connection following the flag, the draft is made in the Outlook mailbox (a new message with the Item's title as subject, or a reply draft in the followed conversation) and opened in Outlook on the web, the row reads *Outlook · Draft started*, and sending it, deleting it and a failure behave as above. A Workspace with a Gmail and an Outlook connection is asked which; an Outlook connection following the category offers nothing.

**A draft Claude wrote says so, and Claude can write it again until you edit it** ("Mail follow-up: a Claude draft looks like any other and cannot be redone: say Claude drafted it, and redo it while you have not edited it", issue 1732): while it is unsent, the row and the form's Conversation section read *Gmail · Claude drafted an answer* (or *Outlook · …*) with Open ↗, and Reply… asks again what Claude should say. Draft with Claude then writes the same draft again, in place and at the same link, where it is unchanged since Claude wrote it. A draft edited in the mailbox (words changed or added, or a file attached), one Cockpit cannot read back, and every draft made before this are yours: Reply… opens it as it is and the line reads *Draft started* from then on. Write it myself opens Claude's draft unchanged. A rewrite that fails leaves Claude's draft as it was and says why.

**Follow a sent email… in Capture makes an Item that follows a conversation you started** ("Follow a sent email… in Capture", issue 1710): the Item is titled with the sent email's subject and Waiting for reply (your own sent mail is your turn, due after the Follow-ups delay), its row reads *Gmail · You wrote 2 days ago* or *Outlook · …*, and a reply arriving in that conversation turns it To do as it does for any followed conversation. The conversation's count starts at one, the sent email, and grows with the turns a check sees. An Item the same pick made earlier and you have since finished is not made again: the pick says so and leaves it.

**A LinkedIn Item follows the LinkedIn conversation, not the email** ("Follow the LinkedIn conversation instead of the notification", issue 1711): starring a LinkedIn message notification in Gmail makes an Item whose row reads *LinkedIn · Steven wrote 3 days ago*, and *Open ↗*, the row menu's and the form's *Open in LinkedIn* go to the conversation on LinkedIn (to the notification email only if the conversation has no link that opens; "LinkedIn Item: the row menu and the form say Open in Gmail and open the notification email: name LinkedIn and open the conversation, as the row's Open does", issue 1795). Every later notification about that conversation, in any Gmail conversation and starred or not, is their turn and makes the Item To do. What you answer on LinkedIn is never seen, so after replying its status is yours to set (*Wait for reply…*), and the conversation's section gives who it is with and no count of messages. It offers no *Email…*, as there is no mailbox to write from. Only a notification that links to a `linkedin.com/messaging` conversation counts; any other LinkedIn email, such as a connection request, stays plain mail.

**On its day, a copy of a recurring item appears.** At midnight on the calendar its rule was last set on, a new Item appears at the top of each of the Panels the recurring item files on that are still live, or in the Inbox where none is, in a tab already open as in one opened later. It is made from the recurring item, never from an earlier copy: its title, description, priority and Panels, and no files, so an edit to one copy stays on that copy; it starts To do, due the set number of days after the day it appears. **Never more than one is open**: while the latest is still To do, In progress or Waiting for reply on the day, nothing is added and it keeps its *Overdue* pill; finished with or dismissed, the next appears on the first day the rule comes round after today, never on a day already passed, so a week nobody opened brings back one, not seven. Monthly on the 31st falls on the last day of a shorter month and returns to the 31st; yearly on 29 February falls on the 28th between leap years. **Moving one copy's due date moves only it; moving Next on moves the recurring item.** **Deleting the recurring item stops it**; dismissing one copy skips only it. A recurring item whose Workspace is deleted stops.

**A copy says which recurring item it came from.** A copy's row wears a ↻ among its title marks, which reads *Recurring weekly on Monday* on hover, and its form reads *↻ From recurring item **Weekly review** ›* under its title. The name closes the form and opens the Recurring items modal on that recurring item's form, read first if it was not held. Once the recurring item is deleted the line reads *(since deleted)*, with no link, and the copies stay. An Item that is no copy has neither. **The Item form has no Repeats chip**, at a desk or on a phone: a recurring item is made only from the Workspace's menu, and the old chip's change, sent by a tab still on the previous app, is refused with *Make recurring items from the Workspace menu* and writes nothing.

**Recurring items are made, changed and deleted from the Workspace's menu.** *Recurring items*, under *Show All items tab*, opens one modal over whatever is on screen, with no address, that needs a connection: the Workspace's recurring items soonest first (*Title*, *Repeats*, *Next on*, *Files on*, as cards on a phone), **+ New recurring item** and **Close**. A row or **+ New recurring item** turns the same modal into that recurring item's form, never a second one: title, description, priority, *Files on* (Panels of the Workspace, none for the Inbox) and the schedule, with **← Recurring items**, **Cancel** and **Save** going back to the list and **Delete recurring item** asking, stopping it and going back; its copies keep naming it. A save shows in the list at once. A *Next on* before today, or a Panel no longer in the Workspace, goes back to the form with its reason beside the field. Close, Escape or the scrim closes the modal and drops what was not saved. **Next on** starts one period from today (31 January's month falls on the last day of February) and cannot be moved to before today on the calendar of the time zone the browser was in when it was set, which is stored with the rule; a copy's ↻ and *From recurring item* line change with it at once.

**A change to an Item shows the moment it is made.** Done, In progress, Waiting for reply, priority, due date, title, description, next action, Dismiss, *Not a duplicate* and filing on a Panel (moving, adding, removing and reordering) — from a row, a drag, the form, a selection, an Inbox proposal or Undo — are drawn at once and stay as drawn while the server answers, however slow it is. One the server refuses goes back, with its reason where that surface already gives one (the form beside the field, the selection's bar, above a list's rows for a filing), and a dismissal from the form's duplicate warning says so in the bar. **Undo is offered at the click**, and pressed before the change it reverses has landed it waits for it. Changes reach the server one at a time, in the order they were made. **A filing is built on what is drawn, filings still waiting included**, so two in a row both land, and no read of the Workspace stands between the click and the send unless the tab has been told something changed elsewhere (it then reads once first). The Panel having changed on another device is a filing's refusal: it goes back with *this panel changed while you were looking at it: try the move again*.

Focus horizons and the AI layer beyond the note cleanup and Readings above are not built. Deadline pills have shipped: within a week of its due date a row wears a pill — a quiet amber outline ("Due in 5d"), a soft amber fill within two days ("Due tomorrow"), solid amber on the day ("Due today"), red once it has passed ("Overdue 2d") — and the row itself stays plain. **At a desk the pill sits on the title line, the date under it and the age at the end; on a screen narrower than 768px the title line holds only the title and its marks, the pill takes the date's place under it, a date over a week off still reads "Due <date>", and the age is not drawn**, so the title gets the width. A day is the viewer's own calendar day, and nothing escalates past red, only the count.
